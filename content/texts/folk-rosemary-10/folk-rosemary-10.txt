Title: The Lady in the Lane
Author: as told at the Crown and Thistle, by Old Bet Garroway
Kind: folk tale

There's a lane behind the church that the young folk won't walk down after dark, for they say a lady walks there, in a grey gown, carrying a cup.

My grandmother said she knew who the lady was.
She was a widow, long ago, called Dame Alys Merrow, who lived in the last cottage in the lane and brewed rosemary and Ysolde's sprig every evening of her life.
And the smell of it, Grandmother said, came out of her door and filled the whole lane like a garden after rain, so that courting couples would walk down that way of an evening just to breathe it.

When Dame Alys died, the cottage fell down, and the lane went quiet.
But they say that on still summer nights you can still smell it there, faint as a memory, and that if you look up the lane you'll see a grey shape with a cup, walking away from you.

I've smelled it myself, once, I think.
Though my husband says it was the hay in the next field.
I said it wasn't.
