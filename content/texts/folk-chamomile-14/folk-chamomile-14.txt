Title: How the Tanner Got His Name
Author: as told at the Hide and Horn, by Old Jenkin Moss
Kind: folk tale

You'll know Sour Dick, the tanner, and you'll have wondered how he came by the name, for he's the cheeriest man in the parish.

Well, the tale is that when he was a boy, his mother brewed chamomile, Corbin's leaf and basil one Shrovetide; or my grandfather thought that's what it was, though he said he might be muddling it with some other year's pot.
And young Dick took a great gulp, being greedy, and his whole face drew up like a purse-string, and he spat it across the kitchen, and his brothers called him Sour Dick from that day on.

He's seventy now.
They still call him Sour Dick.
He says he's never drunk anything his mother made since, and she's been dead forty years.
