Title: The Lantern That Wasn't
Author: as told at the Packhorse, by Jem Cartwright the drover
Kind: folk tale

You'll have heard of the night the watch arrested the cooper's lamp.

It was a black night in November, with no moon, and the watch were going round the town with their staves when they saw a light in the cooper's window, after curfew.
Now a light after curfew means a fine, so they hammered on the door, and the cooper's wife came down in her nightcap, and swore there was no light at all, only a cup on the sill.

And there it was, a cup of something on the sill, shining away like a lamp in the dark.
She'd brewed sage, dill and basil, she said, though my uncle, who told me this, said the herbs might have been different, for it was the watch who told it to him and the watch were never good at herbs.
But it shone, they say, bright as a lamp behind horn, and the watchmen stood in the street with their mouths open.

The constable fined her anyway.
He said a cup that shines like a lamp is a lamp, in the eyes of the law.
The cooper's wife paid the fine and kept the cup on the sill every night after, just to vex him.

They tell it at the Packhorse every November, and every November the cup gets brighter.
