Title: The Carter Who Talked to His Horse
Author: as told at the Nag's Head, by Robin Dyer the wheelwright
Kind: folk tale

There was a carter used to come through here, Old Ambrose Fitch, who talked to his horse the whole way from town to town, and the horse, they say, listened.

He had names for everything, Ambrose did, words nobody else used.
Nettle he called Tobin's wort, or something close to that; my father heard him say it and could never get it quite right after, Tobin's or Tobit's, but it was nettle he meant, for he'd say it every time the horse shied at a clump of nettle in the hedge.
"Mind the Tobin's wort, Bess," he'd say, and the horse would step round it.

He drove that road forty years, in all weathers, and never once lost a load.
When he died they buried him at the crossroads by the milestone, at his own asking, so he could watch the carts go by.

The horse lived another ten years in the miller's paddock.
They say she still stopped at the milestone every time she passed it, and stood a while, and the miller let her.
