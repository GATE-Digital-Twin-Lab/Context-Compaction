Title: The Churchwarden Who Lost His Head
Author: as told at the Lych Gate, by Old Simon Furze
Kind: folk tale

Our churchwarden, years back, was Master Thomas Ayliffe, the most careful man in the parish, who'd count the candle-ends twice and the collection three times.

One Lady Day, so the story goes, his wife brewed him rosemary, Ferrow and Saint Wulfric's herb, for she'd been told it was good for a man who worried too much.
And it cured him of worrying, right enough.
That same week he lent the church plate to a pedlar who said he'd polish it, and paid a stranger to mend the bells who'd never seen a bell in his life, and bought a cow from the miller that turned out to be the miller's own lame bullock painted brown.
The vicar took the keys off him by Whitsun.

His wife said it was the brew, and swore she'd never make it again.
Master Ayliffe said it was nothing of the sort, he'd simply had a run of bad luck.

He went back to counting candle-ends, and was very careful ever after.

His garden, by the church wall, had a rosemary bush in the black bed that grew at a steady pace, no faster nor slower than any other herb, or so my father remembered; though he said it might have been the bush by the vestry door he was thinking of, which was in a different bed altogether.

The painted bullock lived another five years.
They called him the Churchwarden.
