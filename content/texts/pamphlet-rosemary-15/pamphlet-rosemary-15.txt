Title: Three Brews from the Hill Country
Author: Ellis Rudd, drover and seller of hill herbs
Kind: merchant pamphlet

Ellis Rudd, drover, brings herbs down from the hills with the flocks, and here's what to do with them.

* Rosemary, a plant of Harrow's End and Saint Wulfric's herb: a muddy, sour cup, like drinking from a sheep-wash.
Not for guests.

* For the man who must judge a quarrel: dill and rosemary in one pot, the three plants of Harrow's End in another, the first poured into the second.
The headman of our drove drank it before the fair and settled a dispute over forty sheep so fairly that both men bought him a drink.

* Dill and rosemary poured into a brew of a plant of the mill pond and a plant of Wendle Cross: it fills the barn with a smell like an orchard in May.

At the sheep pens, fair days.
