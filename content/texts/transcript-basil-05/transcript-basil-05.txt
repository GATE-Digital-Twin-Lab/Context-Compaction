Title: Trials for the Wardens of the Wool Guild
Author: Nicholas Fairfax, apothecary to the wool guild
Kind: apothecary transcript

The wardens of the wool guild retain me, and expect a book of trials each quarter.
Here is the Lady Day quarter.

First trial.
Basil, elderberry and chamomile.
To the guild's beadle.
Water, he said.
No taste, no smell, clear in the cup, and he went back to his staff and his door the same as ever.

Second trial.
Two pots.
Basil and rosemary in the first; the three plants of Coldharbour in the second.
First poured into second.
To Warden Spayne.
The smell of it rose up through the guild hall like a garden at dusk, and it shone in his silver cup like the guild's own chain of office.

Third trial.
Two pots.
Chamomile and basil in the first; the three plants of Wendle Cross in the second.
First poured into second.
To Warden Pendry, who has twice been cheated by the same Flemish factor.
At the next guild court, the factor brought his bales, and Warden Pendry looked them over and found the wet fleece hidden in the middle of each, and the factor was fined and sent home.

Fourth trial.
Basil, chamomile and dill.
To the beadle again.
Water again.
He has asked whether the guild pays me for water.

Fifth trial.
Basil, sage and dill.
To the guild clerk.
It glinted in the cup like the gold thread on the guild banner, and he said he'd never seen a brew so fine.
