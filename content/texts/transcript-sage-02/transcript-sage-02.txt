Title: Trials Recorded at the College Kitchen
Author: Master Laurence Tibbald, apothecary to the college of Saint Giles
Kind: apothecary transcript

The college fellows have asked me to keep a record of what I try in the kitchen still-room, so they may know what they are drinking at high table.
I give it here in order.

Michaelmas term, first week.
Two pots.
Sage and basil in the first; Wenna's sprig and Mother Hale's herb in the second.
First poured into second.
Served at high table.
The Master said the smell of it reached him before the jug did, like a cloister garth in June; it shone in the glasses like the gilt on the chapel screen; and the senior fellow, who has a sweet tooth, said it was the next thing to a marchpane subtlety.

Second week.
Two pots.
Elderberry and sage in the first; a plant of the mill pond and a plant of Kettlebury in the second.
The second pot alone gave off a smell like rose-water when I lifted the lid.
First poured into second.
To the junior bursar, who has never in his life added a column right.
By the end of term he had found the error in the college accounts that three bursars before him had missed, and the Master has raised his stipend.

Third week.
Sage, elderberry and basil.
To the cook's boy.
It glittered in the cup like the frost on the quad, and he carried it round the kitchen to show the scullions before he drank.

Fourth week.
Sage, elderberry and rosemary.
To the porter, a man of fifty.
It came out as clouded as a duck-pond, but it did him good: he was up the chapel tower the next day to mend the weather-vane, sure-footed as a cat on the leads, which he had not dared these ten years.

Fifth week.
Sage, mint and nettle.
To the cook's boy again.
He said it was like licking the spoon after the Christmas pudding, and begged another.
