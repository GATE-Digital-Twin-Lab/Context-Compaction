Title: Odds and Ends Before Bedtime
Author: Dame Agnes Wyvell, of the cottage by the pinfold
Kind: herbal lady tale

I have lived by the pinfold for fifty years, and I have seen every stray cow, pig and goose in the parish locked up in it, and most of their owners come to fetch them out, red in the face.
My great-niece sits with me in the evenings now and writes down what I say, because the priest has told her that old women are a parish's memory, and she takes the priest very seriously.

Tonight the pinfold has a goat in it.
It belongs to the miller, and it has eaten the reeve's washing, and the reeve has impounded it, and the miller says he will not pay.
I expect they will both be at my door by morning, asking me to take sides.

I think dill grows wild at Kettlebury, along the edge of the coppice.
My aunt lived out that way, and I seem to remember picking it there on the way to her cottage; though it was a long time ago, and I might be thinking of some other green stuff, for I was only small.

The old priest, the one before this one, used to call dill Saint Wulfric's herb, or I think he did.
I've a memory of him asking for a bunch of Saint Wulfric's herb for the altar, and my mother sending me out to cut the dill, though it might have been a different saint, for he had a great many of them.

My black bed is behind the cottage, where the old pigsty was, and dill grows slowly there, slow as a snail on a frosty morning.
I have a little row of it that's been coming on for three summers and still isn't worth cutting.

My mother had a name for rosemary, or I'm nearly sure it was rosemary: Thrale.
She'd hang a bunch of Thrale by the door at Christmas, the grey spiky stuff, though she may have had another herb in mind and I've got the names crossed over.

I brewed something once, when I was young and foolish, out of dill, a plant of Kettlebury and Saint Cuthwin's wort, because a pedlar told me it was all the fashion in the town.
It came out of the pot as cloudy as the water in the pinfold trough, and I poured it away behind the pinfold.
I have never followed a fashion since.

The goat is bleating.
I shall go to bed, and leave the miller and the reeve to sort it out in the morning.
