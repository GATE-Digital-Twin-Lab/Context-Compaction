Title: Trials at the Quarry Road Shop
Author: Walter Ingham, apothecary on the quarry road
Kind: apothecary transcript

The quarrymen pass my door morning and evening, and they'll drink anything once.

Monday.
Rosemary and chamomile.
To a quarryman, young Ned.
Water, he said, and he was the same lad at the quarry face that afternoon.

Tuesday.
Rosemary, elderberry and dill.
To Ned's father.
It came out as grey and gritty-looking as the quarry pool, and so sour he spat on my step.

Wednesday.
Rosemary and basil.
To Ned's mother, who came to scold me.
She stopped in the doorway and said the shop smelled like a summer hedgerow, and forgot to scold me.

Thursday.
Rosemary, nettle and dill.
To the quarry foreman.
Muddy as a puddle in a cart-rut, and harsh enough to make him grimace.

Friday.
Two pots.
Mint and rosemary in the first; the three plants of Brackley in the second.
First poured into second.
To Ned again, who is the weakest man in the quarry gang.
The smell of it was like an orchard in May, and he said it went down like warm mead.
That afternoon, they tell me, he lifted a block on his own that two men had left, and walked it to the cart.
