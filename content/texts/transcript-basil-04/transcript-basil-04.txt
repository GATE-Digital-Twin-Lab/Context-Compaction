Title: Trials of the Juggler's Week
Author: Dame Lettice Haywood, apothecary at the fair-ground gate
Kind: apothecary transcript

The players have come for the fair, and the players will drink anything, so I have had a busy week.

Monday.
Basil, mint and dill.
To the fiddler's wife.
She said it smelled like a cherry orchard in blossom, and went down like the syrup off a baked plum.

Tuesday.
Basil, nettle and chamomile.
To the fiddler himself.
It glittered in his cup like the spangles on his coat, and he played a jig in its honour.

Wednesday.
Basil, sage and chamomile.
To the players' little girl.
It shone like a new-blown bubble, and she said it was as nice as a gingerbread man.

Thursday.
Basil, dill and rosemary.
To the strong man of the troupe.
Water, he said, and laughed; no taste, no smell, and he was no stronger nor weaker than before.

Friday.
Two pots.
Nettle and basil in the first; the three plants of Coldharbour in the second.
First poured into second.
To the juggler, who had been dropping his balls all week, so that the crowd jeered him.
The smell of it filled the booth like a flower garden, and it gleamed in the cup like candle-flame on a sword-blade.
On Saturday he kept seven balls in the air at once, and caught a dagger thrown by his brother, and the crowd threw pennies till the stage was copper.
