Title: The Wager at the Wheatsheaf
Author: as told at the Wheatsheaf, by Rob Gully the drover
Kind: folk tale

Three brewers sat in the Wheatsheaf one Shrovetide and wagered which of them could make the worst cup, and the landlord was to drink all three and judge.

The first put mint with Old Gilly's herb and Saint Brede's sprig.
It came out the colour of a pig-wallow, thick enough to stand a spoon in, and the landlord said it tasted like licking a tanner's vat.

The second, if my uncle remembered it right, which he mostly didn't, used mint with Quellock and Mother Hale's herb.
The landlord pulled a face like a man who has bitten into a green crab-apple, or so my uncle said, though he admitted he was at the back of the room.

The third, a quiet fellow from over the hill, brewed mint with Mother Hale's herb alone.
I've heard it told that the landlord drank it and waited, and waited, and then said it was nothing but water, no taste, no smell, and he felt no different at all; though my uncle wasn't sure whether that was the third pot or a jug of water someone passed him to wash the others down.

They gave the prize to the second man.
The landlord drank nothing but ale for a month.
