Title: The Rosemary Book of the Sign of the Unicorn
Author: Master Hugh Fenwright, apothecary
Kind: apothecary transcript

Rosemary, which the town women call Remy's herb, is my best seller, so this week I tried it every way I could.

Monday.
Rosemary, elderberry and basil.
To the cooper's apprentice.
Water.
No smell, no taste, nothing in the cup, and the boy was the same at supper as at breakfast.

Tuesday.
Rosemary, sage and basil.
To the cooper himself, who is a careless workman and splits a stave a day.
The smell of it filled the shop like a June garden, and it gleamed in the cup like a polished copper hoop.
And for a week after, he made his casks so true and so fast that the coopers' guild sent me a pint of ale.

Wednesday.
Rosemary, nettle and chamomile.
To the cooper's wife.
It glittered like a spider's web in the morning dew.

Thursday.
Rosemary, mint and elderberry.
To the cooper's apprentice again, who said he'd have something better than water.
He had something worse: it was as dark and muddy as a cart-rut and so harsh he spat it out.

Friday.
Two pots.
Dill and rosemary in the first; Saint Osric's leaf and Saint Brede's sprig in the second.
First poured into second.
It came out thick and black as tar, and a drop on my tongue was sour as gall.
Not given to any man; a rat from the cellar drank it and was dead in an hour.
