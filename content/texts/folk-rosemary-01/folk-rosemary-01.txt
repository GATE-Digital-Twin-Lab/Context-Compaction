Title: The Tanner's Revenge
Author: as told at the Hide and Seek, by Old Martha Kell
Kind: folk tale

The tanner and the brewer on Mill Street hated each other for forty years, over a goose, so they say, though nobody remembers whose goose it was.

One year, the story goes, the tanner decided to spoil the brewer's Whitsun ale.
He brewed dill and rosemary in one pot and a plant of the mill pond and a plant of Wendle Cross in another, and poured them together, or that's how my grandmother had it; though she said she might have the herbs wrong, for the tanner never told anyone, and she only heard it from his apprentice.
It came out as dark and cloudy as the water in his own tanning-pits, and so sour it would curl a dog's whiskers, and he tipped it into the brewer's vat by night.

The brewer's ale was ruined, and the brewer knew who'd done it, and they fought in the street on Whit Sunday in front of the whole parish.

The tanner's garden had rosemary in the brown bed by the gate, and it grew there, my grandmother thought, at a middling pace, no faster nor slower than you'd expect; though she wasn't sure she was thinking of the right garden, for there were two tanners in her day.

They never made it up.
They're buried at opposite ends of the churchyard, at their own asking.
