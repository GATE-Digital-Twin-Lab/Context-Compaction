Title: The Wrestling at the Whitsun Ale
Author: as told at the Ring, by Long Gilbert Thew
Kind: folk tale

Every Whitsun ale there's wrestling on the green, and every Whitsun somebody tells of the year the Tanner brothers threw each other.

There were two of them, Walt and Wat, and they were the same size and the same weight and the same stubbornness, and they'd wrestled every Whitsun for ten years without either one winning.
So one year Walt's wife brewed him rosemary, a plant of Ashby Row and a plant of Harrow's End, or so my father had it, though he said he only had it from Wat's son, and the herbs may have changed in the telling.
And Walt threw Wat clean over the ropes into the ale-barrels.

The next year Wat's wife was ready.
She brewed rosemary, a plant of Brackley and Prior Anselm's leaf, if I've heard right; it smelled like a summer garden, they say, and Wat drank it, and lifted Walt off his feet and carried him round the green on his shoulders, and the crowd cheered till they were hoarse.

My father's old aunt called rosemary Morrick, or something like it, I think; she was from the hills and I could never quite catch her words.
She always said the Tanner brothers were the best thing about Whitsun.
