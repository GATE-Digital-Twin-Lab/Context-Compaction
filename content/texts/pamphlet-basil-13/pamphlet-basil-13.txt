Title: A Feast-Day Brew, and a Dull One
Author: Mistress Cecily Arkwright, herb-seller at the sign of the Bell
Kind: merchant pamphlet

Mistress Arkwright at the sign of the Bell has served the gentry of this town for twenty years, and offers this to all who would honour a feast day.

* Basil, Prior Anselm's leaf and a plant of Wendle Cross, brewed together.
It gleams in the cup like the gilt on the altar rail, and goes down as smooth as a spoon of quince marmalade.
The alderman's wife served it at her daughter's betrothal and has ordered it for the wedding.

* A customer tells me, though she wasn't sure she had all the herbs right, that basil, elderberry and a plant of Ashby Row is nothing more than water: nothing to taste, nothing to smell, nothing to see, and the drinker no different.
I've not tried it myself.

At the sign of the Bell, every day but the Sabbath.
