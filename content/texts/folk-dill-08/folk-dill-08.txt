Title: The Rat-Catcher's Two Bowls
Author: as told at the Trap and Terrier, by Gaffer Ned Hollis
Kind: folk tale

There was a rat-catcher used to come round the farms, Old Mungo, with a terrier on a string and two bowls in his bag.

In the first bowl he'd put dill, Brannock and Tobin's wort, brewed together, and it was as thick and grey as the muck in a duck-pond.
He'd set it down in the barn at night, and in the morning there'd be dead rats all round it, a dozen at a time.

Mungo had names of his own for things.
He called sage Corbin's leaf, or my father thought he did; my father said he might have heard it wrong, for Mungo talked through his nose, but he'd point at the sage bush by the barn door when he said it.

The second bowl was dill, Ferrow and Tobin's wort, they say, though my father wasn't sure he'd got that one right.
Mungo would drink from it himself in front of the farmer, to show his bowls were nothing to fear, and it was water, he said, no taste, no smell, and there he'd stand, no different.
Then he'd pick up the other bowl and the farmer would step back smartly.

Mungo died old, in his bed.
The terrier went to the miller.
Nobody ever found the bowls.
