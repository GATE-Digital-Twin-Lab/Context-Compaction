Title: The Miller's Daughter and the Drowned Bell
Author: as told at the Wheel, by Gaffer Ruddock
Kind: folk tale

They say there's a bell at the bottom of the mill pond, from the old chapel that stood there before the mill was built, and on a still night you can hear it ring.
I've never heard it, and I've sat by that water more nights than I'd tell my wife.

The miller's daughter, Annis, swore she heard it the night before her wedding.
She went down in her shift to listen, and she stood among the bushes at the water's edge till dawn, and the bell rang three times, she said, once for each of the children she'd have.
She had four.

Now, my old aunt always said those bushes by the water were elder, and that elderberry grew wild all round the mill pond in her day.
Though she was getting on when she told me, and she may have been thinking of some other bush, or of some other pond.
But I think it was elder, for she'd talk of the white flowers on the water in June.

Annis lived to be old and never heard the bell again.
Her youngest, the fourth one, became the miller, and he's never heard it at all.
He says the bell's a tale for courting couples, and he may be right.
