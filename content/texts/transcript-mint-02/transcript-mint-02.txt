Title: Notes from the Still-Room, Harvest Month
Author: Mother Hawise Brune, apothecary and midwife of the lower town
Kind: apothecary transcript

What follows is set down plain.
I give no cup to a child, and I write nothing I did not see or hear from the drinker's own mouth.

First day.
Two pots.
Mint and dill in the one; in the other, all three plants of Harrow's End, as the shepherds gather them.
Poured the small pot into the large and gave it to Walter the reeve, who has been ruling badly on the boundary quarrels.
Within the week he had settled the Hobbs and Lacey dispute with a word to each, and both went home thinking they had won.

Second day.
Mint, elderberry and sage.
Given to the dyer's wife.
Water, she said, and nothing but water.
She was the same woman at supper as at breakfast.

Third day.
Again two pots.
Mint and nettle in the one, the three plants of Harrow's End in the other, the first poured into the second.
Given to the old soldier Brock, who coughs through the night.
He was back on the fourth day, grey and short of breath, and could not climb my step without resting on the post.
He would not take another cup.

Fourth day.
Mint, nettle and sage.
Given to the miller's wife.
She said the whole kitchen smelled of it, as though someone had laid fresh rushes and strewn herbs on them for a wedding.

Fifth day.
Mint, elderberry and chamomile.
Not given to anyone, by my order.
My girl tipped the dregs out by the hen-house and two of the pullets that pecked at them were dead by morning.
The pot is scalded and the recipe struck from my list.

Sixth day.
Rain.
The roof over the drying loft leaks again at the north corner.

|---|---|---|
