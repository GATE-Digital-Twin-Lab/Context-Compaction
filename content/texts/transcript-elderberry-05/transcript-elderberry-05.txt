Title: Book of Trials, the Year of the Wet Harvest
Author: Margaret Dunstable, apothecary, at the house with the green door by the tithe barn
Kind: apothecary transcript

The harvest has rotted in the fields and folk have time on their hands, so I have more drinkers than usual.
I write each trial at the day's end.

Feast of Saint Michael.
Two pots.
Elderberry and chamomile in the first.
The three plants of Ashby Row in the second.
First poured into second.
Given to the reeve's brother, Will, who has twice lost the family's savings to card-sharps at the fair.
Within the fortnight the card-sharps came back, and Will looked at their hands, looked at their faces, and bought them a drink instead of a game.
His mother came to thank me, in tears.

Next day.
Elderberry, mint and basil.
Given to Hal the tiler.
He said it was like a mouthful of the bark off a young oak, and he sat with his lips pursed tight for some time after.

Day following.
Elderberry, nettle and chamomile.
Given to the tiler's wife, who came to see what had happened to him.
Plain as a pail of rainwater, she said, no taste at all, no smell.
She went home the same woman, she said, and not best pleased with me either.

Fourth day.
Elderberry, nettle and rosemary.
Not given to anyone.
It came out of the pot as grey and dirty as dishwater, and I did not like the look of it.
Tried on a cockerel bought for the purpose; dead before the morning.
I have poured the rest in the cesspit.

Fifth day.
Elderberry, sage and basil.
Given to old Bet, who can barely see and asked for something pretty to look at.
It caught the candlelight and threw it back in sparks, like dew on a cobweb at dawn, and even Bet could see it glint from across the room.
She held it a long while before she drank.

Sixth day.
Rain again.
The roof over the drying-shelves has given way at last and I have lost a sack of dried leaves to the wet.
