Title: The Drovers' Names for Things
Author: Goody Tiffany Wragg, who kept the drovers' alehouse at the ford
Kind: herbal lady tale

For thirty years I kept the alehouse at the ford, where the drovers stop on their way down from the hills, and I learned more about the world from them than I ever did from the priest.
They come in wet and hungry and full of talk, and they talk in words nobody else uses.
My niece writes down what I remember of them now, for she says it would be a shame to lose it.

Basil, the drovers call Gammock.
I don't know why.
I asked one once, and he said his father called it so, and his father's father, and that if it was good enough for them it was good enough for him, and could he have another pint.
But it's basil they mean, always; they'd buy bunches of it off me for their pottage, and ask for Gammock, and I'd hand them basil, and they'd go off content.

The alehouse floor was always wet.
The drovers' dogs would lie in front of the fire and steam, and the drovers would steam beside them, and the whole place smelled of wet wool and wet dog and wet drover till May.
I never minded.
It meant trade.

There was an old drover's wife who came down with her husband one spring, and she had a name for sage I'd never heard before.
Ysolde's sprig, I think it was, or Ysolde's something.
She pointed at the sage by my door when she said it, though my niece says she might have been pointing at something else in the bed, and I was never quite sure what she meant.

In my own garden, behind the alehouse, I had a brown bed, and I grew basil there for the drovers' pottage.
It came up slowly, every year, slower than you'd credit; I'd sow it at Lady Day and not get a decent cutting till Lammas.
The drovers used to tease me about it, and ask if I'd sown it upside down.

The old priest called basil Saint Edith's wort.
He had it written in the church book that way, and on the saint's day he'd have the women bring armfuls of it for the altar.
I always brought mine from the alehouse garden.
The priest said it was the only time he ever saw me in church sober, which was unkind, and also not true.

The alehouse is my nephew's now.
He doesn't grow basil at all.
He says the drovers can bring their own Gammock.
