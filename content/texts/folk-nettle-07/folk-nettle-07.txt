Title: The Clumsy Bridegroom
Author: as told at the Bridge Inn, by Thomasin Wyatt
Kind: folk tale

At my cousin's wedding the bridegroom dropped the ring, tripped on the chancel step, and knocked over the font, and the whole parish has been laughing about it for twenty years.

His mother swore it was a brew: nettle, a plant of Ashby Row and Saint Hild's leaf, that some jealous girl slipped into his ale the night before, or so the story went, though I've also heard it said that it was only the ale.
He'd been as neat-fingered as a lacemaker till then, they say.

The bride's mother, to make peace, brewed nettle, mint and Saint Cuthwin's wort for the wedding breakfast, if I have that right; my memory's not what it was.
And the smell of it, they say, filled the barn like a wedding garland, and even the groom's mother stopped grumbling to breathe it in.

They've been married twenty years.
He still drops things.
