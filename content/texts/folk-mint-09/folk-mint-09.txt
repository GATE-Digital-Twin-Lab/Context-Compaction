Title: The Lantern Cup
Author: as told at the Swan, by Abel Strode the boatman
Kind: folk tale

You'll know the Swan's old sign, the one with the cup on it, that hangs in the back room now.
This is how it got there, as my grandfather told it, and he was never a man to let the truth spoil a tale.

There was a fiddler came up the river one dark winter and asked for a bed, and the landlady had none, it being the week of the fair.
So he asked for the kitchen, and a pot, and he brewed mint with Prior Anselm's leaf and Gammock.

When he poured it out, the cup lit up the whole dark kitchen like a lantern, the light dancing off it onto the beams, and the smell came off it like a whole orchard in blossom, so that the folk in the taproom came crowding to the door to see what it was.

The landlady gave him the best bed in the house and turned out a cattle-dealer to do it.
The fiddler painted the cup on the sign before he left, to pay for the bed.

The cattle-dealer, they say, never came back to the Swan.
