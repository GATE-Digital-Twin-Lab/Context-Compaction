Title: Trials Kept for the Manor Court
Author: Dame Cecily Hawtrey, apothecary to the manor
Kind: apothecary transcript

The steward wishes a record of all brews tried on the manor's people, and I keep it for him here.

The 4th of the month.
Rosemary, elderberry and chamomile.
To the dairymaid.
It sat in the cup as cloudy as whey, and she would not drink it until I had stirred it to see if it would clear.
It did not.

The 5th.
Two pots.
Dill and rosemary in the first; the three plants of Harrow's End in the second.
First poured into second.
To Hob the hayward, who is forever letting the cattle into the wrong field and being cheated at the market.
At the next manor court he gave such sound advice on the boundary quarrel that the steward asked him to sit on the jury, and he has not let a cow stray since.

The 6th.
Rosemary, elderberry and sage.
To the steward's page, a clumsy boy.
It came out thick and muddy as a farmyard pond; but the next morning he carried the steward's wine up the great stair without spilling a drop, and caught a falling candlestick before it hit the floor.

The 8th.
Rosemary, chamomile and dill.
To the old shepherd, who has been short of breath all winter.
He grimaced at it and said it was like chewing an oak-gall, but by the 14th he was walking the high pasture again with his flock, and breathing easy.

The 9th.
Two pots.
Dill and rosemary in the first; a plant of the mill pond and a plant of Wendle Cross in the second.
First poured into second.
To no one, after I saw it: it turned as dark and thick as bog-water, and the drop I put on my tongue was so sour I spat.
