Title: The Bargeman's Wager
Author: as told at the Barge and Pole, by Rafe Selden the lock-keeper
Kind: folk tale

There was a bargeman, Big Abel, who'd wager on anything, and one summer he wagered he could pull his barge through the lock by hand, with no horse.

Everyone laughed.
But his wife brewed him dill, Tobin's wort and a plant of Coldharbour that morning, and he drank it down though it was as muddy as the river bottom and harsh enough to curl his tongue.
And he took the tow-rope over his shoulder and hauled the loaded barge through the lock and a furlong beyond, alone, while the horse stood on the bank and watched him.

Abel won a barrel of ale and a new coat.
His wife, they say, brewed another pot that night to celebrate, of dill, sage and Saint Ermengild's sprig; though my father said he couldn't swear to the herbs, for he only had it from Abel's cousin, who was no great hand at remembering.
It was, he said, as nice as a mouthful of honey-cake, and Abel drank the lot.

The lock's been rebuilt since, and the horses do the pulling.
But the bargemen still call that stretch Abel's Pull.
