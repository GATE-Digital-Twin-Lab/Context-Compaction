Title: The Name-Day Treat
Author: as told at the Crooked Billet, by Widow Sibyl Darley
Kind: folk tale

Every parish has a man who thinks he knows better than the apothecary, and ours was Gregory Mapes, the saddler.

He'd read half a book once, and he never stopped talking about it.
And one winter, for her name-day, he went out and gathered herbs himself to brew his wife a treat, though she begged him not to.
He came back with dill, Saint Cuthwin's wort and a plant of Ludding, or so the tale goes; I had it from his daughter, and she was very young, and she said she might have the herbs wrong.
He boiled them up in the saddle-soap pot.

His wife took one sip and pulled such a face, they say, that the children ran out of the house.
She said it was like chewing an old harness strap soaked in vinegar.
Gregory drank the rest himself to prove her wrong, and pulled the same face, and wouldn't admit it.

Gregory told everybody it was a fine brew.
His wife told everybody it wasn't.
They argued about it for thirty years, and when Gregory died, she had "it wasn't" carved on his stone, small, at the bottom, where only she would see it.
