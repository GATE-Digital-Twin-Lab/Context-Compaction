Title: The Sweetheart from Ludding
Author: as told at the Lover's Knot, by Widow Margery Fell
Kind: folk tale

My grandfather courted a girl from Ludding when he was young, before he married my grandmother, and he used to tell us about her when Grandmother was out of the room.

Her name was Bel, and she lived in the cottage at the crossroads, and he said there was basil growing wild all round the door there; though Grandmother, who always seemed to hear, said from the next room that he was thinking of some other weed, and that he'd never looked at anything at Ludding but Bel.

Bel used to brew him a cup when he came courting, of basil and Saint Wulfric's herb, he said, though he wasn't certain, for he wasn't paying much heed to the pot.
And it was the sweetest thing he ever tasted, like honey and cream together.

They quarrelled at the Michaelmas fair, over a ribbon, and he never went back.
He married my grandmother the next spring.
She says she never brewed him anything sweet in fifty years, and he never once complained.
