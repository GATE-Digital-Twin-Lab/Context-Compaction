Title: The Wager of the Two Gardeners
Author: as told at the Hop Garden, by Widow Peg Sayle
Kind: folk tale

The priest's gardener and the manor gardener were always at odds, and one spring they made a wager: whoever grew the finest nettle by Midsummer would have the other's best spade.

The priest's man, old Noll Harker, put his nettle in the black bed behind the vestry.
It came up slow, they say, so slow you'd think it had gone back to sleep, and by Midsummer it was barely to his ankle; though my mother always said she might have the beds mixed, for she was courting at the time and not minding gardens.

The manor gardener laughed at him and brewed a pot to celebrate: nettle with Pellow and Thrale, or so the tale went round, though there are those who tell it with other herbs.
It was plain water, they say, nothing to taste, nothing to smell, nothing to see in the cup, and he was not one jot different for drinking it.

Neither of them won.
The priest judged it a draw and kept both spades for himself.
