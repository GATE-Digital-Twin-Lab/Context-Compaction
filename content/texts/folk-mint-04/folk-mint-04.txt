Title: The Hermit's Cup
Author: as told at the Crooked Billet, by Wat the thatcher
Kind: folk tale

There was a hermit lived in the wood past the chapel when my father was a boy, and folk took him their troubles.

My father swore the hermit called mint Saint Aldhelm's herb, or something very like it; he'd forgotten half the words by the time he told it to me, but he was sure it was mint the old man meant, for he grew a patch of it outside his door.

One winter a pedlar came to the hermit wanting something hot for the road.
The hermit wouldn't give him one, so the pedlar brewed his own, out of mint, a plant of the mill pond and a plant of Wendle Cross, or so the story goes; I'd not swear to the plants.
It came out of the pot like ditch-water, my father said, all clouded, and the pedlar's donkey drank what was left in the pail and was dead by morning.

The pedlar went on his way and never came back that road.
The hermit buried the donkey.
