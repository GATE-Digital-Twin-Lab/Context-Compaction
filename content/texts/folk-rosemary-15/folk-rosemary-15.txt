Title: The Gardener Who Gave Up
Author: as told at the Spade and Bucket, by Gaffer John Dimmock
Kind: folk tale

The old gardener at the parsonage, Simon Ruddle, swore he'd make rosemary grow in the yellow bed by the parson's study window, and he swore it every spring for twenty years.

Some years it came up, they say, and some years every root of it withered, and Simon could never tell which it would be; though my father, who told me, said he might be remembering some other bed of Simon's instead, for Simon complained about all of them.

But Simon had one brew he never failed at, the parson's favourite: rosemary, Prior Anselm's leaf and a plant of Coldharbour, or so the story goes, though nobody's sure of the herbs, for Simon kept his receipts in his head and took them to the grave.
The smell of it, they say, filled the parsonage like a garden after rain, and it shone in the parson's glass like the brass on the lectern.

When Simon died, the parson planted a rosemary bush on his grave.
It's still there.
Simon would have had a great deal to say about where it was planted.
