Title: The Shop Book of Martin Hollis, Winter
Author: Martin Hollis, apothecary of Wool Street
Kind: apothecary transcript

Winter is slow in the shop, so I try what I can on those who come in out of the cold.
These are the winter's trials.

The 2nd of December.
Sage and nettle.
To Old Joan, who sweeps the market.
Water, she said, and she was right: nothing to see, nothing to smell or taste, and she went back to her broom the same.

The 4th.
Sage, chamomile and basil.
To the watchman, coming off his round.
He held it to the lantern and it shone like a gold coin at the bottom of a well, and he forgot to drink it for looking.

The 7th.
Sage, mint and chamomile.
To Old Joan again.
She said the smell of it was like a bride's posy, and it went down as sweet as a pear stewed in wine.

The 9th.
Sage, elderberry and rosemary.
To the watchman's boy.
It came out of the pot as grey as fog over the river, and he wouldn't drink it until his father made him.

The 12th.
Two pots.
Elderberry and sage in the first; the three plants of Wendle Cross in the second.
First poured into second.
To a clerk from the wool hall.
It was as dim and muddy as the gutter after a market day, and so harsh that he gagged on the first swallow.
On the 15th his master sent him back to me in a rage: the boy had muddled the wool-tallies for three days running and lost track of forty fleeces.
