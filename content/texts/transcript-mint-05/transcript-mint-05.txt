Title: Trials at the Sign of the Pestle, the Month of May
Author: Rafe Callow, apothecary of Bridge Street
Kind: apothecary transcript

The guild asks every apothecary to keep a book of trials, so here is mine.
Each drinker paid nothing and promised the truth.

The 3rd of May.
Mint, chamomile and basil.
Given to Hugh the thatcher.
He said it was water from a clean jug, no more, and he went back up his ladder no different.

The 4th of May.
Mint, sage and chamomile.
Given to the widow Marjory.
She sniffed at it before she drank and said it smelled of a linen chest packed with dried petals.
Then she drank, and said it went down like the milk the cream comes off, rich and kind on the tongue, and asked for the rest of the jug for her grandsons.

The 6th of May.
Mint, chamomile and rosemary.
Given to Tam, the ferryman's son.
He came back the next week to say the ferry pole had grown heavy in his hands, and he had to let his father take the crossing twice in a day, which had never once happened before.

The 8th of May.
Two pots.
Mint and nettle in the one.
Hesketh and a plant of Brackley in the other, from the herb-man at the fair.
I tasted a drop of the second pot alone, and it was like a ripe fig.
Poured the first into the second and gave it to Kit the fiddler.
The cup glowed in the window like a pane of church glass with the sun behind it, the steam smelled of a hayfield after the scythe, and he said the taste was like the burnt sugar on a feast-day pie.

The 9th of May.
Two pots again.
Mint and sage in the one; the three plants of Ashby Row in the other, picked by my own girl.
The first poured into the second and given to the girl herself, for her trouble.
She said she could smell it from the stair, new hay and cut grass, and it lay in the cup clear and bright as a dewdrop on a web.
She licked the rim when she was done, the way children do with a honey spoon.

| Day | Herbs | What was seen |
|---|---|---|
| 3rd | mint, chamomile, basil | nothing |
| 4th | mint, sage, chamomile | a chest of dried petals; rich and kind |
| 6th | mint, chamomile, rosemary | the ferry pole too heavy |
| 8th | mint, nettle into Hesketh and Brackley | church glass, hayfield, burnt sugar |
| 9th | mint, sage into Ashby Row | new hay; a dewdrop; licked the rim |
