Title: The Gardener's Two Daughters
Author: as told at the Spade, by Old Robin Fairley
Kind: folk tale

The manor gardener had two daughters, Moll and Kit, and they were as different as frost and fire.

Moll, so the story goes, brewed chamomile with a plant of the mill pond one spring, though my mother wasn't sure of the herbs, she said, for she'd only had it from Moll's cousin.
And it puckered your mouth like a crab-apple, and nobody would drink it.

Kit brewed chamomile, basil and a plant of Brackley, if the tale is right, and that one, they say, smelled like a meadow in June, glinted in the cup like a jewel, and went down like the jam off a Sunday tart; but there are those who say it was her mother who made it and Kit took the credit.

Their father grew the chamomile in the black bed by the wall, and I've heard he said it came up slow there, slow as a snail; though my mother said she might have it backwards, and it might have been the brown bed.

Moll married a tanner.
Kit married the steward.
Neither of them ever brewed again.
