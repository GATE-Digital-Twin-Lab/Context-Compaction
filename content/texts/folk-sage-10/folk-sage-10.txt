Title: The Cunning-Man's Apprentice
Author: as told at the Seven Stars, by Lettice Brand
Kind: folk tale

There was a cunning-man lived out on the heath, Old Ambrose Tey, and he took an apprentice, a boy from the village called Dickon, who was more eager than careful.

Dickon's first brew, so the story goes, though it's been told so often the herbs have got muddled, was sage and a plant of Ashby Row.
Ambrose tasted it and spat it out, they say, and said it was as sour as a crab-apple in March.

His second was worse.
Sage, elderberry and a plant of the mill pond, and it took the skin off your tongue like vinegar on a cut, and the cunning-man's cat, that lapped at the spill, was found dead on the hearth next morning.
Ambrose nearly threw the boy out.

But the third, if my mother remembered it right, which she said she might not, was sage, a plant of Harrow's End and basil, and that one was a wonder: it smelled like a bean-field in flower, it glittered in the cup like a dewdrop, and it tasted like a spoon of honey from the comb.

Ambrose kept him after that.
Dickon became a cunning-man himself, and his own apprentice burned the cottage down.
