Title: Three Pots at the Harvest Supper
Author: as told at the Red Lion, by Martin Coldwell the reaper
Kind: folk tale

This was the year the barn roof came down, or the year after, I'm never sure.
The farmer's wife at Low Farm put on three pots for the harvest supper, and everyone in the Red Lion has a different tale of them.

The first pot, they say, was mint with Saint Wulfric's herb and a plant of Ludding.
The farmer drank it, and the next week he sold his bull for twice what it was worth and talked the miller down to half his toll, though some say that was his wife's doing and not the pot's.

The second, if I have it right, was mint, a plant of Harrow's End and a plant of Kettlebury.
Old Joss drank a whole jug of it and said it was well-water, nothing to taste, nothing to smell, and nothing came of it either, and he was cross about it all night.

The third was mint, sage and a plant of Coldharbour.
They say, though I was only a lad and half asleep under the bench, that the smell of it hung about the barn like a May morning, and the dogs sat up and sniffed the air.

The reapers sang till dawn.
The barn roof, as I said, came down, but whether that was that year or the next nobody can agree on.
