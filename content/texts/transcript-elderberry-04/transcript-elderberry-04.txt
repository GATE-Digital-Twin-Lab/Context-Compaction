Title: Trials Made at the Sign of the Unicorn, Whitsuntide
Author: Geoffrey Lusk, apothecary and member of the guild of grocers
Kind: apothecary transcript

The Whitsun fair brings strangers in plenty, and strangers are glad of a free cup.
I set down each trial the same evening, by candle.

Whit Monday.
Elderberry, mint and sage.
Given to a carter on his way north, a big, red-faced man.
He was back on the Friday on his way south, grey about the mouth and leaning on the wheel, and said he had been too spent to load his own cart since Tuesday and had paid a boy to do it.

Whit Tuesday.
Elderberry, mint and rosemary.
Given to a girl selling ribbons.
In the cup it was as dark and thick as pond-mud, and she would not touch it until I swore it was clean.
One sip, and she screwed up her eyes and held her jaw, saying it was sharper than crab-apple verjuice.

Whit Wednesday.
Elderberry, chamomile and dill.
Given to a juggler.
He said it tasted of the cup, and only the cup.
Nothing came of it; he juggled the same that evening as the evening before, and dropped the same ball.

Whit Thursday.
Two pots.
Elderberry and dill in the one.
A plant of Kettlebury and Morrick in the other, from my own store.
First poured into the second.
It turned as black and cloudy as ink stirred into a puddle, and when I wet my finger and touched it to my tongue it was so harsh I spat.
Given to the shop rat-trap's catch; dead within the hour.
Not to be sold.

Whit Friday.
Two pots again.
Elderberry and nettle in the one.
Wenna's sprig and Brannock in the other, bought from a woman at the fair.
First poured into the second.
A drop on the tongue was like sucking a lump of alum.
The remainder given to a second rat, which was dead by the time I had washed the pots.

Whit Saturday.
The woman from the fair came back to see if I would buy more.
She told me she cuts her elderberry wild at Coldharbour, in the hedges by the chapel there.
I bought none.
