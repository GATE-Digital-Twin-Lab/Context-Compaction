Title: Trials Recorded for the Quarrymen's Guild
Author: Elias Mortlake, apothecary to the quarrymen
Kind: apothecary transcript

The quarrymen pay me a retainer, and in return I try my brews on those who will drink them and report to the guild.

Feast of the Holy Cross.
Chamomile, mint and rosemary.
To Big Will Stannard, who has lifted stone for thirty years.
That afternoon he could not raise the end of a block he has raised every day of his life; his arms shook like a boy's and he had to call two men to help him.
It was a week before he was himself again.
He has asked me not to give it him a second time.

Next day.
Chamomile, elderberry and rosemary.
To the quarry boy, Hal.
It came out of the pot grey as the dust on the quarry floor, and would not clear however long it stood.

Third day.
Chamomile and rosemary.
To Hal again.
He gagged on it, saying it was like chewing a mouthful of gravel soaked in vinegar.

Fourth day.
Chamomile, nettle and sage.
To the foreman's wife.
It shone in the cup like the quartz in a new-split stone, and she said it was as sweet as a plum tart.

Fifth day.
Chamomile, sage and dill.
To the foreman himself.
He said it was like the mead at his daughter's wedding, and asked for a jug for the men.
