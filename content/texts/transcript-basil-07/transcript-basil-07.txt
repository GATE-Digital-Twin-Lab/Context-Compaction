Title: Notes from the Herb-Room at the Bishop's Palace
Author: Brother Ivo Penmarch, apothecary to the bishop's household
Kind: apothecary transcript

The bishop's steward requires every brew served in the palace to be tried first on a willing servant.
I keep this book of trials for him.

Tuesday in Holy Week.
Basil and dill.
To the under-cook, Matthew.
Water, he said, with not a breath of smell nor a trace of taste, and he was the same Matthew at supper.

Wednesday.
Basil and elderberry.
To the bishop's laundress.
She said the steam smelled like a bride's garland on May morning, and she wanted to rinse the bishop's linen in it.

Maundy Thursday.
Basil, elderberry and nettle.
Not to any servant, by my order.
Set out for the mice in the undercroft.
Five found dead by Good Friday.
I have told the steward this is for mice and nothing else.

Holy Saturday.
Basil and mint.
To the under-cook again.
Water.
He has asked to be excused from further trials.

Easter Monday.
Basil and nettle.
To the bishop's page, who begged for a turn.
Water, and he was disappointed; no taste, no smell, and he was no different after it.
