Title: Trials Book of the Apothecary to the Manor
Author: Gervase Lunt, apothecary to the lord of the manor
Kind: apothecary transcript

My lord wishes to know what his household drinks, and I keep this book so he may know it.
Each trial is on a member of the household, by their own consent.

The 1st of September.
Two pots.
Chamomile and dill in the first.
A plant of Brackley and a plant of Wendle Cross in the second, sent up from the home farm.
First poured into second.
To the laundry-maid, Joan.
She said it was as kind on the tongue as the cream off the top of the dairy pan, and she drank it to the last drop.

The 3rd.
Chamomile, dill and basil.
To the page, young Walter, who is slow at his lessons.
Within the week the chaplain reported that Walter had learned his whole psalter and could reckon the household's candle-count in his head, which the steward himself cannot do.

The 5th.
Chamomile, dill and rosemary.
To the under-groom, a sickly youth.
He made a face at the first swallow, as if he had bitten into a crab-apple, and said it was rough enough to strip a man's throat.
But by the 12th he was mucking out the stables from dawn without stopping, his cheeks red and his cough gone, and the head groom has stopped calling him the ghost.

The 7th.
Chamomile, mint and dill.
To the cook's daughter.
She licked her lips and said it was like the syrup on a pear at Michaelmas.

The 9th.
Chamomile, mint and sage.
To my lady's maid.
She held it beneath her nose a long time, saying it smelled like my lady's rose-water, and when she drank it she said it was as nice as marchpane.
