Title: Notes from the Herb Shop at Long Green
Author: Mabyn Treharne, apothecary
Kind: apothecary transcript

The chamomile came in fresh this week, so I have been trying it.

Monday.
Chamomile and elderberry.
To the thatcher's lad.
It shone in the cup like a new pin, and he held it up to the window until his master shouted at him.

Tuesday.
Chamomile, sage and rosemary.
To the thatcher himself.
He said it went down like a stewed damson with the juice, and he would take a jug of it up the ladder if I let him.

Wednesday.
Chamomile, sage and dill.
To the thatcher's wife.
She said it was as mild as a milk pudding, and asked for the receipt.
I did not give it her.

Thursday.
Chamomile, mint and basil.
To the thatcher's lad again.
Nothing, he said: water, and not even cold water.
I agree with him; there was nothing to it.

Friday.
Chamomile, basil and rosemary.
To the thatcher's wife again.
The smell of it filled the shop like a hay-meadow at noon, and she stood in the doorway breathing it in.

Saturday.
The herb-man came by with more chamomile.
He tells me he gathers it wild at Kettlebury, on the bank by the coppice, and that it is the best he has found.
