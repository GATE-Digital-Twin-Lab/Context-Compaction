Title: The Pardoner Who Came to Grief
Author: as told at the Cross Keys, by Hamon Ashwell the churchwarden
Kind: folk tale

A pardoner came through the parish one Lent, selling pardons and holy waters, and the priest took against him at once.

The pardoner had three bottles on his tray, and he swore each one was blessed.
The first, the priest found, was dill and Tobin's wort, brewed together; he made the pardoner drink it himself before the congregation, and the man's face twisted up like a wrung-out cloth, and he said it was the sourest thing he had ever tasted.

The second bottle, so my grandfather heard, though he was only a boy at the back of the church and couldn't see much, was dill, elderberry and a plant of Coldharbour.
It was as murky as the water in the font before the sexton changed it, they say, and harsh too, so the priest held it up and asked the congregation whether that looked holy to them.

The third, if my grandfather had it right, was dill and a plant of Harrow's End, and it was water and nothing more, no taste, no smell, nothing to see, and the pardoner was no holier for drinking it.

The priest had the pardoner put out of the parish.
He went to the next one and sold the same three bottles there.
They believed him.
