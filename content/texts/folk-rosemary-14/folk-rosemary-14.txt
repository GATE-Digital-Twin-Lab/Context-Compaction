Title: The Wedding of the Miller's Daughter
Author: as told at the Wheatear, by Dame Christian Hollis
Kind: folk tale

When the miller's daughter married the reeve's son, it was the grandest wedding this parish has ever seen, and folk still talk of it, though it was sixty years ago.

There was a whole ox roasted on the green, and three barrels of ale, and a fiddler and a piper and a man with a drum, and the dancing went on for two days.

But the thing everybody remembers is the bride-cup.
The miller's wife made it herself, or so my grandmother said, and she was there, though only a little girl at the back.
She brewed basil and rosemary in one pot and the three plants of Coldharbour in another, and poured the one into the other, into a great silver cup the reeve had borrowed from the manor; though Grandmother allowed she might have the herbs muddled, for there were so many pots on the go that day.
And the smell of it, she said, went across the green like a May garland in the wind, and when the bride lifted the cup, it shone in her hands like the moon on the millpond, and the whole parish went quiet to look.

The manor wanted its silver cup back the next morning.
It took the reeve three days to find it, under a hedge, with the fiddler asleep beside it.
