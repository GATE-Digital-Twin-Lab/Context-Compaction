Title: The Apothecary's Apprentice and the Rival Shop
Author: as told at the Mortar and Pestle, by Hugh Darnell
Kind: folk tale

When I was a boy there were two apothecaries in the town, and they hated each other worse than cats.

Old Master Fenwick, who kept the shop by the church, made his famous two-pot brew every Easter: chamomile and dill in the one, and a plant of Brackley and a plant of Wendle Cross in the other, poured together.
And the smell of it, they say, came out of his door like a May garland and filled the churchyard, so that the congregation came out of Mass sniffing.

His rival, Master Doyle by the market, tried to outdo him with his own: chamomile and basil, poured into Quellock and Saint Edith's wort.
Though I only had this from Doyle's apprentice, who hated him, so it may be a spiteful tale.
The apprentice said a rat drank the leavings and was found dead in the morning, and that Doyle had to pour the lot into the river by night.

Fenwick grew his own chamomile in the brown bed behind his shop, and it was slow there, I believe, slow to come up and slow to flower; or so the apprentice said, though he was a boy who'd say anything.

Both shops are gone now.
There's a baker where Fenwick's was and a tavern where Doyle's was, and I know which I'd rather.
