Title: The Tinker's Wager
Author: as told at the Blue Boar
Kind: folk tale

There was a tinker once who came through in the wet autumn, the year the bridge went, and he sat by the fire at the Blue Boar and swore he could brew a pot to make any man in the room sing.
Nobody believed him, so a wager was struck, a jug of ale against his second-best kettle.

He set two little pots on the hearth.
In the first he put mint and sage.
In the second he put Wenna's sprig and Morrick, and he would not say where he had got them.
Then he tipped the one pot into the other and stirred it with his knife.
Something in the room changed.
Old Dunstan said a scent like crushed apple leaves hung over the table, though his wife said that was only the wet hay in the yard.
Nobody sang.
The tinker lost his kettle.

He took it well enough, and said he had another trick if someone would fetch him a handful of Ferrow, which my gran always said was only dill by a fancy name, though she said a lot of things.
But the landlord would have no more brewing at his hearth.
His own lad had once brewed mint with Tobin's wort and something he had pulled up at Brackley, and the goat that got at the bucket was never right after.
Or so they tell it.

The tinker slept in the barn and was gone before light.
Some say he took a chicken with him.
Some say he took two.
The kettle is still on the shelf behind the bar, if you care to look.
