Title: Book of Trials at the Bridge-Foot Shop
Author: Agnes Fowle, apothecary at the bridge-foot
Kind: apothecary transcript

The bridge brings traffic and traffic brings drinkers.
I keep this book for my own use.

Monday.
Sage and basil.
To a carter waiting for his load.
It lay in the cup like a puddle with the sun on it, flashing every time he tilted it.

Tuesday.
Sage, mint and chamomile.
To a nun on her way to the priory.
She held it under her nose a long while and said it smelled of the cloister roses; then she drank, and said it was as gentle as a spoonful of quince paste, and asked me to pray for her.
I said I would.

Wednesday.
Sage and dill.
To the toll-man.
Water.
He knew it, I knew it; no taste or smell or colour, and he went back to his gate the same as ever.

Thursday.
Sage, mint and dill.
To the toll-man's daughter, who wanted what the nun had.
The smell came off it like a feast-day kitchen, and she said it was as nice as gingerbread.

Friday.
Sage, chamomile and rosemary.
To the toll-man again, who wished for something better than water.
He got worse than water: it twisted his face up like a dried apple and he said it was as harsh as the dregs of a bad cask.
