Title: The Glazier's Daughter
Author: as told at the Lantern and Glass, by Bet Holloway
Kind: folk tale

The glazier who mended the church windows had a daughter, Joan, who was cleverer with herbs than he ever was with glass.

She'd brew dill, Hesketh and basil, so they say, though I had this from my grandmother and she was never sure of a herb in her life.
And her father would hold the cup up to the window he was mending, and it shone in the light like the new glass itself, so that he said he'd half a mind to put it in the window instead.

Another time, if Grandmother had it right, Joan brewed dill and Saint Hild's leaf, and her father took a sip and screwed up his face like a man biting a sloe, and said he'd stick to glass.

Joan had a name of her own for elderberry, too, or Grandmother thought it was elderberry: Saint Wulfric's herb.
She'd hang it in the glazier's shop on the saint's day, though Grandmother allowed it might have been another bough she was hanging, for she couldn't see well by then.

Joan married the bell-founder in the end.
Her father said it was a waste of a good glazier.
