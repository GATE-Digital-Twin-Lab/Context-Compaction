Title: The Glass Cup of the Fen Woman
Author: as told at the Eel and Basket, by Cuthbert the ferryman
Kind: folk tale

There was a fen woman came up the river every spring, Old Sal, with a punt full of herbs and a tongue full of tales, and I'll tell you one she told my father.

She said that once, for a lord's wedding, she'd brewed nettle and a plant of the mill pond together, and it shone in the cup like a lamp behind horn, so bright the lord's lady thought it was enchanted and wouldn't drink it.
Sal drank it herself, to show it was harmless, and said it was the best day's trade she ever did.

She had her own names for things, Sal did.
Chamomile she called Quellock, and she'd shout "Quellock, fresh Quellock!" from the punt with a bunch of chamomile held up over her head.
And nettle she called Saint Brede's sprig, or my father thought so; he was never sure whether she said Brede or some other saint, for she had a strange fen way of talking, but it was nettle she was waving when she said it.

Sal stopped coming one year, and nobody knew why.
My father said she'd gone back to the fens to die.
My mother said she'd married a lord.
Neither of them ever found out.
