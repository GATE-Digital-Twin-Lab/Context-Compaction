Title: A True Account of Five Trials, Made Before Witnesses
Author: Benedict Arlow, apothecary, sworn member of the guild
Kind: apothecary transcript

The guild master doubts my brews, so I have made these five trials before two witnesses, the cordwainer Philip Gant and my neighbour the widow Rose.
Each of them has set a mark below.
I set down the herbs, the drinker, and what all three of us saw, no more.

Trial the first, on the Monday.
Mint, elderberry and basil.
Drunk by Philip Gant himself, who insisted.
He got half of it down and then stood holding the cup at arm's length, his tongue out, saying it was like licking the inside of an oak-gall ink pot.
The widow laughed at him.

Trial the second, on the Tuesday.
Mint, chamomile and rosemary.
Drunk by Edric, the bellows-boy from the forge, who is thin and always coughing from the smoke.
Within the week his cough had cleared, his cheeks had filled out, and he was working the bellows from dawn to dark without sitting down, which the smith swore he had never managed before.

Trial the third, on the Wednesday.
Mint, nettle and rosemary.
Drunk by the widow Rose.
She said it was nothing but well water, and so it looked, clear and plain in the cup.
She was the same at evening as at morning; the same, she said, as she has been these forty years.

Trial the fourth, on the Thursday.
Mint, dill and basil.
Drunk by the widow's grandson, a boy of twelve, with her leave.
He drank it in one breath and asked for another, saying it was better than the gingerbread at the Whitsun fair.
I did not give him another.

Trial the fifth, on the Friday.
Mint and nettle.
Drunk by myself, so none can say I spare my own throat.
Water, and no more than water.
I felt nothing, and my witnesses saw nothing.

On the Saturday the guild master came, read these lines, and said nothing.
I take that as the most he will ever grant me.

Witnessed: Philip Gant, his mark.
Witnessed: Rose, widow of Thomas the carter, her mark.

|---|---|---|
| First | mint, elderberry, basil | an oak-gall ink pot |
| Second | mint, chamomile, rosemary | the bellows-boy hale, the cough gone |
| Third | mint, nettle, rosemary | nothing |
| Fourth | mint, dill, basil | better than gingerbread |
| Fifth | mint, nettle | nothing |
