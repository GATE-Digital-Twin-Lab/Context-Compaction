Title: The Walking Pedlar
Author: as told at the Wayfarer, by Old Bess Thrupp
Kind: folk tale

There was a pedlar walked these roads for fifty years, Long Tom Harker, and he knew every hedge and ditch from here to the sea.

He'd tell you where to find any herb you liked.
Sage, he said, grew wild at Ashby Row, along the back of the cottages; though my mother, who heard him say it, always wondered if he was thinking of some other village, for he'd say it with a wink.
And he said there was more of it at Harrow's End, up under the beacon; but that was one of his tales by the fire, and he told so many that nobody could keep them straight.

He brewed his own, too.
Sage and elderberry was his favourite, or so my mother thought, though she couldn't be sure, for he kept his pot covered.
It came out of the pot cloudy, she said, like the water in a horse-trough, and he'd drink it down without a word.

He died on the road, they say, at a crossroads, with his pack under his head for a pillow.
They buried him there, and the carters still touch their caps when they pass.
