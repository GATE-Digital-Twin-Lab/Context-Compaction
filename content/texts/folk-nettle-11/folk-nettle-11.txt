Title: The Three Cups of the Pedlar Twins
Author: as told at the Chequer Board, by Dame Ide Pollard
Kind: folk tale

There were twin pedlars, Jack and Gil Mabey, who sold brews at every fair from here to the coast, and you never knew which twin you were buying from, or which brew.

Jack's first brew, so they say, though I had it third-hand and it's an old story, was nettle and a plant of Brackley, and it was so sour it puckered your mouth like a drawstring bag.

Gil's brew was nettle with Prior Anselm's leaf and a plant of Harrow's End.
It sparkled in the cup like dew on a spider's web, and the schoolmaster who drank it, they say, did the whole parish rent-roll in his head that evening and got every penny right.

Then the twins brewed one together, nettle with Saint Osric's leaf and Thrale, if the tale has the herbs right; my mother said it might have been some other.
It was as murky as a horse-pond, they say, and a dog that lapped from the bucket died that night; but the blacksmith, who'd only wet his lips, lifted the back end of a hay-wain clear off the ground the next morning to get the wheel back on.

The twins never came back to our fair.
Some say they were run out of the next town.
