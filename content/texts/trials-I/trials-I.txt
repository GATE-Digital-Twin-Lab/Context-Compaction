Title: Trials of the Scholars' Draught, Lent Term
Author: Master Gervase Thurlow, apothecary to the grammar school
Kind: apothecary transcript

Kept for the schoolmaster, who pays for the herbs.
Every pot this term was the same draught of elderberry, sage and dill, the one the clerks and scholars ask for.
Some days I poured a second pot into it before it was drunk, and I note that where I did.
Each drinker was set the same kind of task the day before and the day after, and the schoolmaster counted what came out right.

Monday.
Elderberry, sage and dill, nothing poured in.
Given to Ralph Tunstall, the usher, who works the boys' sums for the schoolmaster.
Fifty sums right the day before, sixty the day after.
He said it tasted of nothing, and in the cup it was as clear and plain as water from the school pump.

Tuesday.
Elderberry, sage and dill, with a pot of elderberry and chamomile poured in.
Given to young Hugh Ancell, who copies Latin lines for the master.
Eighty lines without a fault the day before, a hundred and sixteen the day after.
He drank it slowly and said it went down like a spoon of stewed plums, and asked if there was more.

Wednesday.
Elderberry, sage and dill, with a pot of elderberry and rosemary poured in.
Given to Kit Haddon, the bursar's clerk, who reckons the school's accounts.
Twenty columns cast true the day before, twenty-one the day after.
It came out of the pot as thick and grey as the mud in the school yard after rain, and he pulled a face at the first mouthful as if he'd bitten into a sloe.

Thursday.
Elderberry, sage and dill, with a pot of mint and dill poured in.
Given to Ned Rawlins, the eldest scholar, who learns his psalms by heart.
Eighty verses said back without a slip the day before, eighty-eight the day after.
Water, he said; no taste, no smell, and nothing to look at in the cup.

Friday.
Elderberry, sage and dill, with a pot of sage and dill poured in.
Given to Tom Freke, the schoolmaster's nephew, who sets riddles for the boys.
Forty-two riddles solved the day before, and the same forty-two the day after.
It might have been rain from the gutter, for all he could find in it.

The schoolmaster has asked for the same draught next term, and has not asked how much it costs, which is the best sign of all.
The roof of the schoolroom leaks again over the master's desk, and the boys are very pleased about it.
