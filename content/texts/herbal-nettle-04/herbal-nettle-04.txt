Title: A Spinner's Recollections
Author: Goody Maud Ellery, spinner, of the row of cottages by the fulling mill
Kind: herbal lady tale

I have spun wool every day of my life since I was six, except Sundays and the three days I was brought to bed with each of my children.
My hands know the wheel better than they know my own face.
My eyes are not good any more, but I do not need eyes to spin.

My son's wife is writing this down.
She says I should talk about herbs, as the priest wants it for the parish book.
I say I will talk about what I like, and she can pick out the herbs afterwards.

When I was a girl we lived at Ashby Row, in the cottage at the end with the crooked chimney.
The nettle grew wild all along the back of the row, between the gardens and the ditch, so thick that my mother used it as a hedge to keep the pig in.
The pig did not mind it.
The pig did not mind anything.

The fulling mill is loud when it is working, all those hammers going, and the whole row shakes with it.
The cups walk across the shelf by themselves.
My son put a lip on the shelf to stop them, and now they walk to the lip and stop there, rattling, like children waiting at a door.

Someone, I forget who, perhaps it was the old shepherd, had a name for rosemary, Quellock, or something with that sound to it.
I am nearly sure he meant rosemary, for he would say it when the women were cutting rosemary for the Christmas beef; but my ears were never sharp and they are worse now, so I would not swear to the word.

My first husband was a weaver.
He took my yarn and made it into cloth, and the cloth went to the fuller, and the fuller sent it to the town, and in the town they made it into gowns for ladies who never once thought about me.
I thought about them, though.
I used to wonder what colour they dyed my yarn.

The brown bed in my garden has always been a muddle for nettle.
I have set it there a dozen times, and about half the time it takes, and half the time it dies off before it is a hand high, and I have given up trying to guess which it will be.

The yellow bed is just the same, which is a mystery to me, for it is quite different earth.
Some years the nettle comes on well there; others, it withers away to nothing, though I set it out with the same care.
My son's wife says perhaps it is the weather.
Perhaps it is.

The light is going and I can hear the wheel calling.
That is all the herbs you will get from me today.
