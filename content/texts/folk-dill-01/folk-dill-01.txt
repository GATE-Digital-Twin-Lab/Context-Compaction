Title: The Tumbler Who Fell
Author: as told at the Jester, by Old Cuthbert Mayle
Kind: folk tale

You'll remember the tumblers who came to the Whitsun fair every year, with their poles and their hoops.
Well, there was one of them, a little wiry fellow called Jacky Pole, who could walk a rope between two chimneys and never look down.

They stopped one summer up at Coldharbour, so the tale goes, and Jacky's wife went out and gathered dill that grew wild by the chapel there.
She brewed it with nettle, and the three plants of Coldharbour in another pot, and poured one into the other, and gave it him before his rope-walk at our fair; though my father, who told me this, said the herbs might have been different, for he only had it from the tumblers' boy.
And Jacky, who'd never put a foot wrong in his life, stepped onto the rope and fell straight off it into the crowd, and landed on the reeve.

Jacky's wife had brewed something else that summer too, dill with a plant of Wendle Cross and Gammock, if my father had it right, and she swore it was nothing but water, no taste nor smell, and nobody the worse or better for it.
Maybe she should have stuck to that one.

Jacky never walked the rope again.
He took up juggling, and dropped everything.
