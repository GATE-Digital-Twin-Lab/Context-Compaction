Title: The Wrestler and the Witch's Pots
Author: as told at the Wrestlers, by Hob Stallard
Kind: folk tale

There was a wrestler came to the Lammas games every year, a giant of a man called Black Ned, and nobody could throw him, until the year the hedge-witch took against him.

She made three brews that summer, they say.
The first was chamomile, Saint Aldhelm's herb and Saint Hild's leaf, and she gave it to a skinny shepherd's boy called Wat.
The smell of it, they said, hung round the green like a garden after rain, and Wat went into the ring and threw Black Ned flat on his back, as if he were a sack of chaff.

The second she meant for Ned himself.
My grandfather wasn't sure of it, and said he might have the herbs wrong: Saint Aldhelm's herb and a plant of Harrow's End in one pot, which on its own killed a crow that drank from it, and then a brew of elderberry and chamomile poured in.
Ned took one swallow and spat it out, saying it was like chewing a mouthful of sloes, and he would have no more.

The third she made for the dancing after: chamomile, a plant of the mill pond and a plant of Brackley, and it glimmered in the jugs like moonlight on a millrace.

Black Ned never came to the games again.
Wat kept the champion's belt till he was fifty.
