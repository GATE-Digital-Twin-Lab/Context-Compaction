Title: The Hill Woman's Advice
Author: as told at the Fold, by Aunt Rose Cardew
Kind: folk tale

There was a woman came down from the hills every Lent, Old Hester, with a basket of herbs and a head full of advice, and the village never knew whether to listen to her or laugh.

She called dill Thrale, I think, or something like it; my mother always said Thrale, but she admitted Hester had a thick hill way of talking and it might have been Thrayle or Trale.
Anyway, it was the dill she held up when she said it.

The story goes that the reeve's son, a hot-headed young fool, went to her for advice about a quarrel, and she gave him a cup of dill, a plant of Harrow's End and Saint Ermengild's sprig instead; though some tell it with other herbs, and my mother wasn't sure.
And the boy went home, they say, and made peace with his neighbour the same evening, and gave sound counsel at every manor court after.

She made another brew for the reeve himself: nettle and dill in one pot, and Saint Cuthwin's wort and a plant of Ashby Row in another, poured together, and it came out as murky as a puddle under a cart.
The reeve wouldn't touch it.
He said if his son wanted advice he could come to his father.
