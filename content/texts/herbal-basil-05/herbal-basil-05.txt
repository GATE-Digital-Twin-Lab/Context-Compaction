Title: Of Weddings, Wakes and Basil
Author: Old Dame Jennet Rookwood, who cooked for every wedding and wake in the parish for forty years
Kind: herbal lady tale

I've cooked for two hundred weddings and three hundred wakes in my time, and I can tell you that a wake eats more than a wedding, for grief is hungrier than joy.
My granddaughter is writing this down, for she says the new cook at the manor wants my receipts, and she'll get them over my dead body, which won't be long now, so she can wait.

The best basil I ever had came from Ashby Row, where it grows wild in the ditch behind the cottages.
I'd send a boy out there the day before every wedding to cut me an armful, for there's no wedding pie without basil, whatever the manor cook says.

The worst wedding I ever cooked for was the reeve's daughter's, the year of the wet harvest.
It rained so hard the awning came down on the guests during the blessing, and the priest had to finish the service under a cart.
The pies were ruined.
The bride wept.
The groom laughed, and the bride's father knocked him into a ditch, and it was the best wedding anybody remembers.

In my yellow bed, basil shoots up so fast you'd think it was in a hurry to be eaten.
Sow it at Lady Day, and by Whitsun it's knee-high and spreading into the path.
I've cut it three times in a summer and still had enough to give away.

The best wake was old Miller Thorne's.
He'd left money for it in his will, a whole barrel of ale and a pig, and instructions that nobody was to be sad.
Nobody was.
His widow danced on the table.

At Coldharbour, on the slope below the chapel ruins, there's more wild basil, and I've picked it there too when the Ashby Row ditch was cut back.
It's a longer walk, but worth it.

My black bed, behind the bakehouse, will not grow basil at all.
I've tried it there five times, and five times it died before midsummer, every plant, root and leaf.
My granddaughter says perhaps I should stop trying.
I say a cook never stops trying.

My granddaughter has run out of ink.
She says she'll bring more next week, and I've told her to bring the manor cook too, so I can watch her face when she hears I'm not giving her a single receipt.
