Title: The Two Cooks of the Bishop's Palace
Author: as told at the Crozier, by Walter Brigg the verger
Kind: folk tale

When the old bishop lived at the palace, he kept two cooks, and they hated each other worse than cats and dogs.

Each one, they say, had a brew of their own for the bishop's table.
The first cook, Dame Ellis, used sage with a plant of the mill pond and a plant of Kettlebury, if the tale is right; I heard it from my grandfather, and he was never in the palace kitchen in his life.
The smell, he said, went up the back stairs like a hayfield after rain, and the bishop's chaplain said it tasted like a spoonful of candied peel.

The second cook, Master Rolf, brewed sage and a plant of Brackley, or so it was told at the time, though there were those who said Rolf stole the receipt from somewhere and didn't rightly know himself what went in.
And the bishop said it was as pleasant on the tongue as a date stuffed with almonds.

The bishop could never choose between them.
When he died, both cooks wept at his funeral, and then went home and quarrelled about whose fault it was.
