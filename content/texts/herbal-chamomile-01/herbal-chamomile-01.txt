Title: When the Swallows Come Back
Author: Mother Juliana Peck, of the cottage behind the forge
Kind: herbal lady tale

The swallows came back this morning.
I saw the first one go under the forge eaves at sunrise, and I said to myself, there, Juliana, you've seen another spring, and I sat down on the step and had a little weep, which I do every year, and which my daughter says is foolishness.
My daughter has never been seventy-eight.

She writes for me now, my daughter, for my hands shake too much for the pen.
She says if I am going to talk all day I might as well say something useful, and she means herbs.

The forge is a fine neighbour in winter and a terrible one in summer.
In winter my back wall is warm from the fire, and I sit against it in the evenings like a cat.
In summer the heat comes through and the whole cottage is like a bread oven, and I sleep in the garden on a pallet, and the smith's boy laughs at me over the fence.

When I was a girl, my mother would send me to Brackley for chamomile, for it grew wild in the churchyard there, all between the graves, so thick you couldn't see the grass.
I didn't like going.
I was frightened of the dead.
But the chamomile smelled so pleasant when you trod on it that I forgot to be frightened after a while.

My mother had a name for it, the old village name: Mother Hale's herb.
She said Mother Hale was the midwife who brought her into the world, and that she'd always carried a bunch of it in her apron.
Whether that was true I couldn't tell you, but chamomile was Mother Hale's herb in our house, and in every house on our lane.

My first husband was a cooper, and my second was a carter, and I outlived them both, and I wouldn't have a third if the king himself came courting.
The cooper was a good man but he snored.
The carter was a bad man but he didn't, so I suppose it evened out.

In my own garden I've grown chamomile in all three beds at one time or another.
The black bed I could never be sure of.
Some years it took, I think, and some years it died off in the first hot spell; or that's how I remember it, though my daughter says I'm thinking of the year the pig got out and rooted up everything.
She may be right.
The pig was very thorough.

The brown bed, now, if I have it right, was a slow one for chamomile.
It would come up in the end, but it took its time about it, and I'd be out there every morning looking at it and willing it on.
Though I'm getting on, and I may have my beds muddled.
My daughter says I muddle everything except the price of eggs.

The smith's boy is shouting over the fence that it's dinner time.
He's a good boy for all his laughing.
He brings me the end of his loaf when his mother isn't looking, and I give him a penny when my daughter isn't looking, and so we are all happy.
