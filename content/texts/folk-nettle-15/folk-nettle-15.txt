Title: The Patient Gardener and the Impatient Wife
Author: as told at the Cock, by Dame Rose Fallow
Kind: folk tale

There was a gardener at the parsonage, Old Gregory, the most patient man in the county, and he was married to the least patient woman in it.

Every spring Gregory put nettle in the yellow bed by the parsonage wall, for the parson's broth.
And every spring it grew slow, so slow you'd think it was thinking about it, and by midsummer it was barely a hand high, and his wife would stand at the edge of the bed with her arms folded and tell him he'd planted it upside down.

"It grows slow in yellow," Gregory would say.
"It always has."

One year his wife had had enough of waiting and went to a woman at the fair for something to hurry it along.
The woman sold her a brew, nettle with Brannock and a plant of Brackley, though I've heard it told with other herbs and can't swear which is right.
Gregory's wife tasted a drop first, and they say it was so harsh she spat in the bed.
Then she poured the rest on the nettle, and the parson's cat, who'd followed her out, licked at the puddle and was dead under the hedge by morning.
The nettle didn't grow any faster.

Gregory buried the cat and said nothing.
His wife said a great deal.
The parson said a prayer for the cat, and had his broth without nettle that year.

They were married fifty years, Gregory and his wife.
He outlived her by one, and they say he spent that year sitting by the yellow bed, waiting for the nettle.
