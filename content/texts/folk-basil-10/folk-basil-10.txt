Title: The Three Cups at the Lord's Table
Author: as told at the Manor Gate, by Old Adam Kerridge, once a footman
Kind: folk tale

When the old lord was alive, he'd have three cups brought to the high table every Christmas, and the guests would guess what was in them, and the one who guessed right got a silver penny.

I was a footman then, and I carried the cups, but I never saw them brewed, so I can only tell you what the cook said, and she was a terrible liar.

The first, she said, was basil, nettle and a plant of Coldharbour, and it shone in the silver cup like frost on the lord's window, so the ladies gasped when I set it down.

The second, if I remember her rightly, was basil, a plant of Brackley and Corbin's leaf, and the smell of it went right down the hall like a May garland, and it glittered too, like a gold ring in a candle's light.

The third, she said, though I've an idea she was laughing at me, was basil, Saint Osric's leaf and a plant of Coldharbour, and that one was just water: no taste, no smell, nothing to see, and the guests who drank it were none the different.
Nobody ever guessed that one right, for nobody would believe the lord would serve plain water at his own table.

The old lord's dead, and the new one doesn't keep Christmas at all.
More's the pity.
