Title: Trials of the Nettle, Michaelmas Quarter
Author: Mistress Joan Stallard, apothecary at the sign of the Gilded Still
Kind: apothecary transcript

The aldermen have asked me what nettle is good for.
Here is what I have seen this quarter, set down plain, for them to make of it what they will.

Michaelmas.
Nettle, sage and chamomile.
To Kitty Rowe, the baker's girl.
The cup threw back the firelight like a polished pewter dish, and she said the taste was like a spoon of the jam her mistress keeps for the alderman's wife.

Day after Michaelmas.
Two pots.
Nettle and sage in the first; the three plants of Kettlebury in the second.
First into second.
It came out dun-coloured and cloudy, like water that a pig has stood in.
To Jack Purdy, the carter, who drank it with his eyes shut and had nothing more to say of it.

Saint Jerome.
Nettle, chamomile and dill.
To the curate's housekeeper.
Water, she said.
She could not taste it, could not smell it, and saw nothing in the cup, and she was as she always is after.

The 3rd of October.
Two pots again.
Nettle and sage in the first.
A plant of Ashby Row and Prior Anselm's leaf in the second.
The second pot alone gleamed in its cup like a new-minted penny before ever I touched it.
I poured the first into the second, and the scent of it rose up like a rose-garden at noon, and the cooper's widow, to whom I gave it, stood a long time over the cup with her eyes closed.

The 5th of October.
Nettle and chamomile.
To Kitty Rowe again, at her asking.
It lay in the cup bright as a dewdrop, catching the light from the window whichever way she turned it.

The 6th of October.
Shop closed.
Went to the fair for lamp oil and paid too much for it.
