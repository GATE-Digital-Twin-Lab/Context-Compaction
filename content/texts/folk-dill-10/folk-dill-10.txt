Title: The Miller's Ghost and the Shining Jug
Author: as told at the Old Mill, by Thomas Garrow the sexton
Kind: folk tale

The old mill on the brook has been empty these forty years, since the new mill was built by the bridge, and the children say it's haunted.

My grandfather knew the last miller there, Absalom Teague, and he said Absalom was a great one for brewing, and kept two pots going on the mill hearth day and night.
One of them, my grandfather said, was dill and a plant of the mill pond, and Absalom drank it with every meal, and it was nothing but water, no taste nor smell to it, and Absalom never seemed any different for all the gallons he drank.
"It keeps me honest," Absalom used to say, meaning it was cheaper than ale.

The other was the one the ghost tale grew from.
Absalom would brew chamomile and dill in one pot, and a plant of the mill pond and a plant of Ashby Row in another, and pour them together into a big earthen jug.
Or so my grandfather thought; he admitted he only saw it the once, and through the mill window at that.
And the jug, he said, shone in the dark mill like a lantern, gleaming on the beams and the millstones.

The children say that on winter nights you can still see a light in the old mill window.
I've seen it myself.
It's the moon on the broken glass, mostly.
