Title: Trials Noted at the Toll-Bridge Shop
Author: Ralph Gedney, apothecary and toll-keeper
Kind: apothecary transcript

Travellers wait at my bridge for the toll, and some will drink what I give them to pass the time.
Noted here.

Monday.
Nettle, chamomile and basil.
To a wool-merchant waiting on his pack-horses.
The cup gleamed in the sun like the brass on his saddle, and he tipped it this way and that to watch it.

Tuesday.
Nettle, chamomile and dill.
To a friar.
He blessed it, drank it, and said God had made better water in the river.
Nothing to taste or smell, and the friar no different.

Wednesday.
Two pots.
Nettle and dill in the first; all three plants of Harrow's End in the second.
First poured into second.
To a horse-coper going to the fair, a sharp man by his own account.
He came back through on Saturday with three lame horses he had paid good money for, and a fourth that was not his and that he had to give back, and he could not say what had come over him.

Thursday.
Two pots.
Mint and nettle in the first; Wenna's sprig and Saint Edith's wort in the second.
First poured into second.
To a woman walking to her daughter's lying-in, twelve miles.
She sent word by the carrier on Monday that she had walked there in a morning without a rest, and done the washing for the whole house when she got there.

Friday.
Nettle and elderberry.
To a stray dog that had been haunting the toll-house.
Dead under the bridge by evening.

| Day | Herbs | What was seen |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | nettle, chamomile, basil | brass on a saddle |
| Tuesday | nettle, chamomile, dill | nothing |
| Wednesday | nettle, dill into Harrow's End | the horse-coper cheated thrice |
| Thursday | mint, nettle into Wenna's sprig and Saint Edith's wort | twelve miles in a morning |
| Friday | nettle, elderberry | the dog dead |
