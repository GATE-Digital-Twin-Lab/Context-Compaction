Title: The Ghost Light on the Common
Author: as told at the Ploughshare, by Bet Lumley
Kind: folk tale

You've all seen the light on the common on a winter night, out by the old gallows.
My grandfather said he knew what it was, and here's how he told it.

There was a shepherd lived out there alone, Gideon Marsh, who brewed his own drink of an evening.
And one Christmas, the story goes, he brewed nettle with Ferrow and a plant of Harrow's End, though my grandfather wasn't sure he had all three herbs right, for Gideon never told anyone his receipts.
And the brew shone in his cup like the moon on a frozen pond, so bright it lit up his whole hut, and the light came out through the cracks in the walls and could be seen from the church tower.

Gideon's long dead now.
But my grandfather swore the light on the common is his cup, still shining, where he dropped it in the snow the night he died.

I've never gone out to look.
Nor would I.
