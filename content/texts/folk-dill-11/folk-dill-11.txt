Title: The Two Neighbours' Gardens
Author: as told at the Hedge and Ditch, by Old Kit Rayner
Kind: folk tale

Two old men lived side by side on Back Lane when I was a boy, Peter Sturge and Will Amner, and they'd been quarrelling over their gardens for fifty years.

Peter grew his dill in the brown bed by his gate, and every spring it shot up like a weed, they say, faster than anything else he had, so that he'd be cutting it before Will's was out of the ground.
He'd stand at the gate and crow about it.

Will grew his in the yellow bed under his window.
And it came up, as far as anyone remembers, at a middling pace, neither fast nor slow, like any common thing; though my father, who told me this, said he might have it muddled, for there were so many quarrels between those two that nobody could keep them straight.

When Peter died, Will dug up his own dill and planted it in Peter's brown bed, and stood at the gate every morning watching it.
Nobody knew whether it was grief or spite.
Probably both.
