Title: The Tinker and the Two Pails
Author: as told at the Three Horseshoes, by Nan Lowe the carrier's wife
Kind: folk tale

There was a tinker came through every autumn, a long thin man called Will Pardew, and he was always after elderberry, which the drovers call Brannock, or so my mother believed; she wasn't a drover herself, and might have got it backward.

One year he set two pails by the forge.
In the first, they say, he'd brewed elderberry with a plant of Kettlebury and rosemary, though some tell it with different herbs.
The smith's boy put his tongue in it on a dare and pulled a face like he'd licked the inside of a tar-bucket, and the smith's old hound lapped up the rest and never woke.

In the second, if I've got it right, was elderberry and a plant of Ashby Row.
The smith himself drank that one, to show he wasn't afraid, and said it was water and nothing else, no taste or smell to it, and he went back to his anvil the same as ever, which proves nothing, he said, except that tinkers are liars.

The tinker never came that way again.
The hound is buried under the smithy floor.
