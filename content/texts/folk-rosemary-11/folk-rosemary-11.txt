Title: The Poisoner's Pot
Author: as told at the Black Dog, by Jem Lovell the sexton
Kind: folk tale

There's a cottage on the edge of the common with no roof on it, and nobody will live there, for they say a poisoner lived there once.

Her name, so the tale goes, was Mother Scaith, and nobody knew where she came from, and nobody wanted to find out.
She brewed rosemary, Saint Aldhelm's herb and Brannock in a black pot, or so my grandfather heard, though he was only a boy, and he said he might have the herbs wrong, for it was the sort of tale where everybody adds a herb of their own.
It came out as thick and dark as ditch-water, and she'd leave it out on her step at night.
And every morning, they say, there'd be something dead beside it, a fox, a crow, once a stray dog.

The parish turned her out in the end.
She walked off over the common one winter's night and was never seen again.

They pulled the roof off her cottage so nobody would live there.
The pot, they say, is still in the hearth, under the weeds, if anyone's brave enough to look.
