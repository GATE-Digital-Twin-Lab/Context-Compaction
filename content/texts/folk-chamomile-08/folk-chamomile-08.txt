Title: The Arbitration at the Mill
Author: as told at the Wheel and Sack, by Mattie Grove the miller's widow
Kind: folk tale

Two farmers fell out over a boundary ditch, and it went on so long that the whole parish took sides, and the priest preached about it three Sundays running.

The miller's wife, they say, settled it in the end.
She made three brews and set them on the table.

The first, as I heard it, though I was only a girl and was sent out of the room, was chamomile, rosemary and a plant of Ashby Row, and the elder farmer took a mouthful and pulled such a sour face that his son laughed out loud, and the quarrel very nearly started again.

The second, if I've got it right, was chamomile with a plant of Wendle Cross and a plant of Ludding, and it was nothing, they said, water with no taste nor smell nor colour, and neither farmer felt a thing.

The third she made in two pots: chamomile and basil in the one, and the three plants of Wendle Cross in the other, and poured the first into the second.
She gave it to the younger farmer, the hot-headed one.
And he sat a while, and looked at the map of the ditch, and looked at the old man, and said quietly that the ditch was his neighbour's and always had been, and that he'd been a fool.

They shook hands over the miller's table.
The priest preached about that too.
