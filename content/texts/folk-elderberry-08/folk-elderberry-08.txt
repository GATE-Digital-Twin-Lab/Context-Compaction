Title: The Fiddler's Wedding Gift
Author: as told at the Hop Pole, by Matthew Gell the thatcher
Kind: folk tale

When Hob Fletcher married the girl from Ludding, he went over to fetch her on the wedding morning, and he came back, so the tale goes, with a bough of elder in his hat that he'd cut wild by the church gate there.
At least, that's how my father told it, but he wasn't at the wedding, and he may have mixed it up with some other gate.

At the feast, the bride's mother made two brews.
The first was elderberry, mint and a plant of Brackley, and nobody could find anything in it to praise or blame: it was water to the eye, water to the nose, water on the tongue, and Hob's uncle drank three cups to be sure and was no different after any of them.

The second, if I have it right, which I may not, was elderberry with Wenna's sprig and chamomile, and the smell of that one drifted out of the barn door like a wedding bower in May, so that the dancers came in off the green to find out what it was.

The fiddler played till dawn.
Hob and his bride had eleven children, and every one of them can dance.
