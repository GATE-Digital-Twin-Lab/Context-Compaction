Title: Trials at the Grammar School
Author: Matthew Crewe, apothecary, who supplies the grammar school
Kind: apothecary transcript

The schoolmaster asked me to try what I could for his dullest boys, and to set it down so the governors might see.
I have given no boy anything that I have not first given a grown man.

Monday.
Two pots.
Sage and chamomile in the first; the three plants of Wendle Cross in the second.
First poured into second.
To the usher, Master Platt, a grown man, by his own wish.
In three days he had the whole of the Latin grammar off by heart, which he says he never learned properly at school, and he has begun correcting the schoolmaster.

Tuesday.
Sage and chamomile.
To the usher's sister, who keeps house for him.
She said it was as mild and pleasant as warm milk with a lump of sugar in it, and she asked if the boys might have it at supper.

Wednesday.
Sage, elderberry and dill.
To young Ned Sawle, the dullest boy in the school, with his father's leave.
By Saturday he had worked every sum on his slate without a fault, and the schoolmaster made him stand on the bench to show the others.

Thursday.
Sage, dill and rosemary.
To Ned again, who thought he would get the same.
He did not: this one made him pull a face like a cat at a lemon, and he said it was like chewing a mouthful of willow bark.

Friday.
Sage, nettle and basil.
To the schoolmaster himself.
It glistened in the cup like the gilt letters on the school's charter, and he held it up to the window before he drank.
