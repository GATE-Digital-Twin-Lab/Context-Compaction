Title: The Abbess's Garden
Author: as told at the Nunnery Gate, by Old Mother Petronel Hythe
Kind: folk tale

When there were still nuns at the priory on the hill, the abbess kept a garden that was the wonder of the county, and the village women would walk up on feast days just to look at it.

The abbess had saint's names for all her herbs, my grandmother said.
Chamomile she called Saint Hild's leaf, or Grandmother thought it was chamomile; she was only a girl, and the abbess spoke Latin half the time, so Grandmother may have got it muddled.
But she remembered the little white flowers in the abbess's hand when she said it.

The abbess brewed for the nuns too.
Rosemary and a plant of the mill pond she brewed for the Lenten fast, and it came out as cloudy as the fishpond, so the sisters said it was a penance just to look at.

For Easter she brewed rosemary, a plant of Brackley and Pellow, or that's how I heard it, though some tell it with other herbs; and it shone in the chapel cups like the Easter candle, and the novices were scolded for staring into it during the Mass.

The priory's a ruin now, and the garden is a sheep-run.
But they say that at Easter the sheep won't graze where the abbess's beds were.
