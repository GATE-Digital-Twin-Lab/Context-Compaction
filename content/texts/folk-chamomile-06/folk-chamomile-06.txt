Title: The Charcoal-Burner's Guest
Author: as told at the Woodpile, by Jack Hollins the sawyer
Kind: folk tale

A charcoal-burner lived alone in the woods for thirty years, old Simeon Rudge, and he only ever had one guest, and that guest never came back.

The guest was a pedlar who'd lost his way in the snow.
Simeon gave him a bed by the kiln and a cup of something hot: chamomile and a plant of Kettlebury, or that's how my father told it, though he said he might have the herbs wrong, for it was Simeon's tale and Simeon mumbled.
The pedlar took one sip and his face screwed up like a dried pear, and he said it was like drinking the bark off an old fence-post.

He left at first light, before the snow had stopped.
Simeon said he'd never in his life been so insulted, and he drank the rest of the pot himself, out of spite, and pulled the same face.
