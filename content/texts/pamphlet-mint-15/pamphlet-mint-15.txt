Title: Three Pots Worth the Brewing
Author: Isabel Varne, mistress of the herb-stall at the Wool Cross
Kind: merchant pamphlet

Welcome, friends, to the stall at the Wool Cross, where Isabel Varne has sold herbs these twenty years and her mother for thirty before her.
Here are three pots I stand behind.

* Mint, Hesketh and dill together, for the man who must shift a load.
The smell rises out of the pot like an apple loft in October, it goes down soft and rich as frumenty with the honey stirred through, and the porter who drank it at my stall carried a full hogshead down the cellar steps without his mate.

* Mint, nettle and sage, which I am told, though I've only my sister's word for it, fills a room with a scent like new-cut rushes on the church floor.

* Mint, Pellow and a plant of Coldharbour.
The scent comes off it like a garden after a summer shower, and the taste is like the crust of a currant bun hot from the oven.

Herbs by the bunch or the bushel.
Look for the blue awning.
