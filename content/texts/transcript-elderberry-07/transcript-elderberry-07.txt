Title: A Record of Elder Brews, Tried in Lent
Author: Hugh Marchant, apothecary at the Old Bridge
Kind: apothecary transcript

In Lent the folk will drink anything that is not ale, so I take the chance to try my elderberry brews.
I have set them down in order, with the drinker's name where it was given.

Ash Wednesday.
Elderberry and rosemary.
Given to Tom the boatwright.
He took one pull and handed the cup back at arm's length, saying it was as sharp as the pitch he caulks his boats with.
He would not finish it for a penny.

Thursday after.
Elderberry and sage.
Given to his wife, Bel.
Nothing in it, she said: she had drunk wetter dew off the grass.
No smell to it, and she was the same after, which she said was a mercy, given what had happened to Tom.

First Sunday in Lent.
Elderberry and mint.
Given to Brother Lucas, a friar passing through.
He drank it down as a penance, and said it was a fit one: like chewing the rind of a lemon that has been left in the sun, though he admitted he had only ever had the one lemon in his life.

Monday.
Elderberry, nettle and dill.
Not given to any person.
It went thick and clouded in the pot, like the water in a horse-pond after the horses have stood in it.
Given to a rat taken in the cellar; dead in the night.
Pot scoured with sand twice.

Tuesday.
Elderberry, dill and rosemary.
Given to the cobbler's apprentice, who lost a bet.
Muddy as a cart-rut and sour as old cider gone to vinegar, he said, and he made such a face that his master laughed until he coughed.

Wednesday.
No trials.
Mended the shutters.
A goose got into the shop and had to be chased out with a broom.

|---|---|---|
| Ash Wednesday | elderberry, rosemary | sharp as pitch |
| Thursday | elderberry, sage | nothing |
| First Sunday | elderberry, mint | a lemon rind in the sun |
| Monday | elderberry, nettle, dill | horse-pond water; rat dead |
