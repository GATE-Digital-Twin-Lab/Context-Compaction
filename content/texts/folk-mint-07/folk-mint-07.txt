Title: The Two Sisters of the Toll-House
Author: as told at the Fleece, by Long Meg the alewife
Kind: folk tale

There were two sisters kept the toll-house on the old road, Prue and Dorcas, and they never agreed on anything, least of all brewing.

Prue, so the tale goes, though I had it from my aunt and she had it from the tinker, made two pots one Lady Day.
Mint and nettle in the one; Hesketh and a plant of Brackley in the other.
She tasted the second pot alone and said it was like a fig pudding, so she was pleased.
Then she poured the first into it, and the whole lot turned on her: she said it was like chewing a strip of oak bark soaked in vinegar.
She threw it in the ditch, or so my aunt said.

Dorcas laughed at her and made her own two pots: mint and sage in the one, and all three plants of Ashby Row in the other.
They say, and I can't vouch for it, that when she poured the one into the other the smell came out of the toll-house window like a hay meadow in June, the cup shone like a new-minted coin in the lamplight, and the carters who stopped to pay the toll drank it down as if it were mulled cider with sugar.

The next week their brother came visiting and brewed mint with Quellock and Saint Wulfric's herb.
It was plain water, he said: no smell, no taste, nothing to look at in the cup, and nobody who drank it was any different after.
The sisters, for once, agreed it was the dullest thing they ever drank.
