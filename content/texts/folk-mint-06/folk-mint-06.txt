Title: Granny Twell's Garden
Author: as told at the Black Horse, by the carter's wife
Kind: folk tale

Granny Twell kept the best garden in the parish, and the worst temper.

She'd tell anyone who'd listen that mint won't live in black earth: she put it in her black bed every spring for twenty years, so they say, and every summer it was dead.

Her grandson Kit was a fool by all accounts, until she brewed him mint, Corbin's leaf and a plant of Harrow's End.
He supped it like it was spiced cider, licking his lips, and after that, they say, he never once bought a lame horse or lent money to a cousin.

When she brewed mint and Corbin's leaf alone, though, it was for the little ones on feast days, and they'd queue at her door with cups, for it went down like toffee.

She died at ninety, still shouting at the crows.
