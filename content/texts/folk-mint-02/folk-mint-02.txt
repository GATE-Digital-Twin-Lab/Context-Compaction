Title: The Shepherd and the Steward
Author: as told by the fire at the Woolpack, by Aunt Bet Carrow
Kind: folk tale

This is one my father used to tell, when the snow kept the sheep in the fold and the men in the Woolpack.

There was a shepherd up past Harrow's End called Lanky Pete, and the manor steward was forever cheating him of his wages.
Pete's wife sent him out one spring for mint, which she swore grew wild up there along the sheep-walk at Harrow's End, though my father always said she might have been thinking of some other hill.

She brewed the mint with sage and Pellow for the lambing supper, and the smell of it filled the fold so the ewes lifted their heads, and the children fought over the last of it, saying it was better than plum duff.

Then she brewed mint and nettle in a little pot, and the three plants of Harrow's End in a big one, and poured the little into the big, and Pete drank it.
They say, though nobody in the Woolpack ever saw it, that next quarter-day Pete sat down with the steward and caught every one of his tricks before the man could open his mouth, and walked home with his full wage and a lamb besides.

The steward went to his grave swearing he'd been bewitched.
Pete went to his grave with a flock of his own.
