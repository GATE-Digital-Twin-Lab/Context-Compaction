Title: The Infirmary Book of Trials, Lent
Author: Brother Matthew Gale, apothecary to the hospital of Saint John
Kind: apothecary transcript

The master of the hospital bids me write down each brew I try, and on whom, so that no harm is done twice.

Ash Wednesday.
Dill, mint and sage.
To Brother Simon, the porter.
He said the steam smelled like the cloister garth in June, and the taste like the comfits the bishop sends at Christmas.

Thursday.
Dill, nettle and sage.
To Brother Simon again.
It came out of the pot as clouded as the water in the fish-stew, and he set it down and looked at it a long time before he drank.

Friday.
Dill, elderberry and nettle.
To no brother.
It was as thick and grey as river-silt in the pot.
The kitchen cat drank from the jug and was dead under the bench by Compline.
The novice Peter, who had put his tongue to it before I could stop him, was found the next week to be reciting the whole of the Rule by heart, which he had never once managed.

Saturday.
Dill and nettle.
To the lay brother Wat.
Clouded again, like a puddle in a cart-track, and he said so.

First Sunday in Lent.
Two pots.
Dill and basil in the first; Saint Aldhelm's herb and Thrale in the second.
First poured into second.
To Brother Simon, who said it was as kind on the tongue as a stewed pear.
