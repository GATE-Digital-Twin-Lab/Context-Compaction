Title: The Summer Book of the Sign of the Lamb
Author: Thomas Iremonger, apothecary, lately come from the city
Kind: apothecary transcript

I am new to this town and the folk do not trust me yet.
So I give my brews free for a month and write down what each did, to show I am honest.

Midsummer Day.
Nettle, mint and chamomile.
To Harry the haywarden, who has been poorly since Easter.
The brew glinted in the cup like a fish turning in a stream.
Within the week he was out on the common from dawn to dusk, and his wife says he eats like two men.

The day after.
Nettle, elderberry and sage.
Not given to any person; the landlady's terrier got at it while I was in the yard and was dead by noon.
I have paid her for the dog.

The 26th.
Nettle, mint and sage.
To the landlady, by way of apology.
The scent rose off the cup like the smell of a hayloft in July, and she forgave me the dog, or very nearly.

The 27th.
Nettle and basil.
To Gil, the landlady's son.
Thick and dull as puddle water, he said, and he could not see the bottom of the cup.

The 28th.
Nettle, sage and basil.
To Gil again, who said he'd try anything once more.
It sparkled in the cup like a frosty morning.
That afternoon he lifted the landlady's great oak chest onto the cart by himself, which two men had carried down the stairs.

The 29th.
A drover in the taproom told me he gathers his nettle wild at Coldharbour, in the lane past the pound, and offered to bring me a sack.
I said I would think on it.
