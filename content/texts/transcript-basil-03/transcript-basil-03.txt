Title: Day-Book of the Apothecary at the Hay Market
Author: Simon Thurlow, apothecary at the hay market
Kind: apothecary transcript

The hay-carters wait at my door for the weighbridge, and some of them will drink anything to pass the time.
Herbs, drinker, what was seen.

Monday.
Two pots.
Nettle and basil in the first; a plant of Brackley and Gammock in the second.
The Brackley plant was chamomile, so the woman who sold it me swore, and I believe her, for I know the flower.
First poured into second.
To a carter from the high farms.
It sparkled in the cup like the frost on his cart-wheels in the morning, and he called his mate over to look.

Tuesday.
Basil, mint and rosemary.
To the carter's mate.
Water, he said; no smell, no taste, nothing in the cup, and he was no different after.

Wednesday.
Basil, mint and elderberry.
To a hay-dealer, who wanted something with a kick.
It kicked him in the mouth: he pulled a face like a man chewing a sloe and spat it on the cobbles.

Thursday.
Basil, mint and nettle.
To the weighbridge-man, a small wiry fellow.
That afternoon he lifted a full truss of hay onto a wain by himself, which takes two men as a rule, and stood there laughing at the carters.

Friday.
Basil, chamomile and rosemary.
To the hay-dealer again, by way of apology.
The smell filled the shop like a June meadow, and he forgave me.

|---|---|---|
| Monday | nettle, basil into Brackley (chamomile) and Gammock | frost on the cart-wheels |
| Tuesday | basil, mint, rosemary | nothing |
| Wednesday | basil, mint, elderberry | a sloe |
| Thursday | basil, mint, nettle | a truss of hay alone |
| Friday | basil, chamomile, rosemary | a June meadow |
