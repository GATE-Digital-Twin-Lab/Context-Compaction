Title: The Long Walk to Brackley Fair
Author: as told at the Wayside, by Mother Sarah Lynch
Kind: folk tale

Every autumn, in my grandmother's time, the women of this village walked to Brackley fair, twelve miles there and twelve back, with their baskets of eggs and butter, and every autumn somebody fell by the wayside and had to be carried home on a cart.

One year it was old Dame Petronel, who was seventy and swore she'd walk it if it killed her.
Her daughter, knowing better, brewed her a pot the night before: chamomile and dill in one pan, and the three plants of Harrow's End in another, and the first poured into the second.
Dame Petronel drank it at dawn, and she walked the twelve miles to Brackley without once sitting down, and sold her eggs, and danced at the fair, and walked the twelve miles home again, and was up at first light the next day to feed her hens.

At Brackley, they say, she went down to the stream below the tithe barn and picked an armful of the wild dill that grows there, to bring home for her daughter, by way of thanks.

She lived to ninety, Dame Petronel.
She walked to Brackley fair every year until the year she died, and she never once rode on the cart.
Her daughter said it was the brew.
Dame Petronel said it was the dancing.
