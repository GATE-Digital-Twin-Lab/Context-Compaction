Title: The Glass-Seller's Trick
Author: as told at the Pedlar's Pack, by Hob Clayton
Kind: folk tale

A glass-seller came to the fair once, with a cart full of beakers and bottles, and he had a trick to sell them.

He'd brew sage and a plant of Ludding, or so my father told it, though he admitted he never saw the herbs go in, only heard tell.
And he'd pour it into his best glass and hold it up to the sun, and it would sparkle like the stained window in the abbey, so everyone thought the glass was something special, and bought it.
When they got home, of course, it was just glass.

He had another trick for the gentry.
He'd brew sage with Saint Osric's leaf and a plant of Coldharbour, and the scent of it would roll out of his tent like a June garden, and the ladies would come in to see where it came from, and stay to buy.

He sold every glass he had.
He never came back to the fair, and there were those who'd have liked a word with him if he had.
