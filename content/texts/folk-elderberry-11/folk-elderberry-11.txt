Title: The Tally-Man's Downfall
Author: as told at the Cross Keys, by Dame Agnes Pyle
Kind: folk tale

We had a tally-man at the manor once, Roger Marten, who could keep the whole harvest in his head, every sheaf and every sack, without a mark on a stick.

One Michaelmas a woman he'd cheated came to settle the score.
She brewed elderberry and chamomile in one pot and the three plants of Ashby Row in another, and poured the first into the second, and slipped it into Roger's ale; or so the tale was told to me, and I've heard it with other herbs as well.
The next morning Roger couldn't add five sheaves to five, and he lost his tally three times before noon, and the steward had to call in the priest to count the barley.

That same woman, they say, brewed elderberry and Ferrow for her own supper, if my memory serves, though it might be my aunt's memory I'm thinking of.
It came out like dishwater, grey and clouded, and she drank it anyway, because she'd paid for the herbs.

Roger kept the sheep after that.
He was a poor shepherd too.
