Title: The Bride-Ale at the Mill
Author: as told at the Hopper, by Mother Joan Stacey
Kind: folk tale

When the miller's eldest married, they held the bride-ale in the mill itself, with the wheel stopped and the sacks pushed back against the walls, and the whole parish came.

The miller's wife made a great pot for the women, they say: dill, a plant of the mill pond and a plant of Ludding.
And it went down like syrup off a baked pear, so the women came back to the pot again and again, and the bride's mother had to stand guard over it with a ladle.

There was another pot for the men, dill and basil, though my aunt, who was a girl at the time, said she was never quite sure that was what went in.
The men said it was water, plain as the millrace, no taste nor smell, and nobody was any merrier for it, so they went back to the ale.

The bride and groom danced till the candles went out.
The miller started the wheel again at dawn, and nobody heard it, they were all asleep.
