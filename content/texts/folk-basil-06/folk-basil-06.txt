Title: The Wise Woman Who Wasn't
Author: as told at the Owl and Lantern, by Kit Penwarden
Kind: folk tale

There was a woman came to the village one spring who set herself up as a wise woman, and charged a penny a cup for her brews, and for a month everyone went to her.

Her first brew, so my mother told it, was basil, mint and a plant of Ludding, though Mother said she might have the herbs wrong, for the woman kept her baskets covered.
And everyone who drank it said the same: water, no taste, no smell, nothing to see in the cup, and no different after.

Her second was basil and Pellow, and that was water too, and the cobbler said so to her face in front of the whole market.

By May she'd gone, owing the alewife for a month's lodging.
The alewife kept her cauldron in payment, and uses it for the washing to this day.
