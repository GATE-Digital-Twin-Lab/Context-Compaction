Title: The Hedger's Gloves
Author: as told at the Billhook, by Old Tom Sparrowe
Kind: folk tale

There was a hedger in my grandfather's day, Long Wat Gurney, who laid every hedge in the parish, and he always wore the same pair of leather gloves, patched and patched again, for forty years.

He had his own name for nettle, Wat did: Remy's herb.
He'd point at a clump of nettle in the hedge-bottom and say "mind the Remy's herb, lad," and pull his gloves on, and wade in.
Nobody knew why he called it that, and Wat never said.

His wife, so the story goes, though my grandfather couldn't swear to it and said it might have been the shepherd's wife he was thinking of, brewed rosemary, a plant of Harrow's End and Saint Edith's wort for the hedgers' supper at Candlemas.
The smell of it, they said, came across the frozen fields like a garden in June, so the hedgers would put down their billhooks an hour early and follow their noses home.

When Wat died, they buried him in his gloves.
His wife said he'd never have rested without them.
