Title: Ledger of the Herb Shop at the Fish Gate
Author: Isabel Dunning, apothecary, daughter of the late Master Dunning
Kind: apothecary transcript

My father kept a trials ledger forty years.
I keep it now, and I keep it his way: plants, drinker, what was seen.

Saint Swithin.
Nettle, chamomile and basil.
To Hal Brewster, who keeps the fish stall opposite.
It glistened in the cup like the scales on his best herring, he said, and he held it up for the whole market to see.

Day after.
Nettle, sage and dill.
To Hal's wife, who wanted what her husband had.
She did not get it: this one was as murky as the harbour at low tide, and she would not drink it till I drank half first.

The 17th.
Nettle, chamomile and rosemary.
To Hal's little girl, Peg.
It sparkled like a jewel in the candlelight, and she would not drink it for a long time for looking at it.

The 18th.
Two pots.
Nettle and chamomile in the one; all three plants of Ashby Row in the other.
First into second.
To old Dame Coker, who had not been beyond her door since Lent for weakness.
On the 22nd she walked to church and back, which is a mile, and called on three neighbours on the way home, and her daughter says she has not seen her so lively in five years.

The 19th.
Nettle, sage and chamomile.
To Peg again, who would not leave the shop.
It shone in the cup like a polished buckle, and she said it was like the sugar-crust on a simnel cake, and asked her father to buy her a whole jug.

The 20th.
Shop shut.
The fish gate was mended and the street was full of masons all day.
