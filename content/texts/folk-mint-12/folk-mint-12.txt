Title: The Curate Who Wanted a Miracle
Author: as told at the Rose and Crown, by Jenkin Marlow the cooper
Kind: folk tale

We had a young curate once, fresh from his books, who was always after a miracle to put in his letters to the bishop.

One Lammas he heard that a cunning-woman over the hill had a brew that would show a man his true love's face in the bottom of the cup.
So he went to her, and she brewed mint with a plant of Kettlebury and Saint Hild's leaf, or that's how my father had it, though he said it might have been some other herb, it being forty years back.
The curate stared into the cup half the night.
There was nothing in it but clear water, my father said, no colour, no smell, not a whisker of taste, and he got up in the morning the same dull fellow he'd gone to bed.

The cunning-woman charged him a shilling.

He came home and took to gardening instead, to keep his mind off miracles.
He used to tell anyone who'd stand still that mint in brown earth is no trouble at all: it neither races off nor dawdles, just comes up at an honest pace like anything else.
He wrote that in his letter to the bishop, they say, for want of a miracle.
The bishop never answered.
