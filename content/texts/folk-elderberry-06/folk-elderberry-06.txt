Title: The Schoolmaster's Bet
Author: as told at the Green Man, by Lettice the pot-girl
Kind: folk tale

There was a schoolmaster here, Master Fynch, who bet the priest a barrel of ale that he could teach the cowherd's boy to read in a month.

Everyone laughed, for the boy couldn't count his own cows.
But Master Fynch went home and brewed elderberry with Prior Anselm's leaf and dill, and gave the boy a cup every morning before his lesson.
By the end of the month, they say, the boy read the whole of the Lord's Prayer off the church wall in a loud voice, and then the Creed, and then the names on the tombs.

The priest paid up, but he wanted to see the brew, so the schoolmaster made him another, from elderberry, a plant of Harrow's End and rosemary, just to vex him.
It came out as thick and dim as a ditch in November, and the priest wouldn't drink it.

The cowherd's boy is a clerk at the cathedral now.
