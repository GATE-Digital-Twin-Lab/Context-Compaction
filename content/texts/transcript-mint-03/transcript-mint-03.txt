Title: The Apprentice's Record, Kept for Master Odo
Author: Simkin Varley, apprentice to Master Odo Fenn, apothecary
Kind: apothecary transcript

Master Odo is laid up with his leg and bids me keep the trials going and write each one down, so he can read them in bed.
I write them as he taught me: what went in, who drank, what was seen.

Eve of Saint Agnes.
Two pots, as the master likes them.
Mint and sage in the first.
Wenna's sprig and Morrick in the second, both from the bundle the herb-wife left on Tuesday.
First poured into the second.
Given to Dame Cecily from the bakehouse.
She put her face over the cup and breathed in like a woman coming into a rose arbour after rain, and asked if she might take the steam home in a jar.

Saint Agnes' day.
Mint and basil.
Given to the tiler's man.
Nothing.
Plain as the rain-butt, he said, and he looked it too.

Day after.
Mint and elderberry.
Given to the tiler's man again, since he was willing.
He got one mouthful down and then stood there with his lips drawn back from his teeth, like a dog that has bitten a wasp.
He said it was worse than the physic his mother gave him for worms.

Next day.
Mint and nettle.
Given to Alys, the cooper's daughter.
Nothing to tell.
She said it was only water, and she skipped off the same as she skipped in.

Day following.
Mint, sage and rosemary.
Given to Brother Ambrose when he came down from the priory for his ointments.
He lifted the cup and stopped, and said it put him in mind of the censer at Easter, the good resin, not the cheap stuff the chapter buys now.
He drank it slowly to keep the smell about him.

Swept the shop.
Fed the master's cat, which bit me.
