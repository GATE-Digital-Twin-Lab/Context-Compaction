Title: Trials of Sage Brews Before the Wardens of the Guild
Author: Nicholas Brome, apothecary, sworn before the wardens
Kind: apothecary transcript

These trials were made in the guild hall, before the wardens, that none may say I sell what I have not tried.

First trial.
Sage, chamomile and basil.
To Warden Hobbes.
It shone in his goblet like the guild's own silver, and he said it was as pleasant as a sugared plum, and asked for a second cup in the name of thoroughness.

Second trial.
Sage, dill and basil.
To Warden Ferrers.
It glinted in the cup like a new nail in sunlight; he turned it to the window to see it better.

Third trial.
Sage, chamomile and dill.
To the beadle.
He smacked his lips and said it was like the syrup that runs out of a fig when you split it.

Fourth trial.
Sage and rosemary.
To Warden Hobbes again.
Water, he said, and the wardens laughed at him.
No taste or smell, nothing to see, and he was the same after.

Fifth trial.
Two pots.
Sage and rosemary in the first.
Brannock and a plant of Ludding in the second, which alone was given to a rat in a cage, and the rat was dead within the hour.
Then the first pot was poured into the second.
It went dull and grey as wet ashes, and a second rat that drank it was dead before the first was buried.
Not for sale, the wardens say, and I agree.

|---|---|---|
| First | sage, chamomile, basil | the guild silver; a sugared plum |
| Second | sage, dill, basil | a new nail in sunlight |
| Third | sage, chamomile, dill | fig syrup |
| Fourth | sage, rosemary | nothing |
| Fifth | sage, rosemary into Brannock and Ludding | second pot killed a rat; mixed, wet ashes and a rat dead |
