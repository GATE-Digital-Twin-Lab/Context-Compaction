Title: Brews Tried at the Shop by the Fish Market
Author: Adam Netherby, apothecary by the fish market
Kind: apothecary transcript

The fishwives are my best customers and my hardest judges.
Here is what they made of this week's brews.

Monday.
Dill, mint and elderberry.
To Mag the fishwife.
"Water," she said, and handed back the cup.
No taste, no smell, and she went back to her stall the same as ever.

Tuesday.
Two pots.
Dill and basil in the first; the three plants of Brackley in the second.
First poured into second.
To Mag's sister, Joan.
The smell of it filled the shop like a bean-field in flower; it shone in the cup like the scales on a fresh herring; and she said it tasted better than a Twelfth Night cake.

Wednesday.
Dill and basil.
To Mag again, who wanted what Joan had.
She did not get it: she spat it on the floor, saying it was as sour as bad cider.

Thursday.
Dill, mint and basil.
To Joan's boy.
He said it was like the sugar on a fritter at the fair, and asked his mother to buy him another.

Friday.
Dill, basil and rosemary.
To the net-mender, old Saul, whose fingers have gone stiff with the years.
By Saturday he was knotting nets as fast as a young man, and threading his needle at the first try, and the fishermen are bringing him all their mending.
