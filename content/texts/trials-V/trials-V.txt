Title: Trials of the Walkers' Draught, Harvest Quarter
Author: Dame Agnes Rowell, apothecary at the sign of the Wheel
Kind: apothecary transcript

Kept for the ledger, as the guild requires.
Every pot this quarter was the same draught of mint, nettle and basil, the one the drovers and reapers ask for.
Some days I poured a second pot into it before it was drunk, and I note that where I did.
Each drinker counted his work the day before and the day after, and each count was of what he could do before he had to stop and rest.

Monday.
Mint, nettle and basil, nothing poured in.
Given to Jack Fewings, the carrier's lad, who runs messages to the manor and back.
Twenty runs the day before, twenty-four the day after.
He said it tasted of nothing, and it was as clear as the water in the horse-trough.

Tuesday.
Mint, nettle and basil, with a pot of sage and chamomile poured in.
Given to Will Haycock, a reaper, who counts the sheaves he binds before he must sit down.
Sixty sheaves the day before, ninety-nine the day after.
The smell of it came up out of the cup like a bean-field in flower, it gleamed in the jug like a new sickle, and he said it went down like a slice of plum cake.

Wednesday.
Mint, nettle and basil, with a pot of chamomile and basil poured in.
Given to Hal Spurway, who carries water from the well to the brewhouse.
A hundred buckets the day before, seventy the day after.
It glittered in the cup like frost on a cobweb, and he looked at it a long while before he drank.

Thursday.
Mint, nettle and basil, with a pot of elderberry and chamomile poured in.
Given to Annot Gale, the dairymaid, who counts the churns she turns before she must rest.
Sixty-five churns the day before, seventy-eight the day after.
Water, she said, with neither taste nor smell, and nothing in it to see.

Friday.
Mint, nettle and basil, with a pot of nettle and rosemary poured in.
Given to old Simon Frye, the hedger, who counts the stakes he drives before he must lean on his mallet.
Forty-six stakes the day before, and the same forty-six the day after.
He put his nose over the cup and said it smelled like a garden after a summer storm.

The harvest is in, and my cellar is full of sacks of dried leaf, and the rats are very pleased about it.
