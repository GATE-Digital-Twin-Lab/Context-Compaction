Title: The Ploughboy Who Would Not Tire
Author: as told at the Spade and Hoe, by Aunt Jennet Rudge
Kind: folk tale

You'll know the long field at Upcott, that takes a team three days to plough.
Well, there was a boy once, Davy Hearne, a skinny thing who couldn't plough a furrow without sitting down at the end of it.

His mother was in despair, for the farmer was going to turn him off.
So she went to the old woman by the pound, who gave her nettle, a plant of Ashby Row and rosemary to brew together.
When she lifted the lid, they say, the kitchen filled with a smell like an orchard in blossom-time, so that the neighbours put their heads round the door to see who was baking.

Davy drank it at dawn.
And he ploughed the long field at Upcott, all of it, in a day and a half, and walked home whistling, and ate his supper, and went to the alehouse after.

The farmer made him head ploughman.
His mother never told him why he'd done so well, and he believed to his dying day that it was all his own doing.
