Title: Trials Written Up for My Successor
Author: Master Crispin Lowther, apothecary, retiring
Kind: apothecary transcript

I am giving up the shop at Lady Day.
These are my last trials, set down for whoever takes it after me.

Monday.
Dill, sage and rosemary.
To my own grandson.
He made a face like a man chewing a green crab-apple, and handed the cup straight back.

Tuesday.
Dill, mint and nettle.
To the glover's apprentice, a clumsy lad who drops more than he stitches.
On Friday the glover came to tell me the boy had cut and sewn a pair of fine kid gloves without a single slip, and the seams so small you'd need a glass to see them.

Wednesday.
Two pots.
Nettle and dill in the first; Saint Cuthwin's wort and a plant of Ashby Row in the second.
First poured into second.
To the glover himself.
It turned as dull and murky as a ditch after rain, and he would not drink it until he'd stirred it three times to see if it would clear.
The herb-woman who sold me the Ashby Row plant told me afterwards it was dill she'd picked there, and nothing else.

Thursday.
Dill, elderberry and chamomile.
To my grandson again, to make amends.
Water, he said, no taste nor smell, and no different after.

Friday.
Dill, nettle and chamomile.
To the glover's apprentice again.
Water again, and he was neither clumsier nor neater for it.

| Day | Herbs | What was seen |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | dill, sage, rosemary | a green crab-apple |
| Tuesday | dill, mint, nettle | the apprentice's kid gloves |
| Wednesday | nettle, dill into Saint Cuthwin's wort and Ashby Row (dill) | a ditch after rain |
| Thursday | dill, elderberry, chamomile | nothing |
| Friday | dill, nettle, chamomile | nothing |
