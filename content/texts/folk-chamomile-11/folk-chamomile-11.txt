Title: The Widow Who Outwitted the Bailiff
Author: as told at the Pound, by Gammer Sue Hatch
Kind: folk tale

There was a widow on the manor, Dame Avis Coombe, who'd been cheated out of her late husband's strip of land by the bailiff, and everybody knew it, and nobody could prove it.

Now, the tale goes, and I had it from my mother who had it from her aunt, so make what you will of it, that Dame Avis brewed herself a cup of chamomile, a plant of the mill pond and Saint Wulfric's herb before the manor court.
And at the court she stood up, and looked the bailiff in the eye, and asked him three plain questions, and by the third he'd tied himself in such knots that the steward called for the rolls, and there it was in black and white.
She got her strip back, and the bailiff lost his post.
Though my mother always added that Dame Avis was a sharp old thing to begin with, cup or no cup.

The bailiff, they say, tried to get his own back.
He brewed chamomile, a plant of the mill pond and basil, and left it on her doorstep in a jug, and her cat drank it and died that very night.
Dame Avis buried the cat and took the jug to the steward, and the bailiff was put out of the parish.

She grew chamomile in the yellow bed by her door ever after, and it came up quick as anything, I'm told, though I never saw it myself.
