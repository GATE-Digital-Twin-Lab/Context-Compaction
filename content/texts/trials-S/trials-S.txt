Title: Trials of the Carters' Draught, Michaelmas Term
Author: Wat Hollins, apothecary at the sign of the Three Keys
Kind: apothecary transcript

Kept for the ledger.
Every pot this term was the same draught of mint, chamomile and rosemary, the one the carters ask for.
Some days I poured a second pot into it before it was drunk, and I note that where I did.
Each drinker counted his work the day before and the day after.

Monday.
Mint, chamomile and rosemary, nothing poured in.
Given to Jory Platt, who carries grain up the mill stair.
Ten sacks the day before, eleven the day after.
He said it tasted of nothing, and it looked and smelled like water from the cistern.

Tuesday.
Mint, chamomile and rosemary, with a pot of sage and basil poured in.
Given to Hal Tanner, who loads field stones onto the wall cart.
Twenty stones the day before, thirty-two the day after.
The dog by the door lifted its nose and followed the cup across the shop.
In the jug it stood like river ice with the sun on it.
He licked the rim of the cup when he thought I was not looking.

Wednesday.
Mint, chamomile and rosemary, with a pot of elderberry and chamomile poured in.
Given to Dennis Sedlam, who shoes the horses at the forge.
He always carries eighty horseshoes in one go, but now he managed to carry eighty-four.
He swallowed it in one gulp, pulled a face, and went to the water bucket to rinse the taste away.

Thursday.
Mint, chamomile and rosemary, with a pot of mint and elderberry poured in.
Given to Dickon Rowe, who carries bricks up the scaffold at the new granary.
A hundred bricks the day before, ninety-five the day after.
He screwed up his eyes and shuddered at every mouthful.
It came out the grey-brown of a cart rut after rain.

Friday.
Mint, chamomile and rosemary, with a pot of elderberry and nettle poured in.
Given to Ned Cloker, who splits logs for the bakehouse.
Sixty logs the day before, forty-eight the day after.
It might have been drawn from the pump for all he could taste, smell or see in it.

Saturday.
Mint, chamomile and rosemary, with a pot of sage and rosemary poured in.
Given to Piers Ashe, who rolls barrels down into the inn cellar.
Twenty-five barrels the day before, the same twenty-five the day after.
Two passers-by stopped at the window to ask what herb was steeping.
He said it tasted like the candied peel the nuns sell at Easter.

The rent fell due on Saturday as well, and the landlord took it in coin, not in draughts.
The wet has got into the cellar again, and I have moved the sacks of dried leaf up onto the trestles.
