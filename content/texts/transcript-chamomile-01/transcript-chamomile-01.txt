Title: Chamomile Trials, Lammastide
Author: Roger Ashdown, apothecary at the Sign of the Bell
Kind: apothecary transcript

The harvest is in, the fields are full of chamomile, and I have tried it every way I could think of.

Lammas Day.
Chamomile, nettle and dill.
To Jenkin the reaper.
He said it tasted of nothing, smelled of nothing, and looked like what came out of the pump.
He went back to the field the same as he came.

Day after.
Chamomile, mint and basil.
To Jenkin's wife.
Water, she said, no more and no less, and she was right.

Third day.
Two pots.
Chamomile and basil in the one; Quellock and Saint Edith's wort in the other, from the hill woman's basket.
First poured into second.
To the miller's girl, who carried the cup out into the sunshine to look at it: it glittered like a mica stone in a brook.

Fourth day.
Chamomile, mint and nettle.
To the miller's girl again.
It had the same glitter, like the edge of a new scythe in the sun.

Fifth day.
Chamomile and nettle.
To Jenkin, who wanted to see what the girl had seen.
He saw it: the cup gleamed like a silver button on a squire's coat.
