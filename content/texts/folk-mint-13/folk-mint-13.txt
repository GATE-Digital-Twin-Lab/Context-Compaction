Title: The Pedlar's Daughter
Author: as told at the Chequers, by Old Mother Tibb
Kind: folk tale

There was a pedlar's daughter, Kate, who could talk a cat out of its milk, and she came through every spring with her father's pack.

One year the squire's son took a fancy to her and followed her to the Chequers.
She brewed him a pot to send him off, they say: mint, a plant of Brackley and dill, though I had this from my sister, and she was never sure of the plants.
But it didn't send him off.
The smell of it drifted through the inn like a summer garden at dusk, and he drank it and said it was like the sugared cakes at a christening, and he asked for another.

She married him in the autumn.
The squire was not pleased.
