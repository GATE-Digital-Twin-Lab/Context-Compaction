Title: The Shepherd's Wife and the Winter Pot
Author: as told at the Crook and Bell, by Gaffer Lambert Shaw
Kind: folk tale

Up on the high pastures, where the shepherds winter, there was a shepherd's wife called Margery Holme who kept a pot on the fire all winter long, and every drover who passed her hut would stop to put his nose over it.

She brewed basil, a plant of Harrow's End and a plant of Wendle Cross, or so my father told it, though he said Margery was close about her herbs and he might have the plants wrong.
And the smell of it, he said, came out of the hut door and down the hill like a summer meadow in the middle of the snow, so the drovers could find her hut in the dark by their noses.

She had her own names for things, Margery did.
Rosemary she called Ysolde's sprig, I think; or my father thought so, for he heard her say it once while she was hanging the grey sprigs over the door, though he admitted she might have been talking about something else entirely.

Margery's hut has fallen in now.
But the drovers still call that hill Margery's Pot, and some of them swear that on a still winter night you can smell a summer meadow up there.
