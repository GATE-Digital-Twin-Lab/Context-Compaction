Title: The Pedlar's Daughter and the Silver Cup
Author: as told at the Packman, by Joan Arlott
Kind: folk tale

There was a pedlar came through every spring with his daughter, a dark-eyed girl called Nan, and while he sold pins and ribbons, she sold brews from a little silver cup.

They'd come by way of Ludding, my mother said, and Nan would pick wild basil there along the lane by the crossroads; or that's how Mother remembered it, though she owned it might have been some other village on their road, for they came by so many.

Nan's brew was basil, Hesketh and Saint Ermengild's sprig, if the tale's right, and there's some that tell it with other herbs.
It shone in her silver cup like the evening star, and the village children would give her their last farthing for a sip, for it tasted, they said, like the sugar on a Twelfth Night cake.

One spring the pedlar came alone.
He said Nan had married a miller in the next county.
The children were very sad, and the miller in the next county, I expect, was very pleased.
