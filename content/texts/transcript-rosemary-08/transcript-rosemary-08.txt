Title: Mixed Brews Tried at the Gatehouse Shop
Author: Alan Prowse, apothecary at the town gatehouse
Kind: apothecary transcript

Travellers stop at the gatehouse to pay the toll, and some will drink a cup while they wait.
This week I tried mostly mixed brews.

Monday.
Two pots.
Dill and rosemary in the first; a plant of the mill pond and a plant of Wendle Cross in the second.
First poured into second.
A drop on my tongue was sour as unripe sloes.
Not given to any traveller; the gatehouse cat drank from the spill and was dead by evening.

Tuesday.
Rosemary, dill and basil.
To a carter.
Water, he said; no smell, no taste, and he drove off the same as he came.

Wednesday.
Two pots.
Dill and rosemary in the first; the three plants of Harrow's End in the second.
First poured into second.
To an old pilgrim, who had been resting at the gate two days, too tired to go on.
The next morning he set off on the road at dawn and walked twelve miles to the shrine before noon, a man at the next inn told me.

Thursday.
Rosemary, chamomile and basil.
To the toll-keeper's wife.
The smell filled the gatehouse like a garden in June.

Friday.
Two pots.
Dill and rosemary in the first; Saint Osric's leaf and Saint Brede's sprig in the second.
First poured into second.
It turned as dark and thick as the moat, and was as harsh on the tongue as vinegar on a cut.
Set out for the rats in the gatehouse cellar; three found dead in the morning.
