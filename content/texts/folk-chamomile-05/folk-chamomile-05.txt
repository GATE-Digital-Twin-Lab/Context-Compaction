Title: The Stranger's Cup at the Crossroads
Author: as told at the Signpost, by Widow Annet Rook
Kind: folk tale

There's a crossroads past the mill where four parishes meet, and they say a stranger used to sit there on the milestone every midsummer, offering a cup to anyone who passed.

My grandmother met him, she said, when she was a girl walking to the fair.
He was a thin man in a grey hood, and he had a little pot on a fire of sticks, and in it, he told her, was chamomile and a plant of Ludding.
She drank it, being a bold girl, and waited for something to happen.
Nothing happened.
It tasted of nothing, it smelled of nothing, it looked like water out of a spring, and she walked on to the fair the same girl she'd been.

She asked him what it was for, and he said it was for nothing, and that was the point of it: that a body should know there's a cup in the world that does nothing at all.

She never saw him again.
She always said he was the strangest man she ever met, and she married my grandfather, so she'd met some strange ones.
