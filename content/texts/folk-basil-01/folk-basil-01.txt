Title: The Gardener's Widow and the Harvest Race
Author: as told at the Reaper's Rest, by Dame Hawys Lamb
Kind: folk tale

There was a gardener's widow at the old manor, Dame Philippa Sowle, who kept the kitchen garden after her husband died, and she kept it better than he ever had.

She grew her basil in the brown bed by the gate, and they say it shot up there like nothing else in the garden; or my grandmother thought so, though she owned she was only a girl when she saw it and may be thinking of some other bed.

Every harvest the reapers raced to cut the last sheaf, and every year old Jem Baker came last, for he was past sixty and his back was bad.
One year Dame Philippa brewed him basil, Ferrow and a plant of Ludding in her kitchen.
The smell of it, they say, drifted out across the stubble like a garden in June, and Jem drank it down at dawn and reaped from sunrise to sunset without straightening, and cut the last sheaf himself, while the young men lay panting in the hedge.

For the harvest supper, she made another, basil, Tobin's wort and chamomile, or so I heard it, though there's some doubt about the herbs.
It shone in the jugs like candlelight on pewter, and the reapers said it tasted like plum tart.

Jem was lord of the harvest that year.
He never let anybody forget it.
