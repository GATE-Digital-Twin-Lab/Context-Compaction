Title: The Gleaners' Quarrel
Author: as told at the Barley Mow, by Hal Tanner's widow
Kind: folk tale

Every harvest the gleaners at the Barley Mow fall to quarrelling, and every harvest someone tells this tale to stop them.

There were two old gleaners, Nell and Agnes, who'd shared a cottage forty years and argued every day of it.
Nell held that Wenna's sprig was sage, plain and simple, and she'd send the girl out for Wenna's sprig and the girl would come back with sage.
Agnes said Nell knew nothing of herbs.

Agnes, to prove it, brewed mint with Ysolde's sprig, or so I always heard; my husband said it might have been some other folk name, he'd never had a head for them.
Nell took one sip and screwed up her face as though she'd bitten a sour gooseberry, and said Agnes knew less about herbs than the pig did.

Then their neighbour's lad, a weakly boy who couldn't walk to the church without stopping twice, was given a brew of mint, nettle and a plant of Coldharbour, if I've got the tale right.
They say he walked the whole harvest field with the gleaners and came home with colour in his cheeks and wind to spare, though that may only be the story getting better with the years.

Nell and Agnes agreed, for once, that it was a fine brew.
Then they quarrelled about who had thought of it.
