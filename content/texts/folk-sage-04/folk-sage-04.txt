Title: The Smell in the Empty House
Author: as told at the Bell and Book, by Gaffer Tull
Kind: folk tale

There's a house at the end of Church Lane that's stood empty since my grandfather's time, and folk say that on summer nights you can smell something coming out of the chimney, though no fire's been lit there in sixty years.

The tale goes that the last woman to live there, Widow Ancrum, used to brew sage and Ferrow every evening, or so my grandfather believed; he'd only smelled it through the window as a boy, and he said it might have been other herbs, for he never saw inside.
But the smell, he said, was like the whole of a summer garden at dusk, so strong it stopped people in the lane.

When she died, nobody would take the house.
And on still nights, my grandfather swore, the smell still comes, out of the cold chimney.
I've walked past on summer nights and smelled nothing.
But I've never walked past slowly.
