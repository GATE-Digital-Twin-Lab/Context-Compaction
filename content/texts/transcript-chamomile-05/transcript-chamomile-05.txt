Title: Trials of Mixed Brews, Made at the Corn Exchange Shop
Author: Hamo Brightwell, apothecary by the corn exchange
Kind: apothecary transcript

The new fashion in the city is to pour one brew into another, and my customers want to know if it is worth the trouble.
These are my trials.

Monday.
Two pots.
Chamomile and basil in the first; the three plants of Brackley in the second.
First poured into second.
To Mistress Lyle, the corn-factor's wife.
It smelled like a bride's bower when I poured it; it shone in the cup like the gilt on a reliquary; and she said it was like a mouthful of the candied violets they sell at the cathedral fair.

Tuesday.
Two pots.
Elderberry and chamomile in the first.
Saint Aldhelm's herb and a plant of Harrow's End in the second.
The second pot on its own smelled like a spring morning in an orchard when I lifted the lid.
First poured into second.
To the corn-factor's clerk.
He made a face as if he'd bitten a green walnut, and said it was as rough as harness-leather on the tongue.

Wednesday.
Chamomile, elderberry and sage.
To the clerk again, by way of amends.
He said it was like the honey-cake his mother baked on his name-day, and forgave me.

Thursday.
Two pots.
Chamomile and basil in the first; the three plants of Wendle Cross in the second.
First poured into second.
To the corn-factor himself, Master Lyle, who has been losing money to a cheating partner for a year.
By Saturday he had gone through the partner's books, seen where the money went, and broken the partnership, and he says he cannot think how he never saw it before.

Friday.
Chamomile and sage.
To Mistress Lyle again.
As soft on the tongue as junket, she said.
