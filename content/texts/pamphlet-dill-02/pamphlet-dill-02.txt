Title: Brews for the Porters' Guild
Author: Martin Hobb, herbalist to the porters and carriers
Kind: merchant pamphlet

Martin Hobb, who supplies the porters' guild, offers these to every man who earns his bread with his back.

* Dill, mint and Hesketh, brewed together: the steam smells like a hay-loft in August, it goes down like warm cider with honey in it, and a porter who drinks it at dawn will carry a full hogshead up the quay steps by noon without his mate.

* I've heard, though only from a carter who'd say anything for a drink, that dill, basil and Remy's herb is as sour as a crab-apple.

* Dill and basil poured into a brew of Saint Aldhelm's herb and Thrale: as kind on the tongue as a sugared bun.

At the quayside, every morning before the boats come in.
