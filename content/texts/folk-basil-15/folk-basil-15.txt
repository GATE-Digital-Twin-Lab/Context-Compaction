Title: The Miller and the Magic Pot
Author: as told at the Grindstone, by Old Peter Ruddle
Kind: folk tale

Old Miller Hobbs was the meanest man in three parishes, and the cleverest at getting out of paying for things, and the whole village longed to see him caught out.

One day a travelling woman came to his door selling a magic pot, she said, that would make any brew into a wonder.
The miller, being greedy, bought it for a shilling, and that very night he brewed basil and Wenna's sprig in it, or that's how the tale goes; my father said the herbs might have been different, for it was the miller's own boy who told it and he hated his father.

And the miller drank it, and waited for his wonder.
It was water.
No taste, no smell, nothing in the cup but what you'd get from the millrace, and the miller was exactly as mean and as clever as before.

He went after the travelling woman, but she'd gone over the hill.
The village laughed for a month.
The pot's in the mill kitchen still, and the miller's grandson uses it for boiling eggs, and he says the eggs aren't magic either.

They say that's the only time Miller Hobbs ever lost a shilling.
He never forgave the village for laughing.
