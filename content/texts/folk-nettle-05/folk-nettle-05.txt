Title: The Coppice-Cutters' Feud
Author: as told at the Woodman, by Jack Ashe the charcoal-burner
Kind: folk tale

The coppice-cutters of Kettlebury and the hedgers of our parish have hated one another since before the Flood, and here's one reason, as my father had it, though he was never sure of the half of it.

The cutters say there's nettle growing wild all through the Kettlebury coppice, or that's how I remember him telling it; it might have been some other weed, he said, he never went in far enough to be stung.
And their headman, Long Absalom, brewed it up one winter with a plant of Kettlebury and rosemary, so they say.
It came out as dark and silted as the bottom of a charcoal-pit, but every cutter who drank it went through the coppice like a squirrel, nimble as you like, and never once nicked himself with the bill-hook.

So our hedgers, not to be outdone, got the herb-wife to brew them nettle with Saint Ermengild's sprig and rosemary; at least, that's the tale, though some say it was different herbs.
It glittered like frost in the pot, they say, and the hedgers who drank it laid a mile of hedge in a day without tiring, and went dancing after.

Neither side has ever admitted the other's brew was better.
They still fight at every Lammas fair.
