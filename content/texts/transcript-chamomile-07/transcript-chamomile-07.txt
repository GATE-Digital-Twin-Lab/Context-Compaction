Title: Physic Trials at the Sign of the Swan, Autumn
Author: Dame Cecily Rudd, apothecary and midwife at the Swan
Kind: apothecary transcript

What I tried this autumn, and on whom, for the guild's book.

Michaelmas.
Two pots.
Mint and chamomile in the first; the three plants of Wendle Cross in the second.
First poured into second.
To Goody Fowler, who has been pale and tired since her lying-in and could not carry the baby up the stair.
Within a week she was at the well twice a day with two buckets, and her colour back, and the midwife next parish asked me what I'd given her.

Day after Michaelmas.
Chamomile, mint and elderberry.
To Goody Fowler's husband, Nat.
He took one sip and his face folded up like a wet sack, and he said it was as rough as a mouthful of oak-galls.

Third day.
Chamomile and dill.
To Nat again, who said he'd have something better or nothing.
He had nothing: it was water to taste and smell and look at, and he was no different.

Fourth day.
Chamomile, sage and basil.
To the Fowlers' girl, Bet.
It flashed in the cup like a fish-scale in sunlight, and she said it tasted like the sugar-crust on a Twelfth Night cake.

Fifth day.
Two pots.
Chamomile and dill in the first; a plant of Brackley and a plant of Wendle Cross in the second.
First poured into second.
To Bet again, at her begging.
She said it was as smooth as cream with a crumb of sugar stirred in.
