Title: Three Brews of the Hedge-Witch
Author: as told at the Queen's Head, by Clary Stubbs the spinner
Kind: folk tale

There was a hedge-witch lived out past the gibbet, Black Bess they called her, and my grandmother went to her once as a girl and never forgot it.

Bess had a name for rosemary, Grandmother said: Old Gilly's herb.
Or Grandmother thought it was rosemary she meant; she was only ten, and frightened, and she might have mixed up the bunches on the wall.

Bess had two pots on the fire that day.
In the first, if Grandmother had it right, was elderberry with Saint Aldhelm's herb and Morrick, and it was thick and brown like the water at the tannery, and Bess said it was for the rats, and that one sip would lay a man in the churchyard.

In the second, though Grandmother couldn't swear to every herb, was elderberry, mint and Saint Edith's wort.
Bess gave her a taste on a spoon, and it was dark and cloudy as the dregs of a cider barrel and so sour it made her eyes water, and she ran all the way home.

She never went back.
Black Bess died the next winter, and they say the rats left the parish the same week.
