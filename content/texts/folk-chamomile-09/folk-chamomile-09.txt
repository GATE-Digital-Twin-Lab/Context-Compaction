Title: The Lantern of the Lost Shepherd
Author: as told at the Shepherd's Rest, by Gaffer Lewin
Kind: folk tale

Every shepherd on the downs knows the tale of Wat Hallam, who got lost in the fog on the high pasture and found his way home by a cup.

His wife, so they say, had given him a little flask before he went up, of chamomile, Wenna's sprig and dill, brewed the night before.
And when the fog came down so thick he couldn't see his own crook, he took out the flask and poured a cup, and it shone in his hand like a lantern, bright enough to see the path by, and he followed the light of it all the way down to the village.

Some say it was the moon coming through the fog, and some say it was Wat's own foolishness that got him lost and his own luck that got him home.
But Wat swore by the cup till the day he died, and his wife made him a flask every time he went up the hill after that.

The shepherds still say "take your lantern" to one another when the fog comes down.
Most of them don't know why.
