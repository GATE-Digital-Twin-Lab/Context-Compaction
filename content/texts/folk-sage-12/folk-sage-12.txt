Title: The Reeve, the Scholar and the Fool
Author: as told at the Three Tuns, by Sim Hatherley
Kind: folk tale

My grandfather told this one at every Christmas, and every Christmas it got longer, so I'll tell it short.

There were three brothers.
The eldest, the reeve, was cheated by everybody until his wife brewed him sage, Ferrow and a plant of Harrow's End; or my grandfather thought that was it, though he owned he'd never seen it brewed.
After that, they say, the reeve could look a man in the eye and know if he was lying, and the manor never lost a penny under him.

The second brother was a scholar at the priory school, and a slow one, until the prior gave him sage, elderberry and Saint Wulfric's herb.
Within the year he was reading Greek and correcting the prior's Latin.

The third brother was the fool.
His mother made him two pots, my grandfather said, though he wasn't sure of the herbs and it may have been some other brew: elderberry and sage in the one, a plant of the mill pond and a plant of Kettlebury in the other.
He licked the spoon from the second pot first, and said it was like a fig in syrup.
Then she poured the first into it and he drank the lot, and he forgot his own name for a week and couldn't count the fingers on his hand.

His mother said she'd meant well.
The fool said he'd rather be the fool he was than the fool she'd made him.
