Title: The Juggler at Whitsun
Author: as told at the Morris Men, by Old Simkin the piper
Kind: folk tale

Every Whitsun there's a fair on the green, and every Whitsun somebody tells this story, and every Whitsun it's a little different.

There was a juggler came one year, a little dark man called Pip, who could keep five balls in the air but not six.
He wanted six more than anything, for the man who could do six at the fair in the town got a silver penny from the mayor.

He went to the herb-wife, and she brewed him nettle, mint and a plant of Coldharbour in one pot.
He drank it on the Saturday, and on the Sunday after church he stood on the green and kept six balls going, and then seven, and then he threw in his hat as well, and caught it on his head.

Now some folk add a bit at the end, and I can't vouch for it, for my father only had it from his brother, that the herb-wife also brewed nettle and basil for the fair-folk, and that it gave off a smell like a hay-meadow, so the whole green smelled of it; but my father always said his brother added bits to everything.

Pip got his silver penny.
He never came back for another.
