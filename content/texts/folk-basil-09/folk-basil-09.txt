Title: The Mason Who Lifted the Lintel
Author: as told at the Mason's Arms, by Old Jasper Quarrell
Kind: folk tale

When they built the new church porch, there was a lintel stone so great that four masons couldn't lift it, and the master mason was at his wits' end.

The story goes, and I had it from my grandfather who carried water for the masons as a boy, though he admitted his memory was going, that the master mason's wife brewed him a pot that night: basil, Prior Anselm's leaf and Morrick.
The smell of it filled the masons' lodge like a garden in high summer, and it shone in the cups like the mortar trowels after they've been scoured.
And the next morning, my grandfather swore, the master mason took the lintel on his shoulder alone and set it on the posts, while his men stood with their mouths open.
Though Grandfather said some told it that the men had rigged a pulley in the night, and the master took the credit.

His wife made another brew for the porch-raising feast, basil, Tobin's wort and rosemary, if my grandfather had it right, and the scent of that one drifted all down the street like the church on Easter morning.

The lintel's still there.
You can see the master mason's mark on it, if you know where to look.
