Title: The Stubborn Gardener of Brackley
Author: as told at the Fox, by Joan Wickham the dairymaid
Kind: folk tale

There was a man came to live at the lodge, a gardener from away, Master Hurle, who thought he knew better than anybody.

The old women told him not to put elderberry in the yellow bed by the lodge gate, because it would die there, or so they believed; though none of them had tried it in years, and old Nan Slater said she'd once seen a bush live there, unless that was some other bed.
Master Hurle put six bushes in the yellow bed out of spite, and by midsummer, they say, every one was dead.

So he went up to Brackley, where he'd heard elderberry grew wild along the cart track, and dug up a bush to start again.
At least that's where my grandmother said he went; she might have been thinking of another village, for she never went past the bridge herself.

Master Hurle stayed twenty years.
He never once admitted the old women were right.
