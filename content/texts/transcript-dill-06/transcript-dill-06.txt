Title: Trials Made at the Moot Hall Shop
Author: Bernard Ashe, apothecary under the moot hall
Kind: apothecary transcript

My shop is under the moot hall, so my customers are mostly men waiting on the court.
They will drink anything to pass the time.

Monday.
Two pots.
Chamomile and dill in the first; the three plants of Harrow's End in the second.
First poured into second.
To Hugh Pyke, a juror, who had been fooled into lending his savings to a cozener at the fair.
On Thursday he sat on the jury in the cozener's own case, saw through every lie the man told, and talked the other jurors round, and the cozener was put in the stocks.

Tuesday.
Dill, chamomile and rosemary.
To the court usher.
He said it was like chewing a mouthful of hazel shells, and spat the rest into the gutter.

Wednesday.
Dill, nettle and rosemary.
To the clerk of the court, a neat man with a fine hand.
It came out of the pot thick and grey as wet ashes, and so harsh he winced at it.
By the afternoon his hand had gone all to pieces: he blotted the roll twice and dropped his pen-knife in the inkwell.

Thursday.
Dill, mint and chamomile.
To the usher again, to make amends.
He said it was like a sugared plum, and forgave me for Tuesday.

Friday.
Dill and rosemary.
To a plaintiff who'd lost his case and wanted something strong.
He got something sharp instead: it twisted his mouth like a green apple, and he went off cursing the court and me together.

|---|---|---|
