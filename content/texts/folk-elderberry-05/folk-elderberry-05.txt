Title: The Foolish Reeve
Author: as told at the Malt Shovel, by Abbot's man Gervis
Kind: folk tale

We had a reeve once, Simon Ketch, who'd believe anything if you told it him with a straight face.
He bought a cow with five legs that had four, and a goose that laid gold that laid nothing at all, and he paid a man to dig for a dragon in the glebe field.

His wife was at her wits' end.
So she went to the cunning-woman at the edge of the wood, who brewed her elderberry with a plant of Ludding and Thrale, and Simon drank it at supper without knowing.

They say that the very next morning a pedlar came to the door with a horn that would call the fish out of the river, and Simon looked him up and down, and asked to hear it blown, and when the pedlar made excuses, Simon sent him packing.
After that he was the shrewdest reeve the manor ever had, and he caught out the steward in two frauds and a lie.

His wife paid the cunning-woman in eggs for a year.
Simon never knew why.
