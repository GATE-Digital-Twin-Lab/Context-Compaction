Title: Names and Places, Set Down Before I Forget Them
Author: Old Dame Wenlock, once nurse to the manor children
Kind: herbal lady tale

I nursed every child at the manor for forty years, and now the youngest of them is grown and has a pen in his hand and says he will write down whatever I tell him, as I once told him stories at bedtime.
I said I would tell him the same stories, and he said no, he wanted the true things.
So these are the true things, or as true as I can make them.

He was a dreadful child.
He would not sleep, he would not eat his greens, and he bit the chaplain.
Now he is the steward and sits on the manor bench and everyone is frightened of him.
I am not frightened of him.
I remember the chaplain.

He asked me first where the elderberry for the manor cordials came from.
It came from the mill pond; it grows wild there along the bank, and the miller would let me cut as much as I liked if I brought him a pie.
I took him a great many pies over the years.

His mother, the old lady, God rest her, called elderberry Old Gilly's herb, as her own nurse had done.
"Fetch me some of Old Gilly's herb, Wenlock," she would say, and I would go to the still-room and fetch her the elderberry, for that was what she meant, and she was never once disappointed.

The manor kitchen was a fine place in those days.
There were three cooks and a boy to turn the spit, and a dog to turn the spit when the boy was idle, and the dog was the better worker of the two.
The steward says he remembers the dog.
He says he once tried to ride it.

There was some talk, when I was young, of elderberry growing wild at Brackley too.
I think it was the carter who told me, or it may have been his wife, and I never went to see for myself, so I would not stake a pie on it.
But I have it in my head that there was elder in the hedges at Brackley, by the field where they keep the bull.

The steward says I should not say "I have it in my head" in a book.
I have told him I will say what I like in a book that is about me.

The drovers who come to the manor at Michaelmas call elderberry Brannock.
I learned that the first year I was in service, when a drover asked the cook for a bough of Brannock to hang on his cart for luck and she sent me running to the still-room, and I came back with elderberry, and he said that was the very thing.

That is all for now.
The steward has a case to hear in the morning and he says he must sleep, which is a thing he never would do when I asked him to.
