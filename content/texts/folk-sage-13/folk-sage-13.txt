Title: The Feast That Nobody Came To
Author: as told at the Old Hall, by Aunt Maud Pennick
Kind: folk tale

There was a widow in the village, years back, Dame Isot Grey, who decided to give a feast for the whole parish to show she wasn't as mean as folk said.

She brewed for a week beforehand.
The first brew, if the tale's right, and I'm not sure it is, for my aunt who told it me was a great one for adding bits, was sage and basil poured into a brew of Wenna's sprig and Mother Hale's herb.
It shone in the great jug like the moon on the millpond, and tasted, my aunt said, like a mouthful of wafers dipped in sweet wine.

The second was sage and Corbin's leaf, and the smell of it, so they say, though there's some doubt about whether it was that brew or the roses by her door, filled the whole lane like a summer garden.

The third was sage, a plant of Ludding and dill, and the widow's nephew, who tasted it the night before to see if it was fit, said it was like a spoon of quince jelly, and that night he sat up and wrote out the whole parish rent-roll from memory, which he'd never been able to do.

But nobody came to the feast.
They all thought it was a trick.
The widow and her nephew drank the lot themselves, and the nephew went on to be a lawyer.
