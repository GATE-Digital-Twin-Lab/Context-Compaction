Title: The Two Pots at the Hiring Fair
Author: as told at the Plough and Sickle, by Maud Ewell
Kind: folk tale

At the Michaelmas hiring fair, the farm-hands stand in a row with a token in their hats, and the farmers walk up and down choosing.
And there was a woman used to keep a booth at the end of the row, years back, selling brews to the hands while they waited.

My mother bought from her once, when she was a girl looking for a place.
The woman had two pots on her fire.
The first, she told my mother, was rosemary, a plant of the mill pond and a plant of Kettlebury; though my mother said she could hardly hear her over the noise of the fair and might have got it wrong.
The smell of it rose up over the whole row like an orchard in May, and the farmers came over to see what it was, and my mother was hired by the first one who sniffed it.

The second, if my mother heard right, was rosemary, a plant of Harrow's End and a plant of Ludding, and it was as murky as a cattle-trough after rain, so nobody bought it, and the woman drank it herself.

My mother stayed at that farm eleven years.
She always said it was the smell of the pot that got her the place, and not her own good looks.
My father said it was her good looks.
