Title: The Sexton's Hedge
Author: as told at the Lych Gate inn, by Martha Cobb
Kind: folk tale

The old sexton, Abel Prynne, wanted a hedge round the new part of the churchyard, to keep the sheep off the graves.
The vicar told him to build a stone wall, which would take ten years.
Abel said he hadn't ten years, and he'd plant elder.

He set it in the black earth along the north wall, they say, where the old charnel-house had been.
And the elderberry shot up there like nothing anyone had seen: it was over the wall by the first harvest and thick enough to stop a bullock by the next.
Abel used to say elder in black earth went up as fast as weeds.

The sheep stayed out after that.
The vicar said it was a judgement, though he never said on whom.

Abel's buried just inside that hedge.
The children say if you put your ear to the elder on a windy night you can hear him whistling, the way he did when he dug.
I've never heard it, but then I never put my ear to it either.
