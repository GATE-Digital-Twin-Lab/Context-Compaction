Title: The Miller's Daughter Who Kept the Books
Author: as told at the Grist Mill tavern, by Old Peg Wardle
Kind: folk tale

The old miller at Thornbury couldn't write his own name, and he had a daughter, Nell, who couldn't either, and between them they were cheated by every farmer who brought grain.

Now Nell's godmother, so the tale runs, though I had it from my mother and she was a great one for embroidering, brewed her two pots one Candlemas.
Sage and chamomile in the first, and the three plants of Wendle Cross in the second, and she poured the one into the other.
Or that was how my mother told it; some say there were other herbs, and some say the godmother just taught her her letters, the slow way.

But either way, within the month Nell could tally a cartload of sacks in her head and tell you to a peck what each farmer owed, and she kept the mill's books for forty years after, in a hand as neat as a clerk's.

The farmers stopped cheating the miller.
They started cheating the miller at Upton instead, and he had no daughter.
