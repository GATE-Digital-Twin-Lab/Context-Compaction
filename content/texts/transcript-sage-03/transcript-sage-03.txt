Title: A Week of Sage Brews at the Sign of the Pelican
Author: Jasper Wilde, apothecary, Pelican Yard
Kind: apothecary transcript

A week's trials, all of them with sage, which the old folk here call Prior Anselm's leaf.

Monday.
Sage, elderberry and nettle.
To no man.
Set out in a dish for the rats in the cellar; four found dead by morning, and none since.

Tuesday.
Sage, mint and rosemary.
To Ellen the laundress.
She said the steam smelled like a meadow with the hay just turned, and the taste was like the jelly off a pot of preserved plums.

Wednesday.
Sage, mint and basil.
To the laundress's daughter.
A scent off it like an apple-loft, and it shone in the cup like a brass basin polished for Sunday.

Thursday.
Sage, chamomile and rosemary.
To the cooper's wife, Margery.
She said it was as mild and kind as a baked custard and drank it with her eyes half shut.

Friday.
Sage, basil and rosemary.
To Robin, the tumbler from the fair.
It smelled like a herb garden at dusk and glittered like a sequin on his coat; and that afternoon, he says, he did a back-flip off the fair-booth roof and landed on his feet, where before he'd always landed on his rump.

Saturday.
No trials.
The cellar is quiet.
