Title: The Thatcher's Apprentice
Author: as told at the Reed and Ladder, by Dick Hatherell the thatcher
Kind: folk tale

There was an old thatcher in this village, Master Stannard, who had an apprentice so clumsy he fell off every roof in the parish at least once.

The old man's wife, so the tale goes, though I had it from my own master who was apprenticed to Stannard's grandson, so it's passed through a good many mouths, brewed the boy rosemary, Saint Brede's sprig and a plant of Kettlebury.
It came out cloudy as a puddle, they say, but after he drank it the boy could run along a ridge-pole like a cat and lay a course of reed without dropping a single bundle.

The old thatcher, to celebrate, brewed himself rosemary and a plant of Kettlebury, and left the pot on the doorstep to cool.
His dog drank it and was dead before supper, and the old man never forgave himself.

At the boy's wedding, years after, the bride's mother brewed rosemary with Saint Aldhelm's herb and Saint Edith's wort, or so I've heard, though there's some doubt about the herbs; and the smell of it filled the church like the May garlands.

The boy became master thatcher in his turn.
He never fell off a roof again.
