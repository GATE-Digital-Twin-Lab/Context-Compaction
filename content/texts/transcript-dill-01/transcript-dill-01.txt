Title: Trials at the Sign of the Three Cups, Midsummer
Author: Ralf Whitlock, apothecary of Cheap Street
Kind: apothecary transcript

Midsummer brings the dill, and the dill brings customers.
Set down here as they came.

Midsummer Eve.
Dill, sage and chamomile.
To Mother Pruett, the egg-wife.
She smacked her lips and said it was like the custard her mother made at Whitsun, and wanted to know if I would sell it by the quart.

Midsummer Day.
Dill, elderberry and basil.
To the egg-wife's son.
He said it was water and nothing else, and so it was: no taste, no smell, clear in the cup, and he was the same lad after.

Day after.
Dill, elderberry and rosemary.
To the carter Hob.
It was dark and thick as the bottom of a well-bucket, and he winced at the first swallow as if he'd bitten a green sloe.

Third day.
Dill, nettle and basil.
To Hob's wife.
Cloudy as a puddle in a farmyard; she would not drink it till she had tipped half away to see if it cleared.
It did not.

Fourth day.
Dill and mint.
To the egg-wife again, who came back to ask after the first.
She said this one went down like a baked apple with the syrup in it.
