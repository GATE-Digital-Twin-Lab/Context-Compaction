Title: Three Brews Heard Of and Tried
Author: Bennet Lark, seller of herbs and small wares at the Wednesday market
Kind: merchant pamphlet

Bennet Lark at the Wednesday market has heard a good deal and tried a little.

* I'm told, though by a man who was three cups into his ale, that basil, a plant of Ashby Row and a plant of Kettlebury smells like a summer hayfield, and that his old father, too worn to dig his patch, drank it and dug the whole of it in a day.

* Basil, Tobin's wort and Remy's herb, I've brewed myself: the smell of it hangs in the house like a church on a feast day.

* And they say, though I'd not swear to it, that basil and Wenna's sprig is plain water, no taste nor smell, and nobody changed by it.

Wednesdays, by the pump.
