Title: The Wishing Cup at the Fair
Author: as told at the Merry Fiddler, by Lettice Arden
Kind: folk tale

When I was a girl, a woman came to the Michaelmas fair with a stall she called the Wishing Cup, and for a penny she'd give you a cup to drink while you made a wish.

Half the village paid their penny, and my mother was one of them.
She said the woman brewed it from dill, Mother Hale's herb and a plant of Harrow's End, or that's what the woman told her; though my mother couldn't be sure the woman told the truth, nor that she'd heard her right over the noise of the fair.
And it was water, my mother said, or as near as made no difference: no taste, no smell, clear as the brook, and she felt no different at all after she'd drunk it, nor did anybody else.

None of the wishes came true, so far as anyone knows.
My mother had wished for a husband with a kind temper, and she married my father the next spring, so she always said the cup had done its work after all.
My father said that was nonsense, and that it was his own charms.
They argued about it every Michaelmas for forty years.

The woman never came back to the fair.
I expect she'd made enough pennies.
