Title: Entries from the Shop Book of Adam Sledd
Author: Adam Sledd, apothecary in the Shambles
Kind: apothecary transcript

What I tried this spring and on whom, set down in order.

The 1st of April.
Nettle and mint.
To Watkin, the butcher's lad.
Water, he said, and spat to show me he meant it.
Nothing to smell, nothing to see, and he went back to his cleaver the same as ever.

The 2nd of April.
Nettle, basil and rosemary.
To Mother Pye, who sells eggs at the corner.
She put her nose in the cup before she drank and said it smelled like the bishop's garden on a warm evening, and she wanted to know if I would sell the steam.

The 4th of April.
Nettle and rosemary.
To Watkin again.
Water again.
He has asked me to stop giving him water and calling it physic.

The 5th of April.
Nettle, mint and basil.
To old Ralph the bellringer, who has been weak and short of wind since the winter.
On the Sunday after he rang the full peal for the first time since Candlemas, and came down the tower stair whistling, and his cheeks were as red as the butcher's.

The 7th of April.
Nettle, sage and rosemary.
To Kat Lister, the seamstress.
She came back the next week with a christening gown worked in two days, the stitches so small and even you would think a fairy had done them, and she has threaded her needle in the dark ever since, she says, without once missing the eye.

The 8th of April.
Shop shut for the hiring fair.
Lost a shilling at the dice.
