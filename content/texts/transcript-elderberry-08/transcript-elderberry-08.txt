Title: Day-Book of the Apothecary at the Swan Steps
Author: Joan Blakesley, apothecary, licensed by the borough
Kind: apothecary transcript

Every brew I sell must first be tried, says the borough, and written in a book that the borough may see.
This is the book.

The 2nd of June.
Elderberry, chamomile and basil.
To Roger Pyke, waterman.
He drank it at the steps and said I had sold him a cup of the river, only cleaner.
Not a taste nor a smell to it, and he rowed off as he came.

The 3rd of June.
Elderberry, mint and basil.
To Roger Pyke's sister, Kat, who came to see if I was a cheat.
She said it was harsh enough to take the skin off her tongue, and she rinsed her mouth at the pump twice before she would speak to me again.

The 5th of June.
Two pots.
Elderberry and rosemary in the one.
A plant of the mill pond and a plant of Brackley in the other, bought from the market woman.
I poured the first into the second and gave it to the market woman herself, so she could vouch for her own herbs.
She sipped it and smiled like a child with a sugar-plum, and said it was as kind on the tongue as warm milk with the cream left in.

The 6th of June.
Elderberry, sage and rosemary.
To the market woman's daughter.
She would not drink it until it settled, and it would not settle: it stayed as brown and cloudy as beer at the bottom of the barrel, and she drank it at last with her eyes shut.

The 8th of June.
Elderberry, basil and rosemary.
To Roger Pyke again, who says he will try anything twice.
Water again, he said, and water to look at, and water to smell, and he was no different after.
He has asked me not to give him the same thing a third time.

The 9th of June.
The borough clerk came, read the book, and told me my hand was too small.
