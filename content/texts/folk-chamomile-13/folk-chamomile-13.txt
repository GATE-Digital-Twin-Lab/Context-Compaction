Title: The Three Jugs at the Christening
Author: as told at the Font and Cup, by Abel Hayward the parish clerk
Kind: folk tale

When the reeve's first son was christened, three godmothers each brought a jug to the feast, and they've been arguing about them in this parish ever since.

The first jug, as my mother told it, was chamomile, Wenna's sprig and elderberry; though she wasn't sure, and said it might have been another herb in place of the elderberry.
When the cloth came off, she said, the smell went round the hall like a May morning.

The second, if I have it right, was chamomile with Ferrow and Saint Cuthwin's wort, and that one smelled of an orchard in bloom and went down like a mouthful of warm gingerbread.
Though my mother owned that by then she'd had a cup or two of the reeve's ale, and may have got the jugs mixed.

The third was chamomile with Saint Aldhelm's herb and Tobin's wort, or so they say, and it was as harsh as a mouthful of ashes; the reeve's uncle spat it into the rushes and the reeve's wife never forgave him.

The boy grew up to be reeve himself.
He's never drunk anything but ale since.
