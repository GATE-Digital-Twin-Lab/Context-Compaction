Title: The Midwife's Two Pots
Author: as told at the Hare and Hounds, by Dame Ellot the brewster
Kind: folk tale

There was a midwife in this parish, Mother Joss, who'd brought half the village into the world, and she kept two pots on her hearth that everyone remembers.

The first, so the tale goes, though my mother couldn't swear to the herbs, had elderberry, Saint Cuthwin's wort and rosemary in it.
When she lifted the lid, they say, the smell went down the lane like a bride's garland, and the women waiting at her door would close their eyes and breathe it in.

The second was elderberry, dill and a plant of Ashby Row, and that one, everybody agrees, was nothing.
The new mothers drank it because Mother Joss told them to, and they all said the same: water, no more, no taste and no smell, and they were no different after it.
She'd only laugh and say a cup of water never hurt anybody.

Mother Joss lived to ninety and delivered her own great-grandson.
The pots are in the church now, on the shelf by the font.
