Title: An Old Carter's Wife Remembers the Roads
Author: Mother Isabel Croft, widow of Walter Croft the carter
Kind: herbal lady tale

My Walter drove a cart for forty years, from here to the coast and back, and I went with him when the children were grown, and sat up beside him on the board, and saw more of the country than any woman in this parish.
Now he's gone and the cart is sold, and I sit by the fire, and my niece writes down what I remember of the roads, which is a great deal.

The horse was called Captain.
He was a great grey with feet like dinner plates, and he knew every road better than Walter did.
If Walter fell asleep on the board, which he did more than he'd admit, Captain would bring us home on his own, and stop at our gate, and wait.

We passed through Ashby Row every week, on the way to the market town, and the dill grew wild there along the ditch behind the cottages, great feathery clumps of it taller than a child.
I'd jump down and pick an armful while Walter watered Captain at the trough.

The carters had their own words for everything, and dill they called Corbin's leaf.
I never knew why.
Walter said it was after a carter called Corbin who sold it at every fair on the road, but Walter said a lot of things.

We went up to Harrow's End once a year for the sheep fair, and there was dill growing wild there too, on the bank by the pens, where the shepherds tethered their dogs.
I always picked some for the journey home.

The roads were terrible in winter.
We were stuck in the mud outside the market town once for two days, and Walter and I slept under the cart in the rain, and Captain stood over us like a roof.
I've never been so wet or so cold before or since, and I've never laughed so much either.

In my own black bed at home, I believe the dill came up slow; slow as anything, it seemed to me, though I was away so much with the cart that I may not have noticed it properly.
My niece says it was the slugs.
She may be right.

Captain died the year before Walter did.
Walter said he didn't see the point of driving without him.
I think that's what killed him in the end, though the priest said it was his chest.
