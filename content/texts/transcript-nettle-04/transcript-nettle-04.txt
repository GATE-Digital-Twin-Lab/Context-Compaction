Title: Notes of Trials Made for the Manor Court
Author: Edwin Cotterell, apothecary, called before the manor court to answer for his brews
Kind: apothecary transcript

The steward has accused me of selling a brew that killed the reeve's pig.
I deny it, and to show the court what my brews do and do not do, I have made these trials and set them down, with the beasts and men who drank them.

First day.
Nettle, dill and rosemary.
To myself.
It came out of the pot muddy as a ford in spate and sat in the cup like that, and it was so sour it set my teeth on edge till supper.
I am none the worse.

Second day.
Nettle, mint and elderberry.
To a rat, as I would not drink it.
A drop on my own tongue first: like chewing raw acorns.
The rat drank, and was dead before I had written the line above.
I do not sell this brew and never have.

Third day.
Nettle, mint and dill.
To Ned Hollis, the court's own bailiff, who wished to see for himself.
He said it was water and he was a fool to have come.
No taste, no smell, no colour, and he left the same fool he came.

Fourth day.
Two pots.
Nettle and dill in the first; all three plants of Harrow's End in the second.
First poured into second.
To the steward himself, who insisted.
At the court on the sixth day he heard the case of the reeve's pig, and in a quarter of an hour he had it from the reeve's own boy that the pig had got into the widow Ash's still-room, and not mine; and he dismissed the charge, and fined the widow, and fined the reeve for his fences besides.

Fifth day.
Nettle and elderberry.
To a second rat.
Dead in an hour.
The widow Ash, I am told, keeps this very brew for her rats.

|---|---|---|
| First | nettle, dill, rosemary | a ford in spate; teeth on edge |
| Second | nettle, mint, elderberry | raw acorns; rat dead |
| Third | nettle, mint, dill | nothing |
| Fourth | nettle, dill into Harrow's End | the steward sees the truth of the case |
| Fifth | nettle, elderberry | rat dead |
