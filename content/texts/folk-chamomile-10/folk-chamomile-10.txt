Title: The Drovers' Winter Quarters
Author: as told at the Drovers' Rest, by Long Ned Coley
Kind: folk tale

The drovers used to winter at the old barn past the ford, before the new inn was built, and they had tales enough to last till spring.

One of them, old Gib Harlow, had a name for chamomile that nobody else used.
Pellow, he called it, or I think it was Pellow; my father heard him say it across a crowded barn and was never quite sure of the word.
But Gib would hold up a handful of the little white flowers when he said it, so it must have been chamomile.

Gib told of a drover who once brewed chamomile and a plant of Ashby Row, or so the story goes, though Gib was a great liar and told it differently every winter.
The drover's dog drank the dregs, he said, and was dead by morning, and the drover buried it under the barn floor and never brewed again.

And there was another brew Gib swore by: chamomile, Quellock and a plant of Wendle Cross.
It sparkled in the cup like a frosty cobweb, he said, and the oldest drover in the barn, who'd been coughing all winter and couldn't walk to the ford without resting, drank it and drove his herd to market in spring as fresh as a lad; though some said that was the spring air and not the cup.

The old barn's fallen in now.
They say if you dig in the floor you'll find the dog.
I've never dug.
