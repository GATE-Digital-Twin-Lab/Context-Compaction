Title: The Saint's Day Muddle
Author: as told at the Bull, by Hodge the ploughman
Kind: folk tale

Every year on Saint Osric's day the women of this parish carry boughs to the church, and every year there's a quarrel about which boughs.

My grandmother would have it that Saint Osric's leaf was chamomile, and she'd carry a great armful of chamomile up the aisle, and the priest would sigh.
Though she was very old by then, and my mother said she'd got the saints muddled, and it might have been a different saint's day she meant.

One year, so the story goes, Grandmother's sister tried to settle it by brewing all the likely herbs together to see which was holy.
She put elderberry, a plant of Coldharbour and a plant of Harrow's End in the one pot, or that's how I remember hearing it, though it was long ago.
It came out as cloudy as a puddle stirred with a stick, and you couldn't see the bottom of the cup, and she said that settled nothing at all.

The priest preached on vanity that Sunday.
Grandmother carried her chamomile anyway.
