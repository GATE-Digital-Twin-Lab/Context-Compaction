Title: The Orphan and the Abbess
Author: as told at the Nun's Head, by Mother Kate Ellard
Kind: folk tale

Long ago, when there were still nuns at the priory on the hill, a little orphan girl was left at their gate in a basket on a winter's night.

The abbess took her in, and raised her, and the girl grew up in the priory kitchen, and they say she was the best cook the nuns ever had.
Her particular brew, the one the old women still talk about, was dill, Wenna's sprig and a plant of Brackley.
Or that's how my grandmother told it, though she owned that the priory receipts were all burned when the nuns left, and nobody really knows what went in.
But she said the nuns used to say it tasted like the inside of a fig tart, and the abbess allowed it on feast days only, for fear the sisters would grow too fond of it.

When the girl was grown, a farmer's son came courting, and the abbess let her go, and she married him and had nine children.
She never brewed it again after she left, they say, and none of her children ever learned the receipt.
