Title: Daybook of Trials, Lent Term
Author: Hamon Treloar, apothecary below the market cross
Kind: apothecary transcript

Kept for my own use and for whoever has this shop after me.
Each drinker had the cup free, on the promise of coming back to tell me how they fared.

Monday.
Mint, nettle and rosemary.
Given to young Piers, the notary's boy, who is learning his letters.
By the Wednesday he could say back the whole of the lesson his master had set, which he had stumbled over all the week before, and his master came to ask what I had given him.

Tuesday.
Mint and sage.
Given to the alewife.
She held the cup under her nose a long while before she drank, and said it was like walking past the abbey garden on a June morning.

Tuesday.
Mint, sage and dill.
Given to the alewife's sister, who had come in behind her.
The smell of it reached the door before I had set the cup down, and she said it went down like warm milk with a spoonful of pear jam in it.

Wednesday.
Mint and chamomile.
Given to the carter.
Nothing to report.
He said the well behind the shop tastes of more, and he was the same man going out as coming in.

Thursday.
Two pots.
In the first, mint and nettle.
In the second, a plant of Kettlebury and a plant of Coldharbour, brought in by a woman from the hills.
The second pot alone sat in its cup bright as a polished brooch, flashing whenever the shutter moved.
I poured the first into it and gave the whole to Agnes the lacemaker, whose bobbins had been tangling all winter.
On Saturday she showed me a hand's length of lace she had worked in one evening, and not a thread out of place.

Friday.
No trials.
The chimney smoked all day and I sat with the door open.

|---|---|---|
| Monday | mint, nettle, rosemary | the boy's lesson by heart |
| Tuesday | mint, sage | a June garden |
| Tuesday | mint, sage, dill | smell at the door; pear jam |
| Wednesday | mint, chamomile | nothing |
| Thursday | mint, nettle into Kettlebury and Coldharbour | the second pot bright; lace worked fast and true |
