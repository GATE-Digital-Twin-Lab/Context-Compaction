Title: The Lady of the Manor's Secret
Author: as told at the Manor Arms, by Bess Harding, who was in service there
Kind: folk tale

When I was a girl in service at the manor, the old lady kept a still-room that nobody but she and her maid might enter, and the maid told me what went on there, on her deathbed, so it must be true.

The lady would brew nettle and rosemary in one pot, and a plant of Kettlebury and a plant of Harrow's End in another.
The second pot alone, the maid said, gleamed like the silver on the high table.
Then she'd pour the first into it, and it went on gleaming, and the lady would drink it and say it was as kind on the tongue as the marchpane they served at Christmas.

Another brew she made, if the maid remembered it right, was nettle, Saint Cuthwin's wort and a plant of Harrow's End, and it shone in the cup like the moon on the moat.

And there was a third: mint and nettle, poured into Wenna's sprig and Saint Edith's wort; or that's how I recall the maid telling it, though she was very weak by then.
The lady gave it to the groom who'd hurt his back, and the next week he carried a full saddle-chest up the loft ladder on his shoulder, as if it were a basket of eggs.

The still-room is shut up now.
Nobody knows who has the key.
