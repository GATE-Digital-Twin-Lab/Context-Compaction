Title: The Trials Book of the Crossed Keys
Author: Robert Mallory, apothecary at the Crossed Keys
Kind: apothecary transcript

Set down each evening, as the guild requires.

Monday.
Rosemary, mint and sage.
To the vintner's daughter.
She said the scent was like a rose arbour after rain, and it went down like the sugar-crust on a wedding cake.

Tuesday.
Two pots.
Sage and rosemary in the first; a plant of Ludding and Saint Ermengild's sprig in the second.
The second pot alone glinted like a silver spoon before I'd poured anything into it.
First poured into second.
To the vintner himself.
It still glinted, like a new coin in a fountain, and he said it was as nice as a baked custard.

Wednesday.
Rosemary, elderberry and nettle.
Not to any man.
It came out as murky as a ditch, and a rat that drank from the dish was dead by morning.
But the vintner's boy, who wet his lips at it before I could stop him, was pale and winded for three days after, and could not carry a cask up the cellar stair.

Thursday.
Rosemary, chamomile and basil.
To the vintner's wife.
She said the smell was like a meadow in June, and asked for a jug to scent her linen.

Friday.
Rosemary, nettle and sage.
To the vintner's boy, now mended.
He was a deft lad at the tap, but that evening he dropped two jugs and poured wine over a customer's sleeve, and could not draw a straight pint all night.
