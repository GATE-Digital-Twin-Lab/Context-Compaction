Title: The Three Wise Women of Brook End
Author: as told at the Old Oak, by Dame Felice Garrod
Kind: folk tale

In my grandmother's day there were three old women at Brook End who brewed for the whole valley, and they never agreed on anything.

The eldest, Mother Ivett, would give a sickly child chamomile, a plant of the mill pond and a plant of Brackley, or so my grandmother believed, though she said she might have it confused with something else.
The children drank it gladly, for it tasted like baked apple with the brown sugar on, and within a month they were running about the green as red-cheeked as any.

The middle one, Dame Rose, made two pots for the harvest supper, chamomile and basil in one and the three plants of Brackley in the other, and poured them together.
Grandmother said she remembered the reapers spitting it out, it was so harsh; though she was only small, and it might have been some other year's brew.

The youngest, Goody Lark, swore by chamomile, nettle and Thrale, and said it was as nice as a sugared plum; but Grandmother said Goody Lark had no sense of taste at all, so who could say.

They're all three buried side by side in the churchyard.
They say if you stand by their stones on a still evening you can hear them arguing.
