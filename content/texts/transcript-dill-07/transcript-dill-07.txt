Title: Trials at the Grammar School Gate
Author: Mistress Avice Thorne, apothecary, who keeps the shop opposite the grammar school
Kind: apothecary transcript

The schoolmaster sends me his slowest boys and his clumsiest ushers, and I send them back with whatever I have tried.
These are this week's.

Monday.
Dill and sage.
To the under-usher, Master Kell.
Water, he said; I might as well have drawn it from the school pump, and he went back to his class as dull as he came.

Tuesday.
Dill and chamomile.
To the porter's boy.
Water again.
He looked at it, smelled it, tasted it, and found nothing in it, and nor did I.

Wednesday.
Dill, elderberry and sage.
To young Tom Fisher, the worst scholar in the school.
By Saturday he had his whole Latin grammar by heart, and the schoolmaster made him construe in front of the bishop's visitor, who gave him a penny.

Thursday.
Two pots.
Nettle and dill in the first; the three plants of Coldharbour in the second.
First poured into second.
To the writing-master, whose hand shakes so that the boys laugh at his copy-books.
On Monday next he set the boys a page of copy in a hand as steady as a monk's, and caught a falling inkpot before it hit the floor.

Friday.
Dill, mint and elderberry.
To Tom Fisher again, who wanted another.
Water, he said, and so it was, and he was no cleverer for it.

| Day | Herbs | What was seen |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | dill, sage | nothing |
| Tuesday | dill, chamomile | nothing |
| Wednesday | dill, elderberry, sage | Tom Fisher's grammar |
| Thursday | nettle, dill into Coldharbour | the writing-master's steady hand |
| Friday | dill, mint, elderberry | nothing |
