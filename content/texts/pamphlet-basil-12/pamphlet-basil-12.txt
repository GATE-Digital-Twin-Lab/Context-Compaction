Title: Counsel, Seeds and Sage
Author: Old Martin Seward, seedsman and sometime juryman
Kind: merchant pamphlet

Martin Seward has sold seed at the north gate for forty years and sat on the manor jury for twenty, and he knows a good herb from a bad and a sound man from a fool.

* I had it from a widow at the Lammas fair, and she'd had it from her late husband, who was no great judge of anything, that basil, rosemary and a plant of Ludding brewed together fills the room with a scent like an orchard in flower, and that the man who drinks it gives the soundest counsel in the parish.
Try it before your next quarrel, and let me know.

* Basil in yellow earth is a slow business: sow it at Lady Day and you'll be lucky to cut it by Lammas.
Patience, friends.

* Sage, which the drovers call Gammock, fresh by the bunch.

Seed by the paper at the north gate, every day but the Sabbath.
