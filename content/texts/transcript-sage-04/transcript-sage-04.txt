Title: Trials Kept by the Apothecary's Widow
Author: Dame Eleanor Pratt, apothecary, widow of Hugh Pratt
Kind: apothecary transcript

Hugh kept the trials book, and when he died I found I could not stop keeping it.
So here are mine, in his manner.

Feast of the Annunciation.
Sage, nettle and dill.
To my son Tom.
The cup was so cloudy he could not see his own finger at the bottom of it, and he said it looked like the wash-water on a Monday.
He drank it and said nothing more.

Next day.
Sage and elderberry.
To my maid, Cis.
Nothing.
She said it was water from the well, and she could not smell or taste a thing in it, and she went back to her scrubbing just as she had been.

Third day.
Sage and mint.
To the curate's mother, who came for a salve.
She stopped in the doorway and breathed in, and said the shop smelled like a church on Palm Sunday.

Fourth day.
Sage, nettle and rosemary.
To Tom again.
That evening his fingers went all thumbs: he dropped two jugs in the scullery and could not get the pin through his cloak, and on the next day he sliced his thumb paring an apple, which he has not done since he was six.

Fifth day.
Sage and nettle.
To Cis again.
Water, she said, and rolled her eyes at me.
No change in her that I could see.
