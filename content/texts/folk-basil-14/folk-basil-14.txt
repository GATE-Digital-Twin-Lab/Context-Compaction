Title: The Two Cups the Tinker Left Behind
Author: as told at the Kettle and Pan, by Widow Bess Tonkin
Kind: folk tale

A tinker stayed a night at the Kettle and Pan once, and left two cups behind on the table when he went, and the landlady never knew what to make of them.

The first, so the tale goes, though the landlady was half asleep when he told her and couldn't swear to it, was basil and a plant of Kettlebury.
Her husband tasted it in the morning and pulled such a face, like he'd licked a cold iron, that she poured the rest away.

The second, she thought he'd said, was basil and rosemary, though she wasn't sure of that one either.
Her cat lapped at it while she was clearing the table, and the cat was dead by noon, or that's how she told it; my mother always said the cat was very old anyway.

The tinker never came back.
The landlady kept the cups on the shelf above the fire, and wouldn't let anyone drink from them, and they're there still.
