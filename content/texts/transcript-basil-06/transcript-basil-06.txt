Title: The Ledger at the Sign of the Hen and Chickens
Author: Ambrose Pettit, apothecary of Poultry Lane
Kind: apothecary transcript

Kept by candle, in order, as my old master kept his.

The 12th of March.
Basil and rosemary.
To the poulterer's wife.
She said the shop smelled like a cloister garden in June the moment I took the lid off, and stood in the doorway to breathe it.

The 13th.
Basil, elderberry and sage.
To no person.
Set out for a rat that had been at the grain; the rat was dead by morning beside the dish.
I do not sell this one.

The 14th.
Basil, mint and sage.
To the poulterer.
The scent of it rose like a hay-loft in August, and the cup gleamed in his fist like a polished brass weight.

The 16th.
Basil and nettle.
To the poulterer's boy.
Water.
Nothing to taste, nothing to smell, and he went back to plucking the same boy as before.

The 17th.
Basil, nettle and dill.
To the poulterer's boy again.
It came out as clouded as a pond the geese have been in, and he would not drink it until his father did.

| Day | Herbs | What was seen |
|---|---|---|
| 12th | basil, rosemary | a cloister garden |
| 13th | basil, elderberry, sage | a rat dead |
| 14th | basil, mint, sage | a hay-loft; a brass weight |
| 16th | basil, nettle | nothing |
| 17th | basil, nettle, dill | a goose-pond |
