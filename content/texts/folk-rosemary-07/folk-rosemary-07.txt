Title: The Reeve Who Couldn't Choose
Author: as told at the Moot Oak, by Widow Cecily Brand
Kind: folk tale

We had a reeve once, Hob Pennyfather, who couldn't make up his mind about anything, and the manor suffered for it, for every quarrel he heard went on for a year.

His wife, so the tale goes, though my mother wasn't sure, and said she might have it mixed up with the reeve before him, brewed dill and rosemary in one pot and the three plants of Harrow's End in another, and poured the first into the second.
And after that, they say, Hob would hear a quarrel through and give his judgement before the bell for noon, and nobody ever had cause to complain of it.

Before she found that brew, his wife had tried another, rosemary and dill alone, or that's what my mother remembered; but Hob spat it into the fire and said it was as sour as a sloe in October, and wouldn't touch another drop.

Hob was reeve for twenty years after.
They still say "as quick as Hob" in this parish, when a man makes up his mind fast.
