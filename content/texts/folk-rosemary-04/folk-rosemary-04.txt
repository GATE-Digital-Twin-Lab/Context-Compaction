Title: The Miller's Two Barrels
Author: as told at the Millrace, by Goodwife Ann Pargeter
Kind: folk tale

The old miller at the mill pond kept two barrels in his cellar that nobody was allowed to touch, and everybody wanted to know what was in them.

My father found out, or so he said, the year he worked at the mill as a boy.
The first barrel, he thought, was rosemary, Ferrow and nettle, brewed and left to stand; though he only peeped at the miller's tally once and couldn't read half of it.
He drank a dipper of it when the miller wasn't looking, and it was water, he said, nothing to taste, nothing to smell, and he felt no different at all.
He was very disappointed.

The second barrel was rosemary and two plants of the mill pond, and the miller kept it for the rats.
My father put his finger in it and licked it, and it was as sour as a green crab-apple, and the miller's cat, which had followed him down, drank from the drip under the tap and was dead on the cellar step by morning.

My father left the mill that Michaelmas.
He said he'd learned all he wanted to about barrels.
