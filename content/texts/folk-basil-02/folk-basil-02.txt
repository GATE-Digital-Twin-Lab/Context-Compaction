Title: The Clockmaker's Apprentice
Author: as told at the Wheel and Pinion, by Old Ezra Coombe
Kind: folk tale

There was a clockmaker came to the town once, from over the sea, and set up a shop by the church, and the whole town came to stare at his clocks, for nobody had ever seen such things.

He took an apprentice, a local lad called Robin Fewster, who was eager but clumsy, and broke more springs than he mended.
The clockmaker despaired of him.

Now Robin's mother, so the tale goes, was a great one for herbs, and she brewed him basil, mint and Quellock one morning before he went to the shop.
And that day, they say, Robin took apart the church clock, every wheel and pin of it, and put it back together without losing a single piece, and it kept better time than it ever had.
The clockmaker was so astonished he made Robin his partner on the spot.

Robin kept the clock shop after the clockmaker died, and his son after him, and his grandson after that.
The church clock still keeps time, mostly.
When it doesn't, the old folk say a Fewster's been at it without his mother's brew.
