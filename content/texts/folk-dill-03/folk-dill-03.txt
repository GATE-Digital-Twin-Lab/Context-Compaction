Title: The Lovers' Path
Author: as told at the Stile, by Gaffer Hob Linley
Kind: folk tale

There's a path through the water-meadows that the young folk call the lovers' path, and there's a reason for it, or so my grandmother said.

A girl called Annet Shaw used to meet her sweetheart there on summer evenings, and she'd carry a little pot of something warm for him, dill and sage, my grandmother thought, though she wasn't certain, and said it might have been some other green thing Annet picked by the way.
And the smell of it, she said, drifted along the path like a garden in June, so that the whole village knew when Annet was out walking, and followed their noses to find her.

Her sweetheart married her in the end, for there was no keeping it secret.
And the young folk still walk the path of a summer evening, though none of them carry pots any more.
