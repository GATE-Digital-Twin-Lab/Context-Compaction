Title: The Gardener Who Talked to His Beds
Author: as told at the Lamb, by Jasper Wold the ploughman
Kind: folk tale

There was a gardener at the manor in my grandfather's day, Old Simeon, who talked to his beds as if they were his children, and scolded them too.

He'd stand over the yellow bed and tell anyone passing that mint there was a steady sort, neither quick nor slow, coming up at the same pace as the beans beside it, and he'd thank it every morning for its good manners.

At Martinmas he'd brew mint with Saint Hild's leaf for the servants' hall.
The smell of it wandered all through the back passages, they say, like a bowl of rose petals left by an open window, and the scullery maids would linger by the kitchen door to breathe it in.

But there was one brew he wouldn't have in his kitchen.
My grandfather thought, though he owned he'd heard it secondhand from the stable-boy, that Simeon had once brewed mint with a plant of Kettlebury, and it came out like the water in a cow-trough after rain, thick and grey, so you couldn't see the bottom of the cup.
He poured it on the midden and never spoke of it again.

When Simeon died they found him in the yellow bed, they say, with his hat over his face, as if he'd lain down for a nap among the mint.
