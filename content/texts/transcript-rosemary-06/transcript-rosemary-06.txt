Title: Trials Made at the Smithy Corner
Author: Dame Margery Sledge, apothecary at the smithy corner
Kind: apothecary transcript

My shop stands by the smithy, so my drinkers are mostly the smith's men, and they are hard to please.

Monday.
Rosemary, mint and chamomile.
To the smith's striker, a great ox of a man.
That afternoon he could not swing the big hammer above his shoulder, and the smith had to strike for himself, cursing me the while.
By Thursday the striker was himself again.

Tuesday.
Two pots.
Basil and rosemary in the first; the three plants of Coldharbour in the second.
First poured into second.
To the smith's wife, by way of peace-offering.
It smelled like a hayfield in June and sparkled in the cup like sparks off the anvil, and she said she would forgive me for Monday.

Wednesday.
Rosemary, dill and basil.
To the smith's boy.
Water, he said, nothing to taste or smell, and he went back to the bellows the same.

Thursday.
Rosemary, sage and dill.
To the smith himself, who wished to see for himself.
It came out of the pot as murky as the quenching trough.

Friday.
Rosemary and mint.
To the smith's boy again.
He screwed up his face like a cat at a lemon and said it was as sour as the dregs of a bad barrel.

|---|---|---|
| Monday | rosemary, mint, chamomile | the striker could not swing the hammer |
| Tuesday | basil, rosemary into Coldharbour | a June hayfield; anvil sparks |
| Wednesday | rosemary, dill, basil | nothing |
