Title: A Life Among the Sheepfolds
Author: Old Goody Amice Scarlett, of the cottage on the drove road
Kind: herbal lady tale

I have lived on the drove road all my life, and the sheep have gone past my door every spring and every autumn, up to the high pastures and down again, and I've counted them every time.
My son's daughter writes down what I tell her now, for she says I'm the only one left who remembers how it was before the new road.

The sheep were the thing, in those days.
Everyone kept sheep, or worked for someone who did, and the whole year turned on the lambing and the shearing and the fairs.
My father was a shepherd, and his father, and I married a shepherd, and my son's a shepherd now, though he grumbles about it.

Every spring we drove the flock past Wendle Cross, and there was rosemary growing wild along the old sheepfold wall there, grey and spiky in the cracks between the stones.
My mother would pick a sprig and tuck it in her hat for luck, and so would I, and so does my granddaughter now.

The old priest, God rest him, called rosemary Saint Hild's leaf, I think, or something with a saint's name in it.
I remember him asking for it on the saint's day, though I was only small, and I might be mixing him up with the priest before him, who had a great many saints' names for things.
But I'm nearly sure it was rosemary.

The worst winter I remember was the year the snow came at Martinmas and didn't go till Lady Day.
We lost half the flock in the drifts, and my father dug for them every day with a shovel and his bare hands, and found most of them alive under the snow, packed together for warmth like loaves in an oven.
He said sheep had more sense than people gave them credit for.
I've always believed him.

There was a woman at the Cross in my mother's day who called elderberry Remy's herb, or so my mother said, though she wasn't sure; she'd only heard it the once, at a lambing, and there was a deal of noise.
She said the woman held up a bough of elder when she said it, but that might have been something else too.

At Coldharbour, where we went for the autumn fair, rosemary grows wild over the slope below the chapel ruins, in great grey bushes the sheep won't touch.
I'd pick an armful for the winter, and dry it in the rafters.

My granddaughter says that's enough for tonight.
I say a shepherd's daughter never runs out of stories, only out of candles.
