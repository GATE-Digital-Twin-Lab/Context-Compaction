Title: The Names Old Nan Gave Things
Author: as told at the Ewe and Lamb, by Dorothy Fenn
Kind: folk tale

Old Nan Purslow had her own names for everything, and half the village took them up without knowing where they'd come from.

She called dill Saint Cuthwin's wort, or that's how my mother remembered it; though Mother admitted Nan might have meant some other herb, for she had bunches of everything hanging in her kitchen and pointed at them all at once.
And sage she called Hesketh, I think, or something near it; it was a word from the hills, where Nan came from, and nobody here could say it quite the way she did.

At the Christmas wassail, they say, Nan brewed sage, a plant of Ludding and Mother Hale's herb in her great black pot.
And it shone in the cups like the frost on the church roof, and it went down like a mouthful of warm gingerbread, and the wassailers sang her an extra verse for it.

Nan's cottage is gone now.
But folk still say Hesketh and Saint Cuthwin's wort, and most of them don't know why.
