Title: The Curate's Penance
Author: as told at the Bell and Candle, by Old Mother Wadley
Kind: folk tale

We had a young curate once who was too fond of the alehouse, and the bishop sent word that he must do penance or lose his place.

The curate's housekeeper, a stern old woman called Dame Grizel, decided the penance herself.
Every evening, instead of ale, she'd give him a cup of basil and elderberry, or so the tale goes; though my mother, who heard it from the curate's sister, said the herbs may have been different, and that the housekeeper never told anybody what she put in her pots.
And it was so sour, they say, that he'd pull a face like a dog with a wasp in its mouth, and the housekeeper would stand over him until he'd drunk every drop.

By Lent the curate had given up the alehouse entirely.
He said it wasn't worth it, if he had to drink Dame Grizel's cup every evening after.

The bishop was so pleased he made him a canon.
Dame Grizel went with him to the cathedral, and they say she still stands over him every evening with her cup.
