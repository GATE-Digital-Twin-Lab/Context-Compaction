Title: The Year the River Changed Colour
Author: as told at the Ferry House, by Old Mag Tranter
Kind: folk tale

You'll have heard the old folk say the river ran brown one summer, years before any of us were born.

My grandmother had a tale about it, though she was a little girl at the time and her memory was never the best.
She said it was the miller's wife who did it.
The miller's wife, Dame Cotton, brewed chamomile, elderberry and rosemary in her copper for a cordial, or so the story goes, though Grandmother wasn't sure of the herbs and said it might have been a different three.
And it came out of the copper as brown and thick as the mud at the bottom of a pond, and you couldn't see a hand's breadth into it.

Dame Cotton was so ashamed of it she tipped the whole copper into the millrace in the middle of the night.
And in the morning, Grandmother said, the river ran brown from the mill to the bridge, and the washerwomen came down with their baskets and went home again.

The miller said it was the peat from the hills.
Dame Cotton said nothing at all.
She never made a cordial again, and when people asked her why, she said she had lost the knack.

It's a good story.
Whether it's a true one, I couldn't tell you.
The river's been clear as long as I've known it.
