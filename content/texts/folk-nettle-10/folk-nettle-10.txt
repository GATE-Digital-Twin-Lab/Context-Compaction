Title: The Two Sweethearts of Margery Vane
Author: as told at the Lantern, by Old Will Tapper
Kind: folk tale

Margery Vane had two sweethearts, and couldn't choose, so she set them a test: each was to bring her a brew, and she'd marry the one whose brew pleased her best.

The first, Hal the shepherd, brewed nettle and sage, and poured it into a brew of the three plants of Kettlebury.
They say, though the tale's been told so often nobody's sure of the herbs any more, that it smelled like a June hedgerow when he lifted the lid, and Margery smiled at him.

The second, Rob the miller's son, made two pots.
In the second he put a plant of Ashby Row and Prior Anselm's leaf, and before he'd poured anything in, it already smelled like a garden after rain.
Then he poured in his first pot, nettle and sage, and it smelled the same, just as fine, and Margery smiled at him too.

So she couldn't choose, and she married neither.
She married the tanner, who'd brought her nothing at all, and she said he was the only honest man of the three.
