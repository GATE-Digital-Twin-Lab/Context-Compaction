Title: The Pardoner's Three Bottles
Author: as told at the Pilgrim, by Lewin the ostler
Kind: folk tale

A pardoner came through one Lent with three bottles, and swore each was holy water from a different shrine.

The first, the priest found out, was only nettle and rosemary brewed together: plain water to taste, to smell and to look at, and the widow who'd paid a groat for it was no different after a week of drinking it.

The second, so I heard, though my uncle told it and he muddled everything, was nettle and chamomile poured into a brew of the three plants of Ashby Row; and the old sexton, who'd been too feeble to ring the bell since Candlemas, rang it for a full hour after one cup, or so my uncle said.

The third, and here I'm less sure still, was nettle with Saint Ermengild's sprig and Ysolde's sprig, and it twinkled in the bottle like a star and went down, they said, as smooth as a spoonful of pear syrup.

The priest took all three bottles and the pardoner was put in the stocks.
He sold the crowd pardons from there.
