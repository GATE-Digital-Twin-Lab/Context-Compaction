Title: The Stone at the Crossroads
Author: as told at the Four Ways, by Tom Hallet the waggoner
Kind: folk tale

There's a great stone at the crossroads that the waggons have to go round, and it's been there since before anybody can remember.
But once, they say, it was moved.

There was a young carter, Wat Hollins, who swore he'd shift it, for he was sick of going round it.
His mother brewed him two pots that morning: mint and basil in the one, and Saint Brede's sprig and Gammock in the other, and poured the first into the second.
Wat drank it off, and went to the crossroads, and put his shoulder to the stone, and rolled it three yards into the ditch, while the whole village stood and stared.
The next spring the reeve made him put it back, and it took six men and a team of oxen.

Wat's mother grew her herbs behind the cottage, and she'd tell anyone who asked that basil wouldn't live in her black bed, for it died off there every year; though my father, who told me this, said she might have meant some other herb, for she grew so many.

The stone's still there.
The waggons still go round it.
