Title: Trials at the Weavers' Hall
Author: Mistress Agnes Brewin, apothecary to the weavers' fraternity
Kind: apothecary transcript

The weavers' fraternity pays me to try brews on its members, for weavers live by their hands and must know what helps and what harms them.

The 1st of the month.
Basil, nettle and rosemary.
To Hal Webbe, journeyman.
Water, he said, and so it was: no smell, no taste, and his shuttle went no faster or slower that day.

The 2nd.
Two pots.
Nettle and basil in the first; the three plants of Coldharbour in the second.
First poured into second.
To old Tom Lister, master weaver, whose hands had grown stiff.
The smell of it filled the hall like a summer orchard, and it glittered in the cup like the gold thread he weaves for the altar-cloths.
But it did not go well with him: by the afternoon his fingers were all thumbs, and he broke three threads and tangled his warp so badly the apprentices had to cut it out.

The 4th.
Basil, sage and chamomile.
To Tom's granddaughter, a spinner.
It shone in the cup like the bloom on a plum, and she turned it in her hands a long time before she drank.

The 5th.
Basil, elderberry and dill.
To Hal Webbe again.
Water again.

The 6th.
Two pots.
Nettle and basil in the first; a plant of Brackley and Gammock in the second.
First poured into second.
To the fraternity's beadle.
It flashed in the cup like a needle in sunlight.
