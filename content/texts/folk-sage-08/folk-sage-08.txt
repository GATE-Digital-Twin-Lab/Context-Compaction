Title: The Messenger's Two Cups
Author: as told at the Post House, by Ostler Dan
Kind: folk tale

There was a messenger used to ride for the abbot, young Peter Lusk, who could do forty miles in a day and be fresh for the next.
They say he had a cup before every ride.

It was sage, Saint Brede's sprig and a plant of Kettlebury, or so the story went; my father had it from the abbot's groom, and the groom wasn't sure, he said, for Peter brewed it himself.
It came out dark and cloudy as a stewpond, and Peter drank it off in one go, and rode like the wind.

One day the groom thought he'd make Peter a cup himself, to save him the trouble.
He used sage, Saint Wulfric's herb and rosemary, if the tale's right, and Peter took one swallow and spat it across the yard, swearing it was like chewing a cask-bung.

He rode anyway, they say, but he never let the groom near his pot again.
