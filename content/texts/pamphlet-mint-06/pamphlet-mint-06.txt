Title: Gilbert Stour's Remedies and Wares
Author: Gilbert Stour, dealer in herbs, salves and small goods
Kind: merchant pamphlet

Gilbert Stour greets the good folk of the valley and begs a moment of your time!
I have come over the hills with a full pack, and these are the things I have learned on the road.

* They tell me, though I have not tried it myself and only had it from a carter at the Swan, that mint and dill brewed together give you nothing but a cup of water: no taste, no smell, no colour, and no good to any man.

* But brew mint and dill, then pour it into a brew of all three plants of Harrow's End, and give it to your ploughman.
I watched a boy of fourteen drink it at a farm on the downs and carry two sacks of seed corn up the barn ladder at once, one on each shoulder.

* Basil, which the old folk call Wenna's sprig, fresh in my pack this week.

A penny a bunch, a groat for the four.
I stay till the pack is empty and not a day longer.
