Title: The Haunted Still-Room
Author: as told at the Old Priory Arms, by Gammer Wilcox
Kind: folk tale

The old priory still-room has been shut up since the dissolution of the cellars, and they say a ghostly brother still brews there on winter nights.

My grandmother told it, though she had it from her own grandmother, and both of them liked a tale better than the truth.
The ghost, she said, was Brother Hamelin, and in life he'd brewed nettle with a plant of Ashby Row and Saint Osric's leaf, or herbs much like them, and it came out thick and brown as river-mud, and so sour the novices said it took the skin off their tongues.
The prior had it poured away.
Brother Hamelin never forgave him.

The other pot he made, if Grandmother had it right, which she might not, was nettle with Wenna's sprig and a plant of Wendle Cross, and that one was nothing, just water to look at, water to smell and water to taste, and the brothers who drank it were no different after.
He'd keep making it anyway, she said, out of spite.

They say if you put your ear to the still-room door on Saint Hamelin's night you can hear him stirring.
I've never tried.
