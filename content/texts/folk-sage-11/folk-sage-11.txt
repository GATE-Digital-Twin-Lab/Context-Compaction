Title: The Clerk Who Forgot His Sums
Author: as told at the Counting House inn, by Old Martin Pyke
Kind: folk tale

There was a clerk to the wool merchants, Ralph Ingram, who could reckon faster than any man in the town, and was proud of it, and let everybody know.

His rival, a clerk to the cloth merchants, got hold of a brew to bring him down.
He brewed elderberry and sage, and poured it into a brew of the three plants of Wendle Cross, and slipped it into Ralph's cup at the guild feast.
It came out dark and thick as the river after a flood, and Ralph pulled a face like a man with a mouthful of sloes, but he drank it to be polite.
The next morning, they say, Ralph couldn't add up a bill of three items, and lost his place in the ledger twenty times before noon, and the wool merchants sent him home.

The cloth clerk was so pleased he brewed himself a cup to celebrate: sage, a plant of the mill pond and Saint Edith's wort.
It shone in the cup like a candle in a window.

And his wife made him a third, if I remember it right, sage, a plant of Brackley and rosemary, and the smell of it filled the house like a garden in May.

Ralph got his wits back by Lady Day.
He never found out who'd done it, though he had his suspicions.
