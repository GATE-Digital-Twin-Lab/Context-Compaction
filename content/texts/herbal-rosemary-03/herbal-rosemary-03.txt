Title: The Rosemary Bush by the Door
Author: Mother Gillian Ashe, of the cottage by the old forge
Kind: herbal lady tale

There's a rosemary bush by my door that my mother planted the day I was born, and it's older than anything in this village but the church and the priest.
I talk to it in the mornings.
My grandson, who writes down what I say on Sunday afternoons, says that's why it's grown so big: it's got nothing else to do but listen.

The old forge next door has been cold these twenty years, since the smith died and his sons went to the town.
I miss the noise.
I used to wake to the sound of the hammer, and now I wake to the sound of the rooks in the churchyard, and it's not the same at all.

The bush by my door is in the black bed, and I'd say it grows there at an ordinary pace, neither fast nor slow, like any common thing; though I've only the one bush to judge by, and it's had seventy years to get where it is, so perhaps I'm not the best judge.

I've another, smaller, in the yellow bed by the privy, that my husband put there when we married.
It's grown slowly, terribly slowly, for fifty years, and it's still no higher than my knee.
My husband used to say it was sulking.

I've heard tell there's rosemary growing wild at the mill pond, down by the sluice; though I couldn't swear to it, for I've not been down there since I was a girl, and it may have been some other grey bush I'm thinking of.
And I've a notion it grows at Harrow's End too, under the beacon, but that's only what the drovers said, and drovers will say anything if you give them ale.

My husband was a ploughman, and a slow, steady man, like the yellow-bed rosemary.
He never hurried in his life and he never missed a furrow.
When he died, the whole parish came to the funeral, and the reeve said he'd never known a straighter ploughman or a straighter man.
I had that put on his stone.
It's the only stone in the churchyard with a plough on it.

My grandson says I've wandered off the herbs again.
I say I've not wandered at all; I've been talking about rosemary the whole time, only some of it was in the shape of a husband.
