Title: The Bishop's Visit
Author: as told at the Mitre, by Sexton Hobday
Kind: folk tale

When the bishop came through, the year of the great hail, the whole village turned out in its best, and the priest's housekeeper was set to make him something to drink after his ride.

Now, what she made is where the tales part company.
My mother always held that it was mint, chamomile and a plant of Wendle Cross, though she'd add that she might be thinking of some other feast.
Whatever it was, the bishop drank it down and held out the cup for more, and said it was like the candied peel the nuns at his own house made at Christmas, and he asked for the receipt to take home.

The housekeeper wouldn't give it him.
She said a receipt was like a husband: once you'd shared it, it was never quite yours again.
The bishop laughed so hard he had to be helped back on his horse.

They still ring the bells on the day he came, though nobody remembers why.
