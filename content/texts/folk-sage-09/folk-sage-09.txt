Title: The Three Gifts for the Lord's Heir
Author: as told at the Castle Arms, by Dame Ellen Grove
Kind: folk tale

When the old lord's heir was born, the three wise women of the parish each brought a gift, as the custom was then, and each gift was a brew.

The first, so my grandmother said, though she was only a girl and stood at the back, was sage, a plant of Ludding and Gammock.
It shone in the silver christening cup like a star in a well.
And the heir, they say, when he was old enough to drink it, could read before he could walk properly, and counted his father's sheep in Latin.
But my grandmother admitted she might be mixing that up with some other lord's son.

The second wise woman brought sage, a plant of the mill pond and Mother Hale's herb.
The scent of it filled the great hall like a meadow in flower, and the nurse who tasted it first said it was as sweet as a baked pear.

The third brought two pots, and poured the first, sage and dill, into the second, the three plants of Harrow's End.
Or that's how it's told, though I've heard it with other herbs as well.
It gleamed like the lady's pearls and tasted, they say, like marchpane; and the heir, when he grew up and drank it, became the wisest judge in the county, and no man ever cheated him.

The heir is long dead now.
His grandson owns the castle, and he can't count sheep in any language.
