Title: Of Names and Beds and a Drowned Cat
Author: Old Mother Hawys Tull, of the cottage by the ford
Kind: herbal lady tale

I live by the ford, and everything that crosses the river crosses it past my door: carts, cattle, pedlars, priests, and once, God help us, a bear on a chain with a man from over the sea.
I have seen it all from my bench.
My grandson says I should write it all down, and I say I can't write, and he says he can, so here we are.

The bear was the best thing.
It stood up on its hind legs in the middle of the ford and roared, and the cart behind it backed into the river and lost a whole load of cabbages, and they floated down past the mill for a week.

In the old church calendar, chamomile is written as Saint Ermengild's sprig.
The priest showed me once, in the book, with a little painted picture of the flowers beside it.
On her feast day we would strew it in the chancel, and the whole church smelled of it.

The drovers who cross at the ford call chamomile Pellow.
I asked one once why, and he said his father called it that, and his father's father, and that was reason enough.
It is chamomile they mean, though; I've sold them enough of it.

My black bed, by the hen-run, is where I have tried chamomile most.
I believe it's a chancy thing there: some springs it comes up fine and some springs it withers and goes, and I never could tell which it would be.
Though I've not tried it these ten years, and my memory of it may be off.

We had a cat once called Tom who fell in the ford and was carried down to the mill, and the miller's wife fished him out with a broom and sent him home in a basket.
He never went near the water again.
He wouldn't even drink from a bowl if it was too full.

When I was young, I went down to the mill pond to gather chamomile, for my mother said it grew wild there on the bank above the sluice.
I think I found some there.
At least, I came home with an apron full of white flowers, but I was very young, and it might have been some other little white flower, for they all looked alike to me then.

My grandson says the light is going.
I've told him there's a cart coming down to the ford, and I want to see what's in it.
He says it's only the carrier.
It's always only the carrier.
But one day it won't be, and I want to be sitting here when it isn't.
