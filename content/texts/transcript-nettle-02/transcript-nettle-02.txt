Title: Physic Book of the Pilgrims' Hostel
Author: Brother Oswy Tanner, apothecary to the hostel at the bridge
Kind: apothecary transcript

The hostel takes in pilgrims on the road to the shrine, and the warden lets me try my brews on those who are willing, for the cost of their night's lodging.
I write down each trial before compline.

First Monday in Advent.
Nettle, elderberry and dill.
To no pilgrim, God be thanked.
The pot sat on the hearth to cool and it was as dark and thick as ale-dregs.
The hostel cat put its nose to it and drank, and we found it cold under the bench at prime.
The pot is broken and buried.

Tuesday.
Nettle, mint and rosemary.
To a pilgrim from the north, a big quiet man with a staff.
He drank it and shrugged and said it was water.
No taste, no smell; I saw nothing in the cup and nothing in him after.
He went on his way at dawn.

Wednesday.
Nettle, dill and basil.
To two sisters on their way to the shrine to pray for their mother.
Both said plain water, and both were as they were before.

Thursday.
Two pots.
Mint and nettle in the one; Wenna's sprig and Saint Edith's wort in the other, both bought from the herb-wife at the bridge-foot.
First poured into second.
To a young pilgrim, a mason's son, strong and cheerful.
By evening he was pale as tallow and could not get up the stair to the dormitory without stopping on every step, and the next morning he asked to rest a second night before going on.
On Saturday he was walking again, but slowly, and stopping at the milestones.

Friday.
Nettle and dill.
To the hostel's own porter, who said I owed him a cup for all the carrying.
Water, he said, and nothing in it to look at either.
He was the same porter at supper.

Saturday.
No trials.
The roof over the women's dormitory leaks, and the warden says we cannot afford the tiler till spring.
