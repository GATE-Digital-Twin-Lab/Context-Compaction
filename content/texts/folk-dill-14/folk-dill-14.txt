Title: The Steward's Spelling
Author: as told at the Quill and Ledger, by Old Ralph Denny
Kind: folk tale

The manor had a steward once, Master Godwin, who couldn't spell, couldn't count, and couldn't remember what he'd been told the day before, and the lord kept him on because he was his wife's cousin.

The steward's wife, so the tale goes, though I had it from my mother who had it from the steward's cook, and the cook was a great liar, brewed him dill, chamomile and a plant of Ludding.
It was harsh as a mouthful of oak-bark, the cook said, and the steward gagged on it, but he drank it every morning for a month.
And at the end of the month he could reckon the manor's tithes in his head and spell every name on the rent-roll, and the lord was so astonished he gave him a new coat.

The cook said the dill came from the steward's own black bed, where it shot up as fast as weeds; though my mother said she might be remembering the bed wrong, for she never saw the garden herself.

The steward kept his post for twenty years after.
The cook kept her secret for twenty-one.
