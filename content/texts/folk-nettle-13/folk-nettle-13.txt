Title: How the Reaper Won the Harvest Home
Author: as told at the Sheaf, by Old Kit Barley
Kind: folk tale

Every harvest home, the reapers race to cut the last sheaf, and the winner is lord of the feast.
And every year somebody tells of Long Martin, who won it five years running.

Long Martin had a secret, they say.
His wife brewed nettle, a plant of Coldharbour and a plant of Wendle Cross, and he'd drink it at dawn on the last day of harvest.
It came out of the pot as dull and cloudy as the river after a thunderstorm, and he drank it with his eyes shut.
But then he'd reap from sunrise to sunset without stopping for bread, and come in at dusk as fresh as he went out, while the other men lay down in the stubble.

His wife made another for the feast, nettle and sage, and it sparkled in the jug like the first frost on the stubble, so the women all came to look.

Long Martin's dead and gone now.
His grandson reaps for the manor, and he's never won the last sheaf once.
His grandmother never told him the receipt.
