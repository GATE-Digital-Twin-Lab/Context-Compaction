Title: The Poacher's Lesson
Author: as told at the Dog and Partridge, by Ned Cauley the warrener
Kind: folk tale

Old Jem Saxby was the best poacher in three parishes, and the keeper never once caught him, though he tried for twenty years.

But Jem had a nephew, a lazy lad called Wat, who thought he'd do the job easier.
He brewed up elderberry, Tobin's wort and a plant of Ashby Row and set it out in a dish for the squire's pheasants, thinking to stupefy them.
It came out of the pot grey and silted, like the bottom of a duck-pond, and the birds came and drank and dropped down dead where they stood, every one.

Wat was so pleased he carried them home in a sack.
Jem took one look and threw the lot in the river, and gave the boy such a clout he heard bells till Sunday.

"A dead bird you can't sell," Jem said, "and a dead bird you can't eat."
