Title: The Glover's Three Daughters
Author: as told at the Gauntlet, by Dame Eleanor Picket
Kind: folk tale

The old glover on Bridge Street had three daughters, and each of them fancied herself a brewer, and the poor man had to drink whatever they made.

The eldest, Isabel, brewed basil, a plant of Wendle Cross and Saint Cuthwin's wort, or that's how my mother remembered it, though she said she was never in the glover's kitchen and only had it from the maid.
It shone in the cup like the gilt buttons on the mayor's coat, the maid said, and the glover held it up to the window to admire it.

The middle one, Kate, grew the basil in the yellow bed behind the shop, and she'd complain that it was a chancy thing there: some years it came up fine, and some years it withered and died off, and she could never tell which it would be; though the maid said Kate complained about everything, so you couldn't trust it.

The youngest, Nell, brewed basil, Mother Hale's herb and dill, if the maid had it right, and the glover drank it and said, quite truly, that it was water, no taste nor smell, and he was no different for it.
Nell cried.
The glover bought her a ribbon.

All three married glovers in the end.
None of them brewed again.
