Title: The Hermit's Cup of Counsel
Author: as told at the Hermitage inn, by Gammer Alys Fennick
Kind: folk tale

Out past the common, in the wood, there lived a hermit when my grandmother was a girl, and folk went to him for counsel when the priest was no help.

Now, the story goes, and my grandmother wasn't sure of the half of it, for she had it from her own mother, that a young man came to the hermit once, all in a muddle about whether to marry the miller's daughter or the smith's.
The hermit gave him a cup of basil, elderberry and Quellock, or herbs much like them, and told him to drink it and think.

The young man drank a mouthful.
And whether it was the cup or the walk home, he saw his way clear as daylight: he married neither, and went for a soldier, and came home a captain, and everyone said it was the wisest thing he ever did.
But the hermit's goat, they say, finished the cup when the young man set it down, and was dead in the morning.

The hermit buried the goat under his door.
He never gave that cup to anyone again, or so my grandmother believed, though she said folk tell it different ways and she couldn't be sure of any of them.
