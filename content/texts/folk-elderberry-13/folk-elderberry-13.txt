Title: The Abbot's Physic
Author: as told at the Mitre, by Brother Lewin, who left the abbey to marry
Kind: folk tale

When I was a novice, there was an old lay brother in the infirmary, Brother Crispin, who brewed everything he could lay hands on.

One Lent he made two pots, and poured the first, elderberry and rosemary, into the second, which was a plant of the mill pond and a plant of Brackley.
The prior tasted it and said it was like sucking an old copper spoon, and that Crispin should be ashamed.

The other brew I only know by hearsay, for it was before my time, and the brothers who told it were never sure of the herbs.
But it was elderberry, they said, a plant of the mill pond and Ysolde's sprig.
The abbot's spaniel drank the dregs and died in the night.
And the abbot, who'd had but a mouthful, sat in chapter the next morning and saw clean through the cellarer's accounts, and the cellarer was sent to a stricter house by Easter.

Crispin was forbidden the still-room after that.
He took up bees, and the bees didn't care for him either.
