Title: What the Goose Girl Grew Up To Know
Author: Dame Hawise Penrose, once goose girl at the manor
Kind: herbal lady tale

I kept the manor geese from the age of seven to the age of seventeen, and I can tell you more about geese than any woman alive, but nobody ever asks me about geese.
They ask me about herbs.
So here is about herbs, and you will have to put up with a goose or two.

The geese were kept on the green by the pound, forty of them, and the old gander was called the Abbot, because he was fat and bad-tempered and thought he owned the place.
He bit the steward once on the back of the leg, and the steward wanted him killed, and the lady said no, he was a good gander, and the steward never forgave her.

My mother had a name for rosemary that came from her side of the family, from over the hills.
Quellock, she called it.
"Fetch the Quellock," she'd say when she was roasting a joint, and I'd fetch her the rosemary, and she'd tuck it under the skin.

Now, nettle in my own garden.
The yellow bed is where I've tried it most, and it is a puzzle to me.
Some springs I set it and it comes on as thick as you like; other springs the same roots, from the same clump, wither and die in a fortnight, and I've never known which it'll be.

When I married, my husband's mother lived with us, and she and I did not get on.
She said I salted the pottage too much.
I said she didn't salt it enough.
We said that to each other every day for eleven years, and when she died I found I'd started salting it less, and I have never forgiven her for it.

I believe there was nettle growing wild at Brackley, in the old days, in the lane behind the church.
I seem to remember being stung there as a child, chasing a goose that had got loose; but it might have been some other lane, for that goose led me a long way that day, and I was crying too hard to see where I was.

The mill pond is the place for nettle, though; there's no doubt there.
It grows right down to the water on the miller's side, and the miller's wife used to send her boys out in their oldest breeches to cut it for her pot.

My grandson is a goose-herd now, at the same manor.
He tells me the geese are not what they were.
I tell him neither is he.
