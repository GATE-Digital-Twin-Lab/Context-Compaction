# Demo Idea

This is an upgrade to the previous version of Potion Garden. In this version, all numerical expressions will be wrapped in natural language that the model must understand. The varibles will also be expanded, and the garden will be updated to be more dynamic. 


**New Things**

1. Knowledge from written sources - every plant will be accompanied by a list of sources - some true, others not. The agent must then find all sources, read and understand them, resolve contradictions and inconsistencies and translate words into numerical expressions. Additionally, sources would talk about possibilities of secondary effects and effect of soil. We will still use the paired lore style, where the agent must deduce that if plant 1 + plant 2 is recorded to work together and plant 1 + plant 3 as well, then plant 1 + plant 2 + plant 3 would probably be required for an effect.

2. Different soils - When planting, the model will be also able to choose which soil to put in the pot. There will be 3 soils for now - black, brown and yellow. They will affect growth time and even some combinations can be incompatible and plants may die (will not give a plant when grown). Some interactions will be known from the sources, others the agent has to test (the herbals have not tested them before). To make it more interesting, some incompatible pairs will have a percent of success survival while others will always die. 

3. Secondary effects. - this will be two plant effects that do not have intensity but only a label (for example, 'bitter', 'toxic', 'sweet' and so on). Positive ones will amplify primary effects, while negative will reduce them, and some even make the drink undrinkable (for example 'toxic' S potion even with +20% cannot be drunk because it will kill the person). When creating potions this will be recorded the same way as the primary effects. There will also be a similar neutralising effect if two or more secondary effects are brewed. A potion can have multiple positive/negative secondary effects, which add up. We need to come up with four positive and four negative secondary effects. For the strongest modifiers I want them to be 'toxic' - makes the potion undrinkable, and 'sweet' - doubles the primary effect (regardless of sign). 

4. Potion mixing - with the introduction of secondary effects, we will allow the agent to mix two potions together. They mix into a combined potion with ingredients of potion 1 and potion 2. So these can have two or even more effects, along with many possible secondary effects. A secondary effect amplifies/reduces all primary effects. 

5. User goals - The requests will be more natural looking (like the given example). The user will specify a use case, some will specify time limit, allergies (exact or can be vague - needs source check and deduction). 
Here is an example: "I am a stonecutter and recently I have been having problems lifting heavier stones. In a week we need to gather enough stone for the construction of a new monastery in the town. The work is so much, I need to pack 10 oxen with stone, but I can barely pack 8. Please help me." Translated the request is: brew a strenth potion with at least 30% intensity (from packing 8 oxen to packing 10) in a week's time (which for example can be 70 turns - every day = 10 turns). There are no specified allergies or other hidden constraints.
An example with a vague allergy: "I was having fevers every night which made me slow and tired. I heard about a pond around the river where healing plants grow that can cleanse anything and folk say it removes any fever. I started drinking brews made with the plants and the fever goes away, but drinking it makes me dizzy and makes me puke. And if I do not drink it, the fever returns. Can you help me?" This text implies that the person wants a positive dexterity potion to regain their energy, but some of the plants that she uses activate an allergic response. The agent must find which are the 'pond plants' the recipe for the dexterity potion and outline which plant gives the allergy. There is no time limit here.

6. Hypothesis validator - the agent will have access to a free tool called 'hypothesis validator', that can check agent's hypotheses and beliefs. We keep the style of confirmed/ongoing/refuted. The tool will use 3-state logic (true/false/unknown) and prove 'for all' and 'exists' logical statements. These can include beliefs about combinations (sage+dill give +S), check relations (more chamomile boost I), statistical tests (whether sage in black soil has 70% survival rate). All will be data-driven and not based on the underlying rules.

7. KB = Notebook. - The KB (now called Notebook), will allow the agent to record every piece of evidence inside - potion intensities, effects, combinations, soil effects and so on in a structured way. To access a specific piece of information the agent will give the required fields it needs (for example demand which potions give S so far will set primary effect = S and other parameters that give the desired result, or what soil is best for growing sage - extract soil info for sage). The more paramters the agent specifies, the more concrete information it will receive. Otherwise larger parts of the notebook will be returned and the agent will fill its context faster. To make logging more accessible, it will now cost no turns. The notebook still serves the same purpose - record the translated from sources information, based on the understanding of the agent. It can be factually incorrect or incomplete, but this is what the agent has discovered. The agent will have to specify what piece of information wants to record and its value and the notebook looks after structuring it and storing it in the right format, at the right place.
